## Corrections and adaptations

1. **N3.2 Q4b is mathematically false as printed.** The source asks why the value of the 7 in 0.20074 is ten times the value of the 4. Actual digit values are 0.0007 and 0.00004, whose ratio is 17.5. The source answer describes column units, which do differ by 10. Keep the number and both digits; revise the question to test the claim, provide the correct ratio and explain the distinction. A preceding teaching screen compares the column units explicitly. No misleading answer is retained.
2. **N3.2 Q3 explicitly asks for a fraction.** The printed answer also permits 0.005. The lesson requires fraction notation to meet the question, accepts equivalent fractions (including 5/1000 and 1/200), and explains decimal equivalence in teaching. Decimal-only submissions receive complete working and may continue immediately.
3. **Video shorthand is not an equality of bare numbers.** The clips display `2 = 20,000`, `5 = 0.005` and `9 = 0.0009` as place-value readouts. The videos are preserved. Written alternatives instead show the digit multiplied by its column unit; no bare digit is asserted equal to its positional value. Mathematical clarification is in the working, without a correction paragraph on video screens.
4. The PDFs use a centred dot as the decimal point; application expressions use a full stop. Thousands separators are normalised for numeric grading. “Units” and “ones” are introduced as synonyms.
5. N3.1 Q3 keeps both requested outputs in paired choices. Tom’s and Priya’s questions retain the claims and reason/example requirement, expressed as choices containing a complete reason and concrete example. They are not free-text reasoning assessments.
6. Follow-up questions display the correct constructed number from part a even after an incorrect submission, so they assess the intended digit’s value. Each part a has already revealed complete working and its accepted answer.
7. Mark allocations and difficulty badges are omitted from the learner interface. All source questions and both worked examples remain. Older decimal ordering/comparison activities are outside these PDFs and are not appended to this canonical lesson.
