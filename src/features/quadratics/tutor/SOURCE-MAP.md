# Lesson 21 (Algebra A7) source map

A7 is written from the four PDF/video pairs `A7.1_Factorising_Quadratics_All_Positive`, `A7.2_Factorising_Quadratics_Negative_Middle_Term`, `A7.3_Factorising_Quadratics_Negative_Last_Term` and `A7.4_Factorising_Quadratics_Difference_Of_Two_Squares`. It is lesson 21 in the course (`?lesson=21`, progress key `L021`), and students see it as lesson 7 of the Algebra chapter.

Every video works its PDF's own Q1, so each rung's worked screen is its video and Q1.

Rungs run in the PDFs' order, easiest first. All positive is the search with no signs to think about; a negative middle term makes both numbers negative; a negative last term gives different signs and more pairs to check; the difference of two squares comes last because it is the special case with no middle term and leans on "one plus, one minus" from the rung before.

Source answers are kept; every one was re-checked by multiplying the brackets back out. There were no slips. Changes from the source:

- **A7.4 Q5a and Q5b are Higher tier.** 4x² − 25 = (2x + 5)(2x − 5) has a number in front of x², and AQA 8300 puts only x² + bx + c (including the difference of two squares) on Foundation; ax² + bx + c is in bold. As agreed with Sunny (1 Oct), the app asks the same question with x² − 100 (a square card of side x with a 10 cm square cut out), and Q5b expands (x + 10)(x − 10) to check it.
- The A7.3 video writes (x − 3)(x + 5) and the PDF (x + 5)(x − 3); the app's working follows the pairs table, and either order is marked right.
- The A7.4 video ends with a 7 m pond (51 m² at x = 10) and Q4 has a 6 m pond (64 m²); both are right.

The working uses the diamond (X) method, chosen by Sunny on 1 Oct after comparing it with the videos' pairs list, a table and the grid run backwards: it puts both jobs in one small picture. The question has its middle number boxed blue and its last number boxed amber. "Find their factors" draws the diamond: the last number on top ("multiply to", amber), the middle number underneath ("add to", blue) and two empty gaps at the sides. Rungs with a negative sign then pick the signs in words ("+ 20: the same sign", "− 9x: both negative"; with a negative last number, the bigger number takes the middle term's sign). Every pair that multiplies to the last number, with those signs, is listed under the diamond one a line ("−4 × (−5)"); the next step adds each pair's sum, crossing the wrong ones and ticking the one that works (purple). The last step fills the diamond's gaps with that pair and builds the green answer from it, and the working stops there. A difference of two squares writes each term as a square (x² = x × x, 49 = 7 × 7), then one plus bracket and one minus bracket. "Expand to check" questions use the grid from Expanding brackets (A3). As in A5 and A6, the parts a step has finished with are greyed out: only the question, what the step before added and what this step adds stay clear; the diamond is clear again on the last step, when its gaps fill.

Practice questions (`maths/practice/bank/quadratics.ts`) use new quadratics; their AQA weights are estimates until the 2022–25 papers are counted. The readiness statements sit with Simplifying expressions in the exam map, next to factorising into one bracket.
