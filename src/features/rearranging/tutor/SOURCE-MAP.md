# Lesson 20 (Algebra A6) source map

A6 is written from the four PDF/video pairs `A6.1_Rearranging_Simple_Linear_Formulae`, `A6.2_Rearranging_Formulae_With_Fractions`, `A6.3_Rearranging_Formulae_With_Squares` and `A6.4_Rearranging_Formulae_With_Square_Roots`. It is lesson 20 in the course (`?lesson=20`, progress key `L020`), and students see it as lesson 6 of the Algebra chapter.

Every video works its PDF's own Q1, so each rung's worked screen is its video and Q1.

Rungs run in the PDFs' order, easiest first: each one adds one new move to undo. Simple formulae are solving equations with letters; fractions add "clear the bottom first"; squares add a square root as the last move; square roots are the reverse (square first), with T = 2√l, where the 2 is squared too, as the hardest question.

Source answers are kept exactly; every one was re-checked, and every rearranged formula is put back into the original with numbers by the verifier. There were no slips. Small differences from the source:

- A6.1 Q5c's reason says Ben "divided only the 6 by 3". He also left y undivided, so the app's reason is "divide all of y − 6 by 3".
- A6.2 Q4a writes the last line as t = d/s rather than d/s = t like the others. The app writes every answer the same way, with the new subject first.
- The A6.3 video's last card draws √(A ÷ 6) with its root sign below the line and no bar (a drawing glitch in the export; the working before it is right). The app's own picture and answers are unaffected. Sunny may re-export it.

Everything is Foundation tier: AQA 8300's "rearrange formulae to change the subject" (A5) has no Higher-only part, and every source formula has the subject once. Nothing was removed.

The working is the board from Solving equations (lesson 19), with letters: the formula split at its = sign, the part each move undoes boxed in purple, the move written on both sides, what cancels struck out, one move per step. Dividing puts the whole of the other side over the number, so the bar sits under all of C − 5 (Ben's mistake); a square root is drawn over the whole of A/6 (Mia's); squaring T/2 squares the top and the bottom, 2 becoming 4 (Sam's). The last move ends on the answer in its green box, written with the new subject first, and the working stops there. "Use your formula" parts are worked a line at a time and end on the answer once. The final screen is an app-only summary.

Formulas are typed after "m =" in a box that can be one line or a fraction (top and bottom), with a √ key that draws a root over the whole answer and an x² key. They are marked by trying numbers, so every correct form is right: (C − 5)/3, C/3 − 5/3 and (C − 5) ÷ 3 all are. Each question's usual slips get their own message (the number moved with the wrong sign, only part of a side divided, upside down, multiplied instead, the root or square on only one letter), and so do an answer still containing the subject and an answer that has lost a letter.

Practice questions (`maths/practice/bank/rearranging.ts`) use new formulae; their AQA weights are estimates until the 2022–25 papers are counted for changing the subject. The readiness statements sit with Solving equations in the exam map.
