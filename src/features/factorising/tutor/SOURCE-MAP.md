# Lesson 18 (Algebra A4) source map

A4 is written from the two PDF/video pairs `A4.1_Factorising_Two_Terms` and `A4.2_Factorising_Three_Terms`. It is lesson 18 in the course (`?lesson=18`, progress key `L018`), and students see it as lesson 4 of the Algebra chapter.

Both videos work their PDF's own Q1, so each rung's worked screen is its video and Q1. Each worked screen also has a Video 2 (`two-terms-another-way.mp4`, `three-terms-another-way.mp4`): a second version of the same A4.1/A4.2 video, working the same Q1 another way, dividing the whole expression by the common factor as fractions so matching factors cancel.

| Rung | Source PDF and video | App coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two terms | `A4.1_Factorising_Two_Terms` | Video + worked example = Q1 (6x² + 9x); Q2, Q3, Q4a and Q5a typed; Q4b (Kim), Q5b (show by expanding) and Q5c (Sam) as choices |
| Three terms | `A4.2_Factorising_Three_Terms` | Video + worked example = Q1 (10x + 15y + 5); Q2, Q3, Q4a, Q5a and Q5c typed; Q4b (Ravi) and Q5b (check by expanding) as choices |

Source answers are kept exactly; every one was re-checked. A4.2 Q5a splits 8x³y as "4 × 2 × x² × x × y"; the app writes every letter out (4 × 2 × x × x × x × y) like the other lines. Everything is Foundation tier: nothing was removed.

The working runs the A3 grid backwards: the terms of the question sit in the boxes, each term is split into its factors, the parts every term shares are boxed, the common factor goes down the side, then the boxed parts are crossed out of every term and what is left of each term fills the bracket along the top (a term that was all common factor leaves 1). The answer is built from coloured pieces, each labelled with where it came from: the common factor outside, and what is left of each term inside. Both follow Sunny's feedback that students need to see where the divisions and the final answer come from. The final screen is an app-only summary. Practice questions (`maths/practice/bank/factorising.ts`) use new expressions; their AQA weights are estimates until the 2022–25 papers are counted for factorising.
