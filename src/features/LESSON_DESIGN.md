# Revily lesson design — agreed pattern

Reference implementations: Lesson 1 and Lesson 2 Variant B. Lesson 1 uses Anushka's source where provided; subsequent variants use original Revily content, without implying tutor review.

## Teaching sequence

Start with an accessible visual exploration. Separate choosing a method from completing a calculation. Model the method with optional, replayable steps, then let the learner try a similar example. Address misconceptions explicitly, remove scaffolding for practice, and finish with reasoning and transfer.

## Study screen

Small Revily logo, Lesson menu, current topic and slim progress bar. Activity takes most of the space. Visual first, one question directly above its response controls. No duplicated instructions, large persistent navigation or footer advice. Opening and closing the menu preserves progress and answers.

## Hints and feedback

Questions begin with Hint collapsed. Method guides and any associated highlighting appear only when requested. Closing the hint hides both. Hints do not submit responses or reveal final results; they remain usable after grading. One-tap choices; numeric/multiple/order responses use Check answer. Keep the submitted response visible. Both correct and incorrect attempts reveal numbered Explanation steps and an explicit Answer. Always enable Continue after submission; no forced guessing. Demonstrations never gate progression.

## Subject-specific visuals

Choose a visual that explains the actual concept. Lesson 2 has a four-level BIDMAS reference (B, I, DM, AS), with equal-priority pairs grouped. It is available inside Hint for all 17 questions. Lesson 3 should use place-value columns, fixed decimal alignment and direct number ordering, rather than copying an operations mnemonic.

## Delivery

Preserve existing variants as comparison references and unrelated local work. Keep new content and specialised visuals in the lesson's variant folder, sharing grading and structured explanation rendering. Produce a storyboard before implementation. Check all answer paths, hint/menu state, meaningful visual controls, mathematical accuracy and 320px mobile rendering. Do not deploy without a request.
