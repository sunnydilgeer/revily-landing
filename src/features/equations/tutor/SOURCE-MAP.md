# Lesson 19 (Algebra A5) source map

A5 is written from the five PDF/video pairs `A5.1_Solving_Equations_One_Unknown`, `A5.2_Solving_Equations_Unknown_On_Both_Sides`, `A5.3_Solving_Equations_With_Brackets`, `A5.4_Solving_Equations_With_Fractions` and `A5.5_Solving_Equations_Squares_And_Square_Roots`. It is lesson 19 in the course (`?lesson=19`, progress key `L019`), and students see it as lesson 5 of the Algebra chapter.

Every video works its PDF's own Q1, so each rung's worked screen is its video and Q1.

Rungs run easiest first. Squares and roots comes second, not last: it uses the same moves as one unknown plus one more. Then both sides, brackets and fractions each build on the one before (fractions turn into brackets on both sides).

Source answers are kept exactly; every one was re-checked. Changes from the source:

- A5.5 Q4a said "a square garden has an area of 3x²". A square with that area has sides of √3·x, so x is not a length and Q4b's answer ("a length cannot be negative") doesn't follow. The app says "a rectangular garden is 3x metres long and x metres wide", with the same equation and answers. The video still says "square".
- A5.4 Q4a and Q5a wrote their last line twice; the app shows it once.
- The A5.1 video ends with a different taxi (£5 a mile, £28, 5 miles) from Q4a (£2 a mile, £17, 7 miles). Both are right; the video's is in its text alternative, and Q4a is unchanged.

Everything is Foundation tier: nothing was removed. The fraction rung (a fraction on each side) is the top of Foundation, but it is in the (non-bold) linear equations content.

The working is a board with two sides, like the videos: the equation split at its = sign, the same move written on both sides in purple, what cancels struck out, and the new line underneath. One move per step, and each move first boxes, in purple, the part of the row above that it undoes: the − 3, the 5 in 5x, the smaller x term, the number outside a bracket, the bottom of a fraction, the ² or the √. Brackets are multiplied out with every product shown (2 × 3x + 2 × 1), following Sunny's feedback on the A5.3 video. The move that leaves the letter on its own ends in the answer, in a green box, and the working stops there: 7x + 5 = 40 is "Subtract 5 from both sides" (7x + 5 − 5 = 40 − 5, 7x = 35), then "Divide both sides by 7" (7x ÷ 7 = 35 ÷ 7, then the answer x = 5). There is no separate answer step or check line (Sunny's feedback: it repeated the answer); the Practice "check your answer" questions still teach checking. Squares finish with both roots and why the negative one works. The final screen is an app-only summary. Practice questions (`maths/practice/bank/equations.ts`) use new equations; their AQA weights are estimates until the 2022–25 papers are counted for equations.
