# Lesson 44 storyboard — Sex chromosomes and genetic diagrams (folder lesson-39)

Third lesson of chapter B6.

Big idea: genes come in versions called alleles, and a genetic diagram (a Punnett square, or circles and lines) shows every way two parents' gametes could combine, so we can work out chances and ratios.

Flow note: the lesson starts with the cross learners can see.
1. **Boy or girl?** 23 pairs, the sex chromosomes, which gametes carry X or Y, the Punnett square, the 1 in 2 chance, then the same cross drawn with circles and lines.
2. **Versions of a gene.** One gene or several; alleles; homozygous; heterozygous; dominant; recessive. One three-mouse drawing (BB, Bb, bb) is reused, with one term per frame.
3. **Crossing two mice.** Genotype and phenotype on the same drawing, then a BB × bb cross (circles and lines) and a Bb × Bb Punnett square with its 3 : 1 ratio; a worked probability; guided practice.

Why this order: the XX × XY cross uses letters learners can already picture (chromosomes), so the Punnett square is learned before alleles add new words. The allele words come one per frame before they are used in a cross. Calculations follow the course pattern: worked example (B39-09, Bb × Bb → 1 in 4) → near-identical guided practice (B39-10, Bb × bb → 1 in 2) → independent calculation (B39-14, Pp × Pp → 3 : 1). The next lesson applies the same crosses to inherited disorders.

1. Start here (B39-01): chromosomes in a body cell (links to meiosis).
2. Boy or girl? (B39-02–04): 23 pairs → sex chromosomes → eggs and sperm → Punnett square → a 1 in 2 chance → another way to draw it. Checks: a male's sex chromosomes; the chance of a girl.
3. Versions of a gene (B39-05–07): one gene or many → alleles → homozygous → heterozygous → dominant → recessive. Checks: which word describes Bb; which genotype gives brown fur.
4. Crossing two mice (B39-08–11): genotype → phenotype → a cross → two Bb parents → a 3 : 1 ratio; worked probability; guided calculation; phenotype of Bb.
5. On your own (B39-12–16): the chance of a girl after three boys; a numbered pea Punnett square (which square is short); a pea flower ratio (independent calculation); invented litter data read without over-claiming; teacher-reviewed written explanation of two black mice having a brown baby.

Out of scope: sex-linked inheritance, codominance, test crosses, more than one gene at a time, the detail of how several genes control height.

Source boundary: supplied pages 64–65 used for scope only; AQA 8464 sections 4.6.1.6 and 4.6.1.8. The book's made-up animal example is replaced with mouse fur colour (named on the page as a single-gene characteristic) and pea plants. All wording, questions, data and diagrams are original. ## Diagram plan
- **Karyotype** (`inherit-sex-pairs`): 23 pairs, one rose and one blue in each, pair 23 (X and Y) boxed.
- **Sex cross** (`inherit-sex-xy/gametes/punnett/chance`): female XX and male XY on the left, eggs along the top and sperm down the side of a grid that fills, then tints. `inherit-sex-lines` redraws it as circles and lines.
- **Three mice** (`inherit-allele-*`, `inherit-cross-genotype/phenotype`): chromosome pairs with allele bands, genotype letters and mice for BB, Bb and bb.
- **Crosses** (`inherit-cross-lines/punnett/ratio/worked`): a BB × bb lines diagram and a Bb × Bb Punnett square with mice in the squares. Letters are coloured by the parent they came from.
- **Questions**: numbered pea Punnett square (`inherit-cross-question`, empty squares in assessment view) and a litter bar chart (`inherit-mice-data`).

### B39-07 · choice

- 0 BB · 1 Bb · **2 bb ✓**
- Hint: How many copies does a recessive allele need to show?

### B39-09 · worked example "Find a probability" · diagram `inherit-cross-worked`
In mice, black fur (B) is dominant to brown fur (b). Two Bb mice are crossed. What is the probability that a baby mouse has brown fur?
1. Draw the Punnett square: each parent can give B or b.
2. Fill the squares: BB, Bb, Bb and bb.
3. Brown fur needs bb, and 1 of the 4 squares is bb.
4. So the probability is 1 in 4, which is 25%. The ratio of black to brown is 3 : 1.
Diagram: the Bb × Bb Punnett square with the bb square outlined and 1 in 4 = 25% brown beside it.

### B39-10 · choice · `calculation`
**Q:** A Bb mouse is crossed with a bb mouse. What is the probability that a baby mouse has brown fur?
- 0 1 in 4 (25%) · 1 3 in 4 (75%) · **2 1 in 2 (50%) ✓** · 3 0 (0%)
- Hint: Draw the square. The bb parent can only give b.
- Explanation: The four squares are Bb, Bb, bb and bb. 2 of the 4 squares are bb, so the probability is 2 in 4 = 1 in 2 (50%).

### B39-11 · choice

- Hint: Phenotype is the characteristic, not the letters.
- Explanation: Bb is the genotype, and heterozygous describes it. B is dominant, so the phenotype is black fur.

### B39-14 · choice · `calculation`, independent
**Q:** In pea plants, purple flowers (P) are dominant to white flowers (p). Two Pp plants are crossed. What is the expected ratio of purple to white offspring?
- 0 1 : 1 · **1 3 : 1 ✓** · 2 1 : 3 · 3 4 : 0
- Hint: Draw the Punnett square, then count purple and white.
- Explanation: The squares are PP, Pp, Pp and pp; only pp is white. Three purple to one white is a ratio of 3 : 1.

**Q:** A breeder crossed two Bb mice several times. The chart shows the babies they got. Which conclusion fits?

### B39-16 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Two mice with black fur have a baby with brown fur. Use a genetic diagram to explain how this can happen. Black fur (B) is dominant to brown fur (b).
- Hint: Start with the baby’s genotype, then work out what each parent must carry.
- Model answer: Brown fur is recessive, so the brown baby must be bb. It got one b allele from each parent. Both parents have black fur, so each must also have a B: they are both Bb. A Punnett square of Bb × Bb gives BB, Bb, Bb and bb, so there is a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a brown baby.
- Marking points: Brown fur is recessive, so the brown baby is bb. · The baby got one b allele from each parent. · So both black parents must be heterozygous, Bb. · A Punnett square of Bb × Bb gives BB, Bb, Bb and bb. · So there is a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of a brown baby.
- Common errors: Saying brown fur is dominant. · Saying one parent must be bb, even though both have black fur. · Saying a 1 in 4 chance means exactly one baby in every four will be brown.
