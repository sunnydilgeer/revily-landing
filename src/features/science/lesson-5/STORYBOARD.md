## Three chapters

1. Chromosomes: optional nucleus refresher; opening nucleus check. Spatial zoom from whole cell to nucleus to a single chromosome to DNA, with a small highlighted DNA section called a gene. Body-cell chromosomes normally occur in pairs. Rods/threads are used before replication; an X is a compact replicated chromosome with two copies, not the permanent shape. Guided genes/chromosome check and pair-count example.
2. Cell cycle: grow, increase mitochondria/ribosomes and replicate DNA; then mitosis separates chromosome sets and divides the nucleus; finally membrane/cytoplasm divide. Each stage shown separately in a replayable walkthrough. Use a simplified two-chromosome starting cell with two copies after replication and two chromosomes in EACH daughter. Guided order and chromosome-count checks. Growth/development contrasted with repair/replacement in mature animals. Optional stepwise four-to-eight chromosome model explains that copies, not final daughter-cell number, increase. Independent order, count and repair contexts; no phase-name memorisation, meiosis or techniques.
3. Stem cells: introduce embryo, adult bone marrow and root/shoot meristem separately. Unspecialised cells can divide to make more stem cells and differentiate into other types. Embryonic cells can form most human cell types; bone-marrow cells have a narrower range including blood cells; meristems can form any plant type across plant life. Then compare. Potential diabetes/paralysis applications, benefit versus viral-infection risk and ethical/religious objections to embryo use. Therapeutic cloning uses matching patient genes to avoid immune rejection in the required model; not a promise of guaranteed successful treatment, no technical method. Plant clones preserve rare species or multiply disease-resistant crops quickly/economically. Original illustrative trial numbers support a claim-evaluation item, explicitly not clinical evidence. Independent source/application/ethical checks and a final balanced written evaluation, saved pending judgement.
