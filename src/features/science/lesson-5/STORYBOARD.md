# Lesson 5 storyboard — Chromosomes and mitosis

B-CELL-005-B v0.2.0 · draftNeedsTeacherReview. Stem cells moved to their own lesson (`lesson-5b`).

Big idea: before a cell divides, it copies its chromosomes, so each new cell gets a complete, identical set. Bodies use this to grow, to replace worn-out cells and to repair damage.

Anchor / route: zoom into the nucleus of a specialised cell (the previous topic), then follow ONE model cell with 2 chromosomes through the whole cell cycle in a single walkthrough: grow → copy (replication) → mitosis → split. Then count chromosomes with new numbers, then ask why bodies need new cells at all.

Why this order:
- Chromosomes, DNA and genes come first, because the cell cycle is about copying and sharing chromosomes.
- Pairs come with chromosomes, so "23 pairs = 46" works as the worked example for the pair-count calculation (B5-04 → B5-31).
- The cell cycle is taught as one six-frame story (the old lesson split it into three 2-frame walkthroughs and a worked example). "Copying is called replication" gets its own frame before mitosis, so learners know what mitosis separates.
- Counting chromosomes follows the story, so the worked example (4 → 4) re-uses every step just taught; practice uses new numbers (6 → 6); "On your own" uses 8 → 8.
- Growth, replacement and repair come last: they are the reason for the cycle, and they hand on to stem cells.
