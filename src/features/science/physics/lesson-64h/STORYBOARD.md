# Physics Lesson 64H storyboard — The motor effect

Chapter P7, Magnetism and electromagnetism. A whole lesson that only Higher students get (catalogue entry in `higher/lessons.ts`; the badge is shown by the app, so no learner text names the tier). Folder `physics/lesson-64h`, id `P-MAG-064H-P`, skill `P-MAG-064H-P`. Visuals: `components/HigherMotorVisuals.tsx` (focus prefix `hmotor-`). It sits after Lesson 64 (Electromagnetism, which deliberately stops before the motor effect and Fleming’s left-hand rule). Lesson 64 is the last Physics lesson, so 64H is the last lesson in the Higher Physics list.

Big idea: a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field feels a force (the motor effect). The force is at right angles to both the field and the current, biggest when the wire is at 90° to the field and zero when it is parallel. Fleming’s left-hand rule gives its direction (First finger Field, seCond finger Current, thuMb Motion); reversing the current or the field reverses it. Its size is F = B I l, where B is the magnetic flux density (how many field lines in a region, in tesla). In a dc motor the two sides of a coil carry opposite currents, so opposite forces turn it, and a split-ring commutator keeps it turning one way.

Flow note: builds on Lesson 64 (a current makes circles of field round a wire, the right-hand thumb rule) and Lesson 63 (field lines from N to S; north red, south blue) without re-teaching them. One set-up runs through the whole lesson: a north pole facing a south pole, drawn in perspective, with the field lines lying flat in the gap and a wire crossing it front to back. Direction first (the force exists and is at right angles), then the rule that finds it, then its size, then the motor, which uses all three: the motor’s coil is just two of these wires carrying opposite currents. The perspective is chosen so the three directions look different on the page: field across, current towards you (drawn down and to the right), force up; the left hand is drawn with the same three directions.

1. **Start here** (P64H-01): what a current makes around a wire (a magnetic field), the prior knowledge the lesson needs.
2. **The motor effect** (P64H-02–04): field between two poles → add a wire with a current (its own field, dashed circles; the two fields act on each other) → the motor effect, force up, at right angles to field and current → the angle matters (top view: 90° full, 45° some, parallel none) → all together. Practice: when the force is biggest; which way the force acts relative to field and current.
3. **Fleming’s left-hand rule** (P64H-05–07): three directions all at 90° → the left hand (First, seCond, thuMb) → line the hand up with the wire (thumb up) → reverse the current or swap the poles (both: force down) → all together. Practice: what the second finger shows; a diagram with the current reversed (away from you) → down.
4. **How big is the force?** (P64H-08–12): magnetic flux density (field lines in a region, weaker/stronger, tesla) → current I and length in the field l (shaded) → F = B I l with units → rearranged for B and I, lengths in metres. Worked: 0.25 T × 3.0 A × 0.40 m = 0.30 N; B from 0.25 N, 2.5 A and 50 cm = 0.20 T. Practice: 0.30 T, 4.0 A, 0.50 m → 0.60 N; I from 0.12 N, 0.20 T, 15 cm → 4.0 A.
5. **The dc motor** (P64H-13–15): coil on an axle between N and S, current away along the left side and back along the right → opposite forces (left down, right up) turn it → split-ring commutator swaps the contacts every half turn (end views: now / half a turn later) → turn it the other way: reverse the current or swap the poles (drawn with the poles swapped, forces and turning reversed) → faster: more current, more turns, stronger field. Practice: why the coil rotates; which numbered part swaps the contacts (assessment view: numbers only).
6. **On your own** (P64H-16–20): F = B I l with a length in cm (calculation); Fleming’s rule with the poles swapped and the current away (assessment view: no force arrow, no direction words) → up; force–current data read without over-claiming; which change makes a motor faster and reversed (swap the poles and increase the current; reversing both current and field cancels); written: why a dc motor’s coil rotates and keeps turning one way, and one way to speed it up.

Numbers (each checked twice, and in `physics64h.test.tsx`): 0.25 × 3.0 × 0.40 = 0.30 N · 50 cm = 0.50 m, 2.5 × 0.50 = 1.25, 0.25 ÷ 1.25 = 0.20 T · 0.30 × 4.0 × 0.50 = 0.60 N (distractors 2.4 N = 4.0 × 0.30 ÷ 0.50, 4.8 N = sum, 0.15 N = no current) · 15 cm = 0.15 m, 0.20 × 0.15 = 0.030, 0.12 ÷ 0.030 = 4.0 A (distractors 0.0036 A = product, 0.25 A = inverted, 0.040 A = cm not converted) · 30 cm = 0.30 m, 0.080 × 2.5 × 0.30 = 0.060 N (distractors 6.0 N = cm not converted, 0.60 N = power-of-ten slip, 0.0096 N = 0.080 × 0.30 ÷ 2.5) · P64H-18 table 1.0/2.0/3.0/4.0 A → 0.05/0.10/0.15/0.20 N (invented; consistent with B = 0.20 T, l = 0.25 m).

Directions (checked with F = I l × B; x right, y into the page, z up): N on the left, current towards you → up (teaching picture); current away → down (P64H-07, reverse frame); poles swapped, current towards you → down (reverse frame); poles swapped, current away → up (P64H-17). Motor: left side current away → down, right side towards you → up, anticlockwise looking into the page; poles swapped → reversed.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "The two fields act on each other" is the only explanation of why there is a force; no catapult-field diagram.
- The top-view frame shows 45° as "some force" with a shorter bar, without saying how much (no sin θ).
- Field lines are drawn straight and evenly spaced in the gap (a uniform field), as the spec’s F = B I l assumes.
- Magnetic flux density is introduced as "how many field lines in a region", as on the page; flux itself is not defined.
- Equation symbols are set in a serif italic in the diagrams so that I and l look different; learner text always says "current, I" and "length, l".
- The dc motor shows the + contact touching the half-ring wired to the left side; the end views show the halves swapping contacts after half a turn. "Contacts" is used instead of "brushes".
- The reverse frame is drawn with the poles swapped (not the current reversed), with forces and turning reversed to match.
