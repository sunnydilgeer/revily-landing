# Physics Lesson 59 storyboard — Uses of light, UV, X-rays and gamma rays

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-59`, id `P-WAV-059-P`, skill `P-EMMORE`, spec 6.6.2.4. It owns the uses of the last four groups of the EM spectrum: visible light (optical fibres), ultraviolet (fluorescent lamps, security pens, suntans), X-rays (images of bones) and X-rays and gamma rays (radiotherapy, medical tracers).

Big idea: each type of EM wave suits a job because of what it does to materials. Light carries data down fibres, UV makes some materials glow, X-rays pass through flesh but not bone, and X-rays and gamma rays can kill cells or be tracked leaving the body.

Flow note: the order runs from the least to the most medical: light in fibres, UV, then X-rays, because "passes through flesh but not bone" is the idea that X-ray images and radiotherapy both build on, and gamma tracers reuse "passes easily through the body". The dangers of EM waves come next thread; here only one clause appears (too much UV is dangerous; the rays kill living cells, so radiotherapy is aimed carefully).

Sections:
1. Start here (P59-01): a picture of bones in an arm.
2. How does light carry data? (P59-02–04): optical fibres → pulses of light → reflected along the fibre. Checks: how data is sent; what happens to the rays.
3. How is ultraviolet used? (P59-05–07): some materials glow → fluorescent lamps → security pens → suntans. Checks: security pen ink; what lamps use.
4. How are X-rays used to see inside you? (P59-08–10): pass through flesh → image → broken bones. Checks: why bones show; what a doctor checks.
5. How do X-rays and gamma rays treat and track? (P59-11–13): radiotherapy → aimed carefully → medical tracers → tracking. Checks: why aimed carefully; which wave a tracer uses.
6. On your own (P59-14–17): a special ink lamp; a numbered fibre diagram; X-rays and gamma rays compared; a written medical tracer explanation.

Out of scope: how optical fibres work (total internal reflection); dangers of each group (next thread); the physics of how fluorescence works; half-life of tracers (Atomic structure owns radiation). The exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 227 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "A coating on the glass absorbs UV and gives off visible light" is added to the fluorescent lamp frame to make the idea concrete; it is standard and not on the page.
- The X-ray frame says bones show up "as pale shapes"; this matches an ordinary X-ray image. The lesson does not explain film or detectors.
- The suntan frame gives one clause on danger only.
