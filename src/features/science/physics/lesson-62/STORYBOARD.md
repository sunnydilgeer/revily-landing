# Physics Lesson 62 storyboard — Dangers of electromagnetic waves

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-62`, id `P-WAV-062-P`, skill `P-WAV-062-P`, spec 6.6.2.3. It owns harm from UV, X-rays and gamma rays (ionising = knocks electrons off atoms), radiation dose and sieverts, the millisievert conversion, weighing risk against benefit, and comparing scan doses by a friendly ratio.

Big idea: high frequency EM waves can damage living cells; radiation dose (in sieverts) measures the risk, and doctors weigh that risk against the benefit.

Flow note: harm first (why it matters), then how the risk is measured and the unit conversion as a worked calculation, then real comparisons and the risk-benefit judgement. Calculation flow: worked example → near-identical guided item → independent item in "On your own" (dimension 'calculation'), for both the unit conversion and the dose ratio. Conversion is an explicit step. The earlier lesson on alpha, beta and gamma radiation is referred to in words only.

Sections:
1. Start here (P62-01): why the dental worker leaves the room.
2. How can EM waves harm us? (P62-02–04): high frequency waves → UV effects → ionising X-rays and gamma rays. Checks: which radiation causes sunburn; what ionising means.
3. How do we measure the risk? (P62-05–07): dose → what it depends on → millisieverts. Worked: 0.005 Sv = 5 mSv. Guided: 0.008 Sv = 8 mSv.
4. Is the risk worth it? (P62-08–11): benefits and risks → dose by body part → comparing doses. Worked: 6.0 ÷ 2.0 = 3 times. Guided: 10 ÷ 2.0 = 5 times; risk vs benefit after an accident.
5. On your own (P62-12–15): 0.012 Sv in mSv; table ratio 12 ÷ 3.0 = 4; the property that makes X-rays and gamma rays dangerous; a written explanation of harm and benefit.

Out of scope: half-life, absorbed vs effective dose, how doses add up over time, the uses of X-rays (earlier lesson), and radiotherapy detail.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 230 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.3. All wording, numbers and examples are original (the page's head and chest CT figures and car-accident sentence are not reused). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:

- "Ratio of doses = ratio of risk" is presented as the page does, as a simple comparison.
