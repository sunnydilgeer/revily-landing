# Physics Lesson 58 storyboard — Uses of radio waves, microwaves and infrared

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-58`, id `P-WAV-058-P`, skill `P-EMUSE`, spec 6.6.2.4. It owns the uses of the first three groups of the EM spectrum: radio waves (radio, TV, Bluetooth), microwaves (satellite communication, microwave ovens) and infrared (cameras, heaters, cooking).

Big idea: each type of EM wave suits a job because of how far it travels and what absorbs it. Radio waves carry signals, microwaves carry signals to satellites and heat the water in food, infrared is given out by hot objects and warms whatever absorbs it.

Flow note: radio first, because "which wavelength travels how far" is one simple pattern (short waves need a clear path, long waves go further, Bluetooth is the extreme). Satellites come next as another signal use, then ovens as the first use where energy is absorbed and heats something, which sets up infrared, where absorption and thermal stores are the whole point. Uses of light, UV, X-rays and gamma rays come in the next lesson; dangers come in the next thread.

Sections:
1. Start here (P58-01): a car radio with no wire.
2. How are radio waves used? (P58-02–04): programmes → short waves need a clear path → long waves travel further → Bluetooth. Checks: which radio waves go furthest; what Bluetooth is for.
3. How do satellites use microwaves? (P58-05–06): up → back → received. Check: what the satellite does next.
4. How does a microwave oven cook? (P58-07–08): microwaves in the oven → water absorbs, energy transferred → the whole food cooks. Check: what absorbs the microwaves.
5. How is infrared used? (P58-09–11): everything gives out infrared → absorbing warms things → cameras → heating and cooking. Checks: why a survey camera is useful; hot versus cold objects.
6. On your own (P58-12–15): choose the wave for a rescue in the dark; a numbered satellite diagram; an oven explanation; a written oven explanation.

Out of scope: numerical wavelengths; how a transmitter or aerial works; why water molecules absorb microwaves; dangers of microwaves and infrared; Higher-tier ideas. The exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 226 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page says "very short wavelength" for FM radio and TV. The lesson says "shorter wavelength" to keep it relative to the long-wavelength radio waves in the next frame.
- The written task and the last guided oven question use the same idea on purpose, as the written task is the summary of the section.
- The rescue question uses infrared cameras for warm people; the page names "see hot objects in the dark".
