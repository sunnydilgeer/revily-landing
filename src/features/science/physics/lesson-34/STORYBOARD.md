# Physics Lesson 34 storyboard — Alpha, beta and gamma radiation

Chapter P4, Atomic structure. Folder `physics/lesson-34`, id `P-ATM-034-P`, skill `P-RADIATION`, spec 6.4.2.1. It owns the four emissions (alpha, beta, gamma, neutron), radioactive decay in one idea, ionising radiation and ionising power, the property table (ionising power, range in air, stopped by), and choosing radiation for a use (medical tracer, sterilising). Nuclear equations, half-life and the dangers of radiation belong to later lessons.

Big idea: unstable nuclei give out alpha, beta and gamma radiation. The stronger the ionising power, the shorter the range, so different jobs need different types.

Flow note: what comes out (the four types) before what it does (ionising), then how far each goes (the table needs ionising power), then the uses (they need range and ionising power together). Each type gets its own frame in the range section, then the table is a "put it together" frame.

Sections:
1. Start here (P34-01): what can an unstable nucleus do?
2. What comes out of an unstable nucleus? (P34-02–04): decay, alpha and beta, gamma and neutrons. Checks: alpha make-up; which is not a particle.
3. What is ionising radiation? (P34-05–06): knocking electrons off, ionising power, strong-moderate-weak. Check: what ionising does.
4. How far does each type go? (P34-07–09): alpha, beta, gamma, table. Checks: what paper stops; identify the type from what stops it.
5. Which radiation for which job? (P34-10–11): tracer, why not alpha, why gamma, sterilising. Check: best tracer.
6. On your own (P34-12–15): alpha and packaging, a detector-reading diagram, ionising order, a written tracer explanation.

Out of scope: how tracers are used to find blockages or the half-life needed (not on the page), dangers and protection (a later lesson), neutron radiation properties.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 199, bottom (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.4.2.1. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Beta is described only as "a fast-moving electron" (the page's wording); where it comes from is left to the next lesson's neutron-to-proton idea.
- Sterilising is taught as a use of gamma through packaging. The page's exam question is not reused.
- Range in air uses the page's wording: "a few centimetres", "a few metres", "a long way".

## Diagram specs
Colours from `physicsPalette` plus P4 particle colours (protons coral, neutrons grey, electrons blue). One colour per radiation type kept across the lesson: alpha coral, beta blue, gamma green wave. Soft shapes, text at least 12px, readable at 360px.

- `nrad-decay`: an unstable nucleus (drawn wobbly or with a starburst outline) with a small radiation arrow leaving, becoming a calmer nucleus. Labels: "unstable nucleus", "radioactive decay", "nuclear radiation".
- `nrad-alpha-beta`: two cards. Alpha: 2 coral protons and 2 grey neutrons in a cluster, label "alpha particle α: 2 protons and 2 neutrons". Beta: a single blue electron with a speed streak, label "beta particle β: fast-moving electron".
- `nrad-gamma-neutron`: two cards. Gamma: a green wave, label "gamma ray γ: electromagnetic radiation". Neutron: one grey circle, label "neutron n".
- `nrad-ionise`: a ray hitting an atom (nucleus and two electrons), one electron knocked out and flying off, and the atom becoming "positive ion" with a "+". Three-step numbering is fine.
- `nrad-power`: an "ionising power" bar labelled "how easily electrons are knocked off", with the three radiation icons on it, no values yet.
- `nrad-order`: the three radiation icons on a bar from "strong" (alpha) through "moderate" (beta) to "weak" (gamma), with a shaded ramp.
- `nrad-alpha-range`: a source, an alpha arrow that stops after a short distance labelled "a few cm of air", and a sheet of paper that stops it.
- `nrad-beta-range`: a beta arrow that goes further ("a few metres of air"), passes through paper, and stops at a sheet of aluminium.
- `nrad-gamma-range`: a gamma wave going a long way, passing paper and aluminium, and stopping only at a thick block of lead, with a small "or metres of concrete" note.
- `nrad-table`: the property table drawn as three rows with icons: alpha (strong, a few centimetres, paper), beta (moderate, a few metres, aluminium), gamma (weak, a long distance, thick lead or metres of concrete). Columns: ionising power, range in air, stopped by.
- `nrad-tracer`: a simple person outline with an injection arrow into the body, a small radioactive dot inside, and a detector outside the body with a dashed line from the dot. Labels: "tracer injected", "radiation detected outside".
- `nrad-tracer-alpha`: the same person with an alpha arrow stopping inside the body, a cross on the detector line, labelled "cannot get out" and "strongly ionising: damage".
- `nrad-tracer-gamma`: the same person with a green gamma wave passing through to the detector, ticked, labelled "passes out" and "weakly ionising".
- `nrad-steril`: a sealed package (box with a lid line) containing instruments, a source on the left sending a green gamma wave through the package, labelled "sealed in packaging" and "radiation passes through".
