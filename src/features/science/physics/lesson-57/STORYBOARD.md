# Physics Lesson 57 storyboard — Electromagnetic waves

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-57`, id `P-WAV-057-P`, skill `P-EMWAVE`, spec 6.6.2.1 and 6.6.2.3. It owns EM waves as transverse waves that transfer energy from a source to an absorber, the same speed in a vacuum, the continuous spectrum and its order of seven groups, visible light as a small part, and atoms producing and absorbing EM waves (one idea, three short frames).

Big idea: EM waves carry energy from a source to an absorber. They form one continuous spectrum of seven groups, from long wavelength and low frequency to short wavelength and high frequency.

Flow note: one everyday story (a campfire) shows source, wave and absorber before any vocabulary; speed comes last in that section. The spectrum follows, because "same speed, different wavelength" only makes sense once waves have been shown to differ. Atoms come last because they add a cause (where the waves come from) to a picture that is already complete. Uses (next two lessons) and dangers (next thread) are not taught here.

Sections:
1. Start here (P57-01): warmth beside a campfire.
2. How do EM waves transfer energy? (P57-02–04): source → campfire → absorbed, thermal store → same speed. Checks: the word for what takes in waves; speed in a vacuum.
3. What is the EM spectrum? (P57-05–08): many wavelengths → continuous → seven groups in order → long to short → visible light small. Checks: order; highest frequency; how much eyes detect.
4. Where do EM waves come from? (P57-09–11): changes in atoms → electrons and nucleus → different change, different frequency. Checks: what produces gamma rays; why many frequencies.
5. On your own (P57-12–15): a spectrum bar with a hidden group; same speed in a vacuum; correcting a statement; a written energy-transfer explanation.

Out of scope: numerical wavelengths and frequencies for each group; the wave equation for EM waves; uses and dangers of each group; how atoms change (energy levels are one clause, taught earlier in Atomic structure). The exam questions on the page were not reused. The mnemonic is original.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 225 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.1 and 6.6.2.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "Electrons moving between energy levels" is given as an example of a change that produces or absorbs EM waves; the lesson does not claim which group of waves results.
- The lightning and thunder example is used only to show light is much faster than sound.
- The page's campfire example is used, but retold with hands held beside the fire so convection is not the obvious explanation.
