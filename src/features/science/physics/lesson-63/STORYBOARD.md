# Physics Lesson 63 storyboard — Magnets and magnetic fields

Chapter P7, Magnetism and electromagnetism. Folder `physics/lesson-63`, id `P-MAG-063-P`, skill `P-MAG-063-P`, spec 6.7.1.1 and 6.7.1.2. It owns poles, like and unlike poles, magnetic materials, magnetic fields and field lines (north to south), field strength at the poles, plotting with a compass, the Earth's field, and permanent versus induced magnets.

Big idea: a magnet has a field around it that pushes and pulls other magnets and magnetic materials, and field lines show its direction and strength.

Flow note: forces between magnets first (what students already feel), then the field as the region where the force acts, then how to see a field (compass and the Earth), then the two types of magnet. The plotting frame is method preparation only.

Sections:
1. Start here (P63-01): pushing two north poles together.
2. How do magnets push and pull? (P63-02–04): two poles → like repel, unlike attract → magnetic materials. Checks: north near south; which material.
3. What is a magnetic field? (P63-05–07): the field → field lines → where strongest. Checks: line direction; where strongest.
4. How do you see a field? (P63-08–10): the compass needle → plotting → the Earth. Checks: why a compass points north; the next plotting step.
5. Permanent or induced? (P63-11–12): the two types → induced magnetism does not last. Check: the difference.
6. On your own (P63-13–16): a numbered field diagram; an iron nail and a pin; south poles together; a written plotting method.

Out of scope: the motor effect and Fleming's left-hand rule (not on these pages); electromagnets (next lesson); magnetic domains; the location of the Earth's magnetic poles.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 231 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.7.1.1 and 6.7.1.2. All wording and examples are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Compass rule: the north end of the needle points in the direction of the field (matching the page's plotting method).
- The Earth's magnetic field is stated as "the compass points north" only; no discussion of the Earth's magnetic south pole being in the north.

- `magnet-forces`: three pairs of magnets: N–N with arrows apart "repel", S–S "repel", N–S with arrows together "attract"; a caption "no contact needed".
- `magnet-materials`: a magnet with four items attracted (iron nail, steel paper clip, nickel coin, cobalt piece) and two not attracted (copper wire, aluminium foil, crossed) labelled with material names.
- `magnet-field`: a bar magnet with a shaded halo region, two small iron filings/magnetic items inside the halo feeling arrows, and the label "magnetic field: where a force is felt".
- `magnet-lines`: a bar magnet with field lines from N round to S with arrowheads, and a tiny north-pole marker at one point with an arrow along the line "force on a north pole".
- `magnet-strength`: the same field with two zoomed circles: one at a pole "lines close together: strong", one far out "lines far apart: weak".
- `magnet-compass`: a compass (round face) with a needle, the north end red, next to a bar magnet with the needle lined up along a field line; label "needle is a tiny magnet".

- `magnet-earth`: the Earth as a globe with a bar magnet drawn inside (labelled "core is magnetic"), a few field lines, and compasses on the surface all pointing north.
- `magnet-types`: two panels: "permanent magnet: its own field" (bar magnet with lines) and "induced magnet: needs a field" (iron nail with faint lines and a magnet next to it).
