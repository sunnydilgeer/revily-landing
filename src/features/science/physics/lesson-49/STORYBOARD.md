# Physics Lesson 49 storyboard — Investigating motion

Chapter P5, Forces (motion). Folder `physics/lesson-49`, id `P-MOT-049-P`, skill `P-MOTION-PRACTICAL`, spec 6.5.4.2.2 (required practical). It owns the method, variables, equipment, safety and expected results of the Newton's Second Law practical: a trolley pulled by a falling hook and masses, with a light gate measuring acceleration.

Big idea: to test Newton's Second Law fairly you change either the mass or the force, never both. Masses added to the trolley change the mass. Masses moved from the trolley to the hook change the force.

Flow note: the equipment and the two quantities (mass and force) first, because the two tests only make sense once students know that the force is the weight of the hook and masses. Then the method and safety in the order the student would work, then the two investigations one after the other, each with its result. Calculation flow: worked example (100 g = 0.10 kg, W = 0.98 N) → near-identical guided item (500 g = 4.9 N) → independent calculation (200 g = 1.96 N). This lesson prepares students for the practical; it never claims to complete it (the first frame says the teacher runs it in the lab).

Sections:
1. Start here (P49-01): a loaded and an empty trolley pulled by the same force.
2. What are we measuring? (P49-02–04): the aim → equipment → the mass → the force → worked weight calculation. Checks: which force accelerates the trolley; a guided weight calculation.
3. How do you run it? (P49-05–07): set up → release → record → no light gate → safety. Checks: why a starting line; a safety step.
4. How do you change the mass? (P49-08–10): change one thing → not on the hook → masses on the trolley → the result. Checks: what stays the same; where the masses go.
5. How do you change the force? (P49-11–12): change one thing → start with masses on the trolley → move them to the hook → record → the result. Check: why move rather than add.
6. On your own (P49-13–16): independent weight calculation; reading a table of results; spotting an unfair test; a written method for the force investigation.

Out of scope: uncertainty and percentage error; graph plotting of the results; how light gates work inside; drawing conclusions from graphs of a against F or 1/m; the exam question on the page (not reused).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 216 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.4.2.2 required practical. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's dotted note (stopwatch and chalk lines instead of a light gate) is kept as one short frame.
- Weight uses g = 9.8 N/kg, with grams to kilograms shown as its own step.
- Safety (falling masses, feet, trolley stopping before the pulley) is proportionate and is one frame plus one check question.
- The results are stated as "more mass, less acceleration" and "more force, more acceleration", with direct proportionality mentioned once.

## Diagram specs
Look as in Lessons 17 and 18 and the other Physics lessons: soft flat fills with a darker stroke of the same hue, `physicsPalette`, text >= 12px, readable at 360px. The set-up is one shared scene used by all the frames in sections 2 to 5: a bench, a small trolley with a piece of card on top, string running along the bench and over a pulley at the right-hand edge, a hook with a stack of small masses hanging beneath the pulley, a light gate (arch shape) over the track with a cable to a data logger box, and a starting line marked on the bench. Frames change what is highlighted or added.
