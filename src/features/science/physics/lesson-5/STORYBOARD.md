# Physics Lesson 5 storyboard — Specific heat capacity

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-5`, id `P-ENE-005-P`, skill `P-SHC`, spec 6.1.1.3. It owns energy transfer by heating raising the thermal store and temperature, the meaning of specific heat capacity, and ΔE = mcΔθ by substitution (find Δθ first). The specific heat capacity required practical is a later lesson.

Big idea: heating transfers energy to the thermal store and raises the temperature, but different materials need different amounts of energy. Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to warm 1 kg by 1 °C, and ΔE = m × c × Δθ works out the energy for any mass and temperature change.

Flow note: heating first (with a describing sentence pattern from the stores lessons), then the idea that materials differ, then the equation with the Greek letters explained, then a three-step worked example (Δθ, substitute, answer), then guided practice, then independent work. Calculation flow: worked (2 kg, c 900, 20 to 25 °C, 9000 J) → guided (3 kg, c 400, 10 to 30 °C, 24 000 J; 0.5 kg water, 60 to 40 °C, 42 000 J) → independent (4 kg, c 500, 15 to 25 °C, 20 000 J; 0.5 kg, c 800, 50 to 30 °C, 8000 J) → written (2 kg, c 500, 20 to 30 °C, 10 000 J).

Sections:
1. Start here (P5-01): water and oil on the same heater.
2. What does heating do? (P5-02–04): energy into the thermal store, an electric water heater, describing the change. Checks: the heater transfer; a cooling cup of tea.
3. What is specific heat capacity? (P5-05–07): materials differ, the definition, water needs a lot, a high value stores more. Checks: definition; what 4200 J/kg°C means.
4. What is the equation? (P5-08–10): words, symbols and units with Δ and θ explained, find the temperature change first. Checks: a temperature change; the unit.
5. How do you work one out? (P5-11–13): three-step worked example, then two guided calculations (one warming, one cooling).
6. On your own (P5-14–17): a numbered bar chart of four materials, two independent calculations, a written working task.

Out of scope: the required practical (a later lesson: it uses power and time to find the energy); rearranging the equation for c, m or Δθ; specific latent heat (a later lesson); the exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 169 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.1.3. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original (the electric heater in a water tank, copper and water, the metal blocks). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The temperature change is always found by subtracting the smaller from the larger, so students never meet a negative Δθ; the page example is a cooling block and both warming and cooling are practised.
- The unit is written J/kg°C as on the page.
- The value for copper (about 390 J/kg°C) is quoted only to show water needs far more energy; it is not something to memorise.

## Diagram specs
Look as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, `physicsPalette`, PhysicsKit thermal store badge and transfer arrows (same colours as everywhere), equation cards with colour-coded parts like Lesson 3. Thermometers are simple rounded tubes with a red column. Text at least 12px.

- `shc-heating`: a metal block above a flame or hot plate with a thermal-store badge that fills up, plus a thermometer showing the temperature rising from a lower to a higher reading; arrow "energy in, temperature up".
- `shc-heater`: a tank of water with an electric heater dipped in; transfer arrows "electrically" (mains to the heater's thermal badge) and "by heating" (heater to the water's thermal badge); a thermometer in the water.
- `shc-describe`: the same tank drawn simply, with a sentence frame beneath with each blank coloured: "electrically to the [heater], by heating to the [water], so the [temperature] increases".
- `shc-different`: two beakers, one of water and one of a copper block, each on the same heater with the same-length "same heating time" tag; the thermometer on the copper reads much higher than the water thermometer.
- `shc-definition`: a 1 kg block with a "+1 °C" thermometer step and a "how much energy?" arrow, the sentence "specific heat capacity = energy to warm 1 kg by 1 °C", and the unit "J/kg°C".
- `shc-water`: a 1 kg tank of water with "+1 °C" and "4200 J" beside it, and a smaller 1 kg copper block with "+1 °C" and "about 390 J" beside it.
- `shc-high`: two thermal stores drawn as jars of the same size: a large "high c" jar that fills slowly with a tag "stores lots of energy", and a small "low c" jar that fills fast.
- `shc-words`: an equation card in words "change in thermal energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change", each word coloured.
- `shc-symbols`: the card in symbols "ΔE = m × c × Δθ" with pointers naming each symbol and unit: ΔE (joules, J), m (kilograms, kg), c (J/kg°C), Δθ (°C); a small note "Δ = change in, θ = temperature".
- `shc-delta`: a thermometer or two thermometers reading "20 °C" and "25 °C" with a bracket labelled "Δθ = 25 − 20 = 5 °C".
Worked example frames share one drawing: a 2 kg metal block with "c = 900 J/kg°C" and two thermometers "20 °C" (start) and "25 °C" (end), a three-step strip along the top ("Δθ", "substitute", "answer") with the current step highlighted.
- `shc-work-1`: the block, its data tags and the temperature change "Δθ = 25 − 20 = 5 °C" highlighted.
- `shc-work-2`: the equation "ΔE = m × c × Δθ" and the substituted line "ΔE = 2 × 900 × 5".
- `shc-work-3`: the finished sum "2 × 900 = 1800", "1800 × 5 = 9000" and the answer "ΔE = 9000 J" in a highlighted box with J circled.
