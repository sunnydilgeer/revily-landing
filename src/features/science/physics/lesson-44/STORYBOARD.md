# Physics Lesson 44 storyboard — Acceleration

Chapter P5, Forces (motion). Folder `physics/lesson-44`, id `P-MOT-044-P`, skill `P-ACCEL`, spec 6.5.4.1.5. It owns a = Δv ÷ t, deceleration, estimating accelerations, v² − u² = 2as for uniform acceleration (one fully rearranged worked example), and g ≈ 9.8 m/s².

Big idea: acceleration is how quickly velocity changes, worked out as change in velocity ÷ time. Slowing down is negative acceleration. For constant acceleration over a distance, v² − u² = 2as.

Flow note: meaning and units first, then the calculation as one worked example with two guided items (speeding up, then slowing down), then estimating (which reuses typical speeds), then the harder equation last with every step shown. Calculation flow: a = Δv ÷ t worked (3 to 15 m/s in 4 s, 3 m/s²) → guided (0 to 20 in 5 s, 4 m/s²; 20 to 8 in 4 s, −3 m/s²) → independent (4 to 28 in 6 s, 4 m/s²). v² − u² = 2as worked (13 m/s, a = −2, 36 m, 5 m/s) → guided (from rest, a = 2, 16 m, 8 m/s) → independent (u = 2, a = 4, 4 m, 6 m/s).

Sections:
1. Start here (P44-01): pulling away from traffic lights.
2. What is acceleration? (P44-02–04): change in velocity, the equation, the unit, deceleration. Checks: unit of acceleration; meaning of −4 m/s².
3. How do you calculate it? (P44-05–07): write, find Δv, substitute, answer. Two guided calculations (speeding up, slowing down).
4. How do you estimate it? (P44-08–09): what an estimate is, a typical speed, work it out. Estimate check.
5. What is v² − u² = 2as? (P44-10–11): uniform acceleration and g, the equation, rearrange, substitute, square root. Guided calculation from rest.
6. On your own (P44-12–15): independent a = Δv ÷ t, independent v² − u² = 2as, g, written task.

Out of scope: unit conversions (km/h to m/s); non-perfect-square square roots; using g in the v² − u² equation as a calculation (the page question is not reused); acceleration from graphs (next lessons); Newton's second law (later lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 211 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.4.1.5. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:

- Deceleration examples use a negative a explicitly; the negative sign is explained in one frame.
- The estimate uses "typical speed" from the previous lesson as an idea, and the question gives the speed so it can be answered without recall.
