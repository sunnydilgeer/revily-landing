# Physics Lesson 31 storyboard — Developing the model of the atom

Chapter P4, Atomic structure. Folder `physics/lesson-31`, id `P-ATM-031-P`, skill `P-NUCMODEL`, spec 6.4.1.3. It owns the history of the atom model: solid spheres, electrons, plum pudding, alpha scattering (three observations, three conclusions), Bohr's energy levels, protons, neutrons. The idea that models change with new evidence runs through it. The modern nuclear model and particle table belong to the next lesson.

Big idea: scientists change a model when new evidence does not fit it. The gold foil experiment showed a tiny, positive, heavy nucleus in mostly empty space.

Flow note: the early models first (they are what the experiment tests), then the experiment as prediction → result → conclusions (the prediction is stated before the result so the surprise makes sense), then Bohr and the particles inside the nucleus, ending on a "put it together" timeline. Chemistry already teaches the atom, so this lesson links in a clause and tells the story physics-style.

Sections:
1. Start here (P31-01): why did the solid sphere idea change?
2. What did scientists first think? (P31-02–04): solid spheres, electrons found, plum pudding. Checks: where electrons are in the plum pudding model; which particle led to it.
3. What did the gold foil show? (P31-05–07): set-up, prediction, results, three observations → three conclusions. Checks: the prediction; what bouncing back means.
4. What came next? (P31-08–10): Bohr, protons, neutrons, timeline. Checks: Bohr's electrons; order of discovery.
5. On your own (P31-11–14): why scientists change models, a scattering diagram, deflection means positive nucleus, a written explanation.

Out of scope: how the alpha particles were detected, the size of the nucleus (next lesson), Higher-tier detail on energy level calculations.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 197 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.4.1.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Plum pudding is described as currants in a sticky pudding, because the page's image is not reused.
- Protons are said to be "named" after experiments divided the nucleus, and neutrons "found" by Chadwick about 20 years after the nucleus, as on the page.
- Three observations map one-to-one to three conclusions, as the spec expects.

## Diagram specs
Style as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, `physicsPalette`, one colour per particle everywhere in P4 (protons warm coral, neutrons grey, electrons blue, positive charge coral tint, negative blue tint). Text at least 12px; readable at 360px.

- `nucmodel-solid`: one plain solid sphere with a small "cannot be split" tag and a tiny crossed-out scissors or a "no parts inside" note.
- `nucmodel-electron`: the same sphere now with a few small blue electrons drawn breaking away, labelled "electron (negative)".
- `nucmodel-plum`: a big coral-tinted ball labelled "ball of positive charge" with small blue electrons scattered through it, label "electrons". A small side note "plum pudding model".
- `nucmodel-gold-setup`: a source on the left firing alpha particles (small coral pairs) as arrows at a thin gold foil (vertical yellow strip), a curved detector screen around the foil. Labels: "alpha particles", "thin gold foil", "detector".
- `nucmodel-gold-expect`: the same set-up; all arrows go straight through or bend slightly. Title "What the plum pudding model predicted". A small plum pudding ball beside the foil.
- `nucmodel-gold-results`: the same set-up with three groups of arrows: most straight through (label "most pass straight through"), a few at large angles ("a few deflected at large angles"), one bouncing straight back ("a tiny number bounce back").
- `nucmodel-gold-conclude`: the results drawing on the left with three numbered arrows to three conclusion cards on the right: "Most of the atom is empty space", "Most of the mass is in a tiny nucleus", "The nucleus is positively charged". Each card lines up with its observation.
- `nucmodel-bohr`: a nucleus with three dotted circular orbits and electrons on them; labels "nucleus", "energy levels", "electrons".
- `nucmodel-protons`: a nucleus drawn as a cluster splitting to show protons (coral, "+") with the label "protons, positive".
- `nucmodel-neutron`: the same nucleus with grey neutrons ("0") among the coral protons; label "neutrons, no charge" and a tiny "James Chadwick" caption.
- `nucmodel-timeline`: a left-to-right strip of four small pictures (solid sphere, plum pudding, nuclear model, Bohr model with protons and neutrons) with arrows between and the caption "new evidence changed the model".
