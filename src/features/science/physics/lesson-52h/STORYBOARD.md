# Physics Lesson 52H storyboard — Momentum

Chapter P5, Forces. A whole lesson that only Higher students get (catalogue entry in `higher/lessons.ts`; the badge is shown by the app, so no learner text names the tier). It sits after Lesson 52 (reaction times), at the end of chapter P5. Folder `physics/lesson-52h`, id `P-MOT-052H-P`, skill `P-MOMENTUM`. Visuals: `components/HigherMomentumVisuals.tsx` (focus prefix `hmom-`).

Big idea: anything moving has momentum, p = m v, in kg m/s. Momentum has a direction (it is a vector). In a closed system the total momentum before an event equals the total after. So in a collision the momentum is shared out, and when objects stick together the same momentum is carried by more mass, so they move more slowly. When things start at rest and push apart (an explosion, recoil) the total stays zero, so the parts move off in opposite directions.

Flow note: builds on velocity (speed in a given direction), Newton's Third Law (pushing apart) and the stopping-distance lessons (heavier, faster vehicles are harder to stop), without re-teaching them. The opening question checks that velocity has a direction, which the vector idea needs. The meaning comes first with familiar traffic (more mass, more speed, more momentum), then the equation and its direction. Calculating is taught on its own before conservation, so the collision sections only add one new idea: before = after. Collisions are worked one step at a time (momentum before → momentum after → divide by the moving mass). Explosions come last because they use conservation with a total of zero and negative momentum.

1. **Start here** (P52H-01): two cyclists at 5 m/s east and west: what is different (their velocity).
2. **What is momentum?** (P52H-02–04): two lanes of road, one drawing: parked car (0) beside a moving car (10 000 kg m/s) → 1000 kg car beside a 4000 kg lorry at 10 m/s (4×) → 10 m/s beside 20 m/s (2×) → p = m v card, kg m/s → right positive, the car going left has −10 000 kg m/s. Checks: which has zero momentum (the parked lorry); identical trolleys in opposite directions.
3. **Calculating momentum** (P52H-05–09): a skateboarder beside a working card: write p = m × v with units → 50 kg × 4 m/s = 200 → v = p ÷ m, 300 ÷ 50 = 6 m/s → m = p ÷ v, 240 ÷ 4 = 60 kg → three ways round. Worked: rowing boat, 600 ÷ 200 = 3 m/s. Practice: cricket ball 0.16 × 25 = 4 kg m/s; cyclist 450 ÷ 5 = 90 kg; 4 kg ball rolling left at 3 m/s = −12 kg m/s.
4. **Momentum before = momentum after** (P52H-10–12): two lab trolleys on a track, one drawing: closed system (no outside forces) → before: A 2 kg at 3 m/s = 6, B 1 kg still = 0 → after: A 1 m/s (2) and B 4 m/s (4), total 6, conservation of momentum → sticky pads: 3 kg, 6 ÷ 3 = 2 m/s → bounce and stick side by side. Checks: 3 kg at 4 m/s sticks to 1 kg (3 m/s); curling stones, B's momentum after (40 kg m/s, diagram).
5. **Explosions and recoil** (P52H-13–15): a skater on ice holding a 5 kg ball, one drawing: total zero → ball thrown at 6 m/s, +30 kg m/s → skater −30 kg m/s, 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5 m/s backwards, recoil → +30 + (−30) = 0, heavier part slower. Checks: trolleys pushed apart by a spring (total still zero); toy cannon recoil (1 m/s backwards).
6. **On your own** (P52H-16–20): three numbered vehicles, which has most momentum (assessment view; the slow van, not the parked lorry); two cars lock together (6 m/s); first mistake in a student's stick-together working (line 3 uses one mass); firework bursting into two pieces (10 m/s to the right); written task: a student jumps from a still boat, explain and calculate the boat's recoil (1 m/s backwards).

Numbers (each checked twice): traffic 1000 × 10 = 10 000; 4000 × 10 = 40 000; 1000 × 20 = 20 000 · skateboarder 50 × 4 = 200; 300 ÷ 50 = 6; 240 ÷ 4 = 60 · boat 600 ÷ 200 = 3 · cricket ball 0.16 × 25 = 4 (distractors 40, 0.0064 divided, 25.16 added) · cyclist 450 ÷ 5 = 90 (2250 multiplied, 445 subtracted, 0.011 inverted) · ball 4 × 3 = 12, left so −12 (−0.75 divided, +7 added) · trolleys 2 × 3 = 6; after 2 × 1 + 1 × 4 = 6 (this bounce also conserves kinetic energy, 9 J, so it is a real possible outcome); stuck 6 ÷ 3 = 2 · guided stick 3 × 4 = 12, 12 ÷ 4 = 3 · stones 20 × 3 = 60, 20 × 1 = 20, 60 − 20 = 40 (B then moves at 2 m/s; kinetic energy 90 J before, 50 J after, so it is possible) · skater 5 × 6 = 30; 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5 · toy cannon 0.1 × 20 = 2; 2 ÷ 2 = 1 · vehicles 10 000 × 0 = 0; 800 × 10 = 8000; 2000 × 5 = 10 000 · crash 1200 × 10 = 12 000; 12 000 ÷ 2000 = 6 (10 unchanged, 15 = ÷ 800, 4) · student working 2 × 3 = 6; mass should be 2 + 4 = 6, so 1 m/s (student: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5) · firework 0.2 × 15 = 3; 3 ÷ 0.3 = 10 (22.5 swapped masses) · boat 50 × 2 = 100; 100 ÷ 100 = 1.

Out of scope: force and rate of change of momentum (F = m Δv ÷ Δt; physics-only content, not in Combined Science); impulse; safety features and crumple zones; kinetic energy in collisions (only used in the storyboard to check the numbers are possible); collisions where both objects move before, or move towards each other; two-dimensional collisions; the book's examples, questions, cartoons and jokes.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 224 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.5.1 and 6.5.5.2 (HT only), with 6.5.4.1.3 for the opening question. The book's cheetah, boy, snooker balls, car crash picture and its gun-recoil question were replaced by traffic, a skateboarder, a rowing boat, lab trolleys, curling stones, a skater throwing a ball, a toy cannon, a firework and a student jumping from a boat. All wording, numbers, questions and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- A closed system is described as "no outside forces act", with friction and air resistance ignored (smooth track, ice). The boat task tells students to ignore the water.
- "Velocity" in the closed-system frames always means along one line, right positive; negative momentum is introduced once (P52H-09) and used in the explosion section.
- The toy cannon's 2 kg is the cannon without the ball, and the boat question uses the boat's mass alone (100 kg), not boat + student; both say "at rest" so the total before is clearly zero.
- Collision outcomes in the trolley and curling-stone examples were chosen to be physically possible (kinetic energy does not increase).
