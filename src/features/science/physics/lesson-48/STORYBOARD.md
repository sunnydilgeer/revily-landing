# Physics Lesson 48 storyboard — Newton's Third Law

Chapter P5, Forces (motion). Folder `physics/lesson-48`, id `P-MOT-048-P`, skill `P-NEWTON3`, spec 6.5.4.2.3. It owns Newton's Third Law: equal and opposite forces on different objects, the skater and wall examples, and the book-on-a-table trap.

Big idea: forces come in pairs between two objects. Each force of a pair acts on a different object, is the same type, is the same size and points the opposite way.

Flow note: the law itself with an everyday trolley, then the wall and the "how does anything move?" puzzle (the answer: different objects), then the trap, because the trap only makes sense once students know a pair acts on two objects. The book example is the last teaching section so students leave with a test they can apply: same type, different objects.

Sections:
1. Start here (P48-01): what a pushed trolley does back.
2. What does the Third Law say? (P48-02–04): equal and opposite → different objects → skaters → the lighter skater accelerates more. Checks: size and direction of the partner force; which objects.
3. If forces are equal, how does anything move? (P48-05–07): wall → same size → the puzzle → look at one object. Checks: size of the wall's force; why skaters can accelerate.
4. What is the book trap? (P48-08–10): book at rest → two forces → not a pair → why not → the real pair. Checks: choose the true pair; why weight and normal force are not a pair.
5. On your own (P48-11–15): a swimmer; two skaters with different masses; the tyre-and-road pair; the key facts of a pair; a written description of man and wall.

Out of scope: the gravitational pair (Earth pulls the book, the book pulls the Earth) beyond the type-of-force point; momentum; rockets; the exam question on the page (not reused).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 215 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.4.2.3. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original except the standard named ideas (skaters, wall, book on a table). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The book trap explains the false pair by "different types of force, both acting on the book". The gravity partner is not taught.
- The tyre-and-road question is answered with the road pushing the tyres forwards; a teacher may want to check this matches how friction is taught locally.
- The skater accelerations use "smaller mass, more acceleration" from the Second Law, with no calculation.

## Diagram specs
Look as in Lessons 17 and 18 and the other Physics lessons: soft flat fills, darker stroke of the same hue, `physicsPalette`, text >= 12px, readable at 360px. Each force is a labelled arrow drawn on the object it acts on; equal forces have equal length. Pair arrows are shown in two colours (one per object) so students can see which object each acts on.

Section 2:
- `newton3-law`: a person pushing a shopping trolley to the right; an arrow on the trolley "you push the trolley" (right) and an arrow on the person "trolley pushes you" (left), equal length; label "equal and opposite".
- `newton3-different`: the same picture with each arrow tinted to match the object it acts on and a small tag "acts on the trolley" / "acts on you".
- `newton3-skaters`: two ice skaters facing each other with their hands touching; arrows on each, pointing apart, equal length; labels "A pushes B" and "B pushes A".
- `newton3-skater-acc`: the same skaters now moving apart; the lighter skater has a long acceleration arrow, the heavier a short one; labelled "smaller mass, bigger acceleration". Masses shown as 50 kg and 65 kg.

Section 3:
- `newton3-wall`: a person pushing a brick wall; arrow "push" on the wall and arrow "normal contact force" on the person, equal length.
- `newton3-wall-equal`: the same, with a stronger push (both arrows longer) beside a weaker one (both shorter), labelled "push harder, pushed back harder".
- `newton3-puzzle`: two skaters with a question mark, and the arrows tinted by object; callout "forces on different objects do not cancel".
- `newton3-one-object`: one skater highlighted with only the arrow acting on her, the other skater faded; caption "look at the forces on one object".

Section 4 (one book-on-a-table scene reused):
- `newton3-book`: a book on a table with the label "equilibrium: resultant force zero".
- `newton3-book-forces`: adds a purple down arrow "weight" and a teal up arrow "normal contact force" on the book, equal length.
- `newton3-book-trap`: the same two arrows with a large caution badge "equal and opposite, but NOT a Third Law pair".
- `newton3-book-why`: the two arrows both circled on the book with tags "different types" and "both act on the book".
- `newton3-real-pair`: the up arrow on the book and a new down arrow on the table (tinted the same pair colour, labelled "book pushes table"), the weight arrow faded; caption "same type, different objects".
