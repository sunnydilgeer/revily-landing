# Physics Lesson 2 storyboard — Conservation of energy

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-2`, id `P-ENE-002-P`, skill `P-CONSERVE`, spec 6.1.1.1 and 6.1.2.1. It owns the conservation of energy principle, the one-clause idea of dissipation, work done = energy transferred, and describing store changes in everyday situations. Detail on wasted energy belongs to the lesson on reducing unwanted energy transfers.

Big idea: energy is never created or destroyed. It is transferred usefully, stored or dissipated, and a force moving an object transfers energy mechanically. Any change can be described by saying which stores lose and gain energy.

Flow note: the principle first (the rule that everything else obeys), then work done (the mechanism behind mechanical transfers), then four described examples that put both to use, so the examples feel like applications, not new facts.

Sections:
1. Start here (P2-01): a ball that bounces lower each time.
2. Is energy ever lost? (P2-02–04): never destroyed, three things can happen, dissipated is not gone, true every time. Checks: the principle; a warm phone.
3. How do forces move energy? (P2-05–07): a force moving an object, work done = energy transferred (joules), transferred mechanically. Checks: work done; a sledge.
4. How do you describe a change? (P2-08–10): ball thrown, ball dropped, bike braking, car hitting a wall. Checks: a falling ball, a braking bike.
5. On your own (P2-11–14): a numbered falling-ball diagram, a sliding box, a "used up" misconception, a written braking explanation.

Out of scope: calculating work done (force times distance); wasted energy in detail, lubrication, insulation (a later lesson); kinetic and g.p.e. equations (the next lessons).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 167 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.1.1, 6.1.2.1. All wording, examples and diagrams are original except that the four generic situations the page names (ball thrown up, ball dropped, vehicle braking, vehicle hitting a wall) are described in plain words with a sledge, a cyclist and a phone as the extra examples. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Dissipation is introduced only as "spread out and wasted, usually into the thermal store of the surroundings".
- The braking example is a bike in the frames and a car in the written task, so the same idea is met twice.
- A crash lists elastic potential and thermal stores as the page does; sound is not mentioned.

## Diagram specs
Shared look as Lesson 1: soft rounded shapes, `physicsPalette`, PhysicsKit store badges (same colours as Lesson 1) and transfer arrows. Text at least 12px.

Section 2 frames share one drawing of a simple "energy in a system" scene: a box labelled "system" with an energy bar on the left and three arrows leaving it on the right.
- `conserve-principle`: energy shown as a coin-like token that can move between two store badges, with a crossed-out "created" and a crossed-out "destroyed" beside it. Caption "energy is never created or destroyed".
- `conserve-three`: a system box with three arrows out: "transferred usefully" (green tint), "stored" (blue tint), "dissipated" (grey tint, arrows fading outwards).
- `conserve-dissipated`: a warm phone with thin grey arrows spreading out into the air around it; label "energy spreads into the surroundings, still there but hard to use".
- `conserve-every-time`: two equal bars labelled "energy before" and "energy after" joined by an equals sign, with one store badge in each bar changing shape from the first to the second.
Section 3 shares one drawing: a girl pulling a sledge on snow with a force arrow.
- `conserve-work`: the sledge and girl, a force arrow along the rope, a movement arrow; label "force moves the object: work is done".
- `conserve-work-energy`: the same, with an equals card "work done = energy transferred" and the unit "joules (J)" on both.
- `conserve-mechanical`: the same, with a transfer arrow labelled "mechanically" from the girl's "Chemical" badge to the sledge's "Kinetic" badge.
Section 4: four small scenes, one per frame, each with store badges and a labelled transfer arrow.
- `conserve-thrown`: a person throwing a ball up; arrow "mechanically" from "Chemical" (person) to "Kinetic" and "Gravitational potential" (ball).
- `conserve-dropped`: a ball falling from a shelf, a downward gravity arrow; arrow "mechanically" from "Gravitational potential" to "Kinetic".
- `conserve-braking`: a cyclist braking, brake pad on the wheel highlighted; arrow "mechanically" from "Kinetic" to "Thermal" (brakes, wheels, surroundings) with a small friction arrow.
- `conserve-crash`: a car touching a wall, contact force arrows on both; arrow "mechanically" from "Kinetic" to "Elastic potential" and "Thermal" (car and wall) plus a faint "other stores" tag.
Question visual:
- `conserve-q-drop` (assessment view): a ball drawn at three heights beside a tall wall or shelf, labelled "Position 1" (top), "Position 2" (halfway), "Position 3" (just above the floor), a plain downward arrow. No speeds, no store names, no energy bars. Neutral accessible description: "A ball shown at three heights as it falls."
