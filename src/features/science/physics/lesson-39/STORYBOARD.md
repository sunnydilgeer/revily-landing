# Physics Lesson 39 storyboard — Weight, mass and gravity

Chapter P5, Forces. Folder `physics/lesson-39`, id `P-FOR-039-P`, skill `P-WEIGHT`, spec 6.5.1.3. It owns mass versus weight, centre of mass, gravitational field strength (g = 9.8 N/kg on Earth, 1.6 N/kg on the Moon), the newtonmeter, W = mg, one rearrangement (m = W ÷ g), and W ∝ m.

Big idea: mass is how much stuff there is and never changes; weight is the pull of gravity on that mass, so it changes with where you are.

Flow note: the difference between mass and weight first (a diagnostic on flour), then the calculation with one worked example (20 kg on Earth), guided practice on the Moon, then the rearrangement and proportionality once students can already substitute. The calculation flow is worked example → near-identical guided (5 kg on the Moon) → independent (60 kg on Earth) plus independent rearrangement (392 N).

Sections:
1. Start here (P39-01): what does 1 kg on a bag of flour tell you?
2. What is the difference between mass and weight? (P39-02–04): mass → weight → gravitational field strength → Moon → newtonmeter. Checks: astronaut to the Moon; instrument and unit.
3. How do you calculate weight? (P39-05–07): equation → substitute → calculate → unit. Checks: Moon calculation; why less on the Moon.
4. How do you find mass, and what is proportional? (P39-08–10): rearrange → example → proportional → graph. Checks: mass from weight; doubling mass.
5. On your own (P39-11–14): 60 kg on Earth; mass from 392 N; reading a table; written mass and weight on the Moon.

Out of scope: gravitational field strength for other planets, the inverse-square law, deriving g, proportional graphs beyond one picture.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 205 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.1.3. All wording, examples and numbers are original (the book's motorcycle and 5 kg examples are not reused). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls: g is always given; decimals such as 9.8 and 1.6 are kept because the specification uses them; the rearrangement is shown fully in one worked example only.

- `weight-moon`: two panels: the same 10 kg object on Earth (long arrow "98 N") and on the Moon (short arrow "16 N"); labels "mass 10 kg" on both and "g = 9.8 N/kg", "g = 1.6 N/kg".
- `weight-meter`: a newtonmeter (spring balance) with a mass hanging from it and the pointer on a scale in N; label "newtonmeter".

Section 4:
