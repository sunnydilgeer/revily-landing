# Physics Lesson 38 storyboard — Contact and non-contact forces

Chapter P5, Forces. Folder `physics/lesson-38`, id `P-FOR-038-P`, skill `P-FORCEBASICS`, spec 6.5.1.1 and 6.5.1.2. It owns scalar versus vector, force arrows, contact versus non-contact forces, and interaction pairs (one idea, equal and opposite; Newton's Third Law is taught in full later).

Big idea: a force is a push or a pull with a size and a direction, drawn as an arrow; forces can act by touching or across a gap, and always come in pairs.

Flow note: vectors and arrows first because every later force diagram needs them, then contact and non-contact, then interaction pairs, which need both ideas (a pair can be contact or non-contact).

Sections:
1. Start here (P38-01): which of these is a force?
2. What is a vector? (P38-02–04): size and direction → scalars → arrows → comparing arrows. Checks: vector versus scalar; reading arrow lengths.
3. Contact or non-contact? (P38-05–07): push or pull → contact → non-contact → sorting. Checks: identify non-contact; the brake.
4. What is an interaction pair? (P38-08–10): both objects feel a force → equal and opposite → Earth and Moon. Checks: book on table; Earth and Sun.
5. On your own (P38-11–15): non-contact on a football; vector list; arrow length to force; rope pull; written contact versus non-contact.

Out of scope: Newton's Third Law in full (later), calculating pairs, free-body diagrams with several forces, drag, electrostatics and magnetism detail.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 204 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.1.1 and 6.5.1.2. All wording and examples are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls: the book's examples of a tennis ball and Earth-Sun are replaced with a football, brake blocks and Earth-Moon; the Earth-Sun pair appears in one question.

## Diagram specs
Look as in Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, gentle tints, hand-drawn feeling, text >= 12px, readable at 360px. Use `physicsPalette` and PhysicsKit helpers (graph axes, energy-store badges, transfer arrows). Walkthrough frames in one section reuse one drawing and change the highlight. Force arrows are one colour per force, with the size in newtons on the label; the arrow length is proportional to the force (say 1 cm per 5 N).

Section 2:
- `contact-vector`: a box with a push arrow to the right; labels "size: 20 N" and "direction: right"; heading "vector: size and direction".
- `contact-scalar`: two cards: "scalar: size only" with mass, time, speed, distance, temperature, and "vector: size and direction" with force, velocity, acceleration, displacement.
- `contact-arrow`: a trolley with an arrow; annotations "length = size" and "arrow points the way the force acts".
- `contact-compare`: a box with a 10 N arrow to the right and a 5 N arrow to the left, the right arrow twice as long; labels "10 N" and "5 N", note "twice as long, twice as big".

Section 3:
- `contact-force`: a hand pushing and a hand pulling a crate; labels "push", "pull", "force in newtons (N)".
- `contact-touch`: three small scenes: brake blocks on a wheel (friction), a rope pulling a sledge (tension), a book on a table (contact force from the table); heading "contact forces: objects touch".
- `contact-nontouch`: a magnet and a paper clip with a gap, a ball falling towards the Earth, a charged balloon near hair; heading "non-contact forces: a gap between the objects".
- `contact-sort`: two columns "touch needed" and "no touch needed" with the small pictures placed in them; no extra text beyond labels.

Section 4:
- `contact-interact`: two people on skateboards pushing each other, arrows on both; label "a force on each".

- `contact-earthmoon`: the Earth and Moon (not to scale) with attraction arrows towards each other, equal length; label "same size, opposite directions".
