# Physics Lesson 61 storyboard — Investigating infrared absorption

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-61`, id `P-WAV-061-P`, skill `P-WAV-061-P`, spec 6.6.2.2 (required practical: infrared absorption). It owns the melting-wax method, the fair test, and "black surfaces are good absorbers" (linked to good emitters from the previous lesson).

Big idea: a plate that absorbs infrared radiation faster warms its wax faster, so its ball falls first, and black surfaces absorb best.

Flow note: the idea and set-up first, then the method in working order (wax, distance, watch), then what the result means and the link to emission. The lesson prepares students for the practical and never claims to complete it.

Sections:
1. Start here (P61-01): black and white cars in the sun.
2. What are we testing? (P61-02–04): absorption depends on the surface → the equipment → the set-up. Checks: what emits the infrared; what differs between plates.
3. How do you run it? (P61-05–07): fix the balls with wax → same distance → watch and record. Checks: why harden the wax; why the same distance.
4. What do the results show? (P61-08–10): energy absorbed → which ball falls → black is a good absorber. Checks: which ball; the conclusion.
5. On your own (P61-11–14): numbered set-up; an unfair change; solar panel; written method with fair-test points.

Out of scope: emission with a Leslie cube (previous lesson), calculations, reflection of infrared.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 229 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.2. All wording and examples are original (cars and solar panels instead of the page's clothes). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Safety is one clause in the last method frame (hot burner, plates and wax).
- The energy-store phrase used: energy transferred by radiation to the thermal store of the wax.
