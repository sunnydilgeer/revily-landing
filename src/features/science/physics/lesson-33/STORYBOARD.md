# Physics Lesson 33 storyboard — Isotopes

Chapter P4, Atomic structure. Folder `physics/lesson-33`, id `P-ATM-033-P`, skill `P-ISOTOPE`, spec 6.4.1.2. It owns atomic number, mass number, nuclide notation (oxygen-16), isotopes, counting neutrons, and one clause saying unstable isotopes decay. Radiation itself belongs to the next lesson.

Big idea: the atomic number (protons) names the element. Isotopes share it but have different numbers of neutrons, so different mass numbers.

Flow note: the two numbers first (definitions), then the calculation that uses them (neutrons = mass number − atomic number, shown fully before practice), then isotopes (which need "same protons, different neutrons"). Calculation flow: worked example ¹⁶₈O (8 neutrons) → near-identical guided items (¹⁹₉F: 10; ⁷₃Li: 4) → independent (³⁵₁₇Cl: 18) → applied (atomic number 6, mass number 14: 8 neutrons).

Sections:
1. Start here (P33-01): which number decides the element?
2. What do the two numbers mean? (P33-02–04): atomic number, mass number, notation. Checks: meaning of the 8 in ¹⁶₈O; definition of mass number.
3. How do you count the neutrons? (P33-05–07): method, worked example, electrons in a neutral atom, two guided calculations.
4. What are isotopes? (P33-08–10): definition, carbon-12 and carbon-14, unstable isotopes. Checks: pick the isotope pair; what stays the same.
5. On your own (P33-11–14): a neutron calculation, a nuclei diagram, an atom described by numbers, a written explanation.

Out of scope: relative atomic mass from isotope abundances, reasoning about ions' neutrons, radiation properties (next lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 199, top (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.4.1.2. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Every calculation is a subtraction with whole numbers; no unit conversions arise.
- Carbon-14 is named as unstable in one clause only; the next lesson teaches decay.
- The page's oxygen-18 example is not reused; carbon and lithium are used instead.
