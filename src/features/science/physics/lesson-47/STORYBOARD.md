# Physics Lesson 47 storyboard — Newton's First and Second Laws

Chapter P5, Forces (motion). Folder `physics/lesson-47`, id `P-MOT-047-P`, skill `P-NEWTON12`, spec 6.5.4.2.1 and 6.5.4.2.2. It owns Newton's First Law (zero resultant force, the five changes in motion) and Newton's Second Law (proportionality, F = ma by substitution, an estimate of the force on a car). The practical is the next lesson; Newton's Third Law follows after that.

Big idea: motion only changes when the forces are unbalanced. The bigger the resultant force, the bigger the acceleration; the bigger the mass, the smaller the acceleration.

Flow note: the First Law first, because "zero resultant force" is the idea the Second Law builds on. The Second Law is taught as words (more force, more acceleration; more mass, less acceleration) before the equation. Calculation flow: worked estimate (car reaching 20 m/s in 10 s, mass 1000 kg, force 2000 N) → near-identical guided item (sledge 60 kg at 0.5 m/s² = 30 N) → independent calculation (car 800 kg at 1.5 m/s² = 1200 N). No rearranging of F = ma.

Sections:
1. Start here (P47-01): a bus at a steady speed.
2. What is Newton's First Law? (P47-02–04): stationary → moving → balanced forces on a bus → a resultant force changes motion → five changes. Checks: skater with zero resultant force; a non-change.
3. What is Newton's Second Law? (P47-05–07): bigger force → directly proportional → bigger mass → F = ma. Checks: empty against full trolley; doubling the force.
4. How do you use F = ma? (P47-08–10): estimate a → estimate m → substitute → unit. Checks: sledge calculation; unit of force.
5. On your own (P47-11–15): independent calculation; two trolleys compared; a puck on ice; doubling the force with numbers; a written task about a loaded van.

Out of scope: rearranging F = ma; inertia as a named idea; the word "inversely proportional" is left out of the learner text (the idea, more mass gives less acceleration, is taught); Newton's Third Law; the exam question on the page (not reused).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 214 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.5.4.2.1 and 6.5.4.2.2. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The estimate uses 20 m/s in 10 s (a = 2 m/s²) for friendlier arithmetic than a typical 25 m/s.
- "Directly proportional" is taught as "double one, the other doubles". The mass link is stated as "more mass, less acceleration" only.
- The bus example says the driving force equals the resistive forces (the real-life case).
