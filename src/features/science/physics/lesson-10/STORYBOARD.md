# Physics Lesson 10 storyboard — Energy resources and their uses

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-10`, id `P-RES-010-P`, skill `P-ENERGY-RESOURCES`, spec 6.1.3. It owns the overview: renewable and non-renewable lists, the general good and bad points, and the uses for transport and heating (and, in one clause, generating electricity). The detailed pros and cons of wind, solar and geothermal, water power, bio-fuels and fossil fuels, and the trends lesson, follow in later lessons.

Big idea: some resources run out (fossil fuels and nuclear fuel) and some never do (the renewable ones). Both kinds are used to make electricity, to move vehicles and to heat buildings.

Flow note: sort the resources first (the list students must know), then show them at work in transport, then in heating. Electricity is used in both, so it links back to "which resource made the electricity?".

Sections:
1. Start here (P10-01): which of four things will one day run out.
2. Which resources run out? (P10-02–04): what we use them for → non-renewable → renewable → good and bad points. Checks: a list of only renewables; why natural gas is not renewable.
3. How do vehicles use them? (P10-05–07): fuels for vehicles → bio-fuels → electric vehicles. Checks: a petrol and bio-fuel mix; an electric train.
4. How do we heat buildings? (P10-08–10): burning for heat → heat from the ground → heat from the Sun → electric heating. Checks: how a gas boiler works; heating without burning fuel.
5. On your own (P10-11–15): numbered resource cards (which are non-renewable); a correct statement about renewables; a geothermal heat pump; a reason fossil fuels are chosen; a written comparison.

Out of scope: how each renewable resource generates electricity, pros and cons of individual resources, greenhouse gases and acid rain, cost, and trends (later lessons); percentages of the energy mix.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 174 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original; the page's cartoons and exam question are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Bio-fuel is listed as renewable, as on the page. Nuclear fuel is listed as non-renewable.
- The "reliable, will run out, damage the environment" summary matches the page's pros and cons boxes; detail is left to later lessons.
- The heating frames use "energy from hot rocks" for geothermal heat pumps at the page's level, not the physics of a heat pump.

## Diagram specs
Same look as Science Lessons 17 and 18 and the other Physics lessons: soft flat fills with a darker stroke of the same hue, PhysicsKit `physicsPalette`. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px, readable on 360px. Two consistent colour families across the resource lessons: non-renewable in a warm dark tone, renewable in a fresh green; icons are simple and rounded.

- `eres-uses`: three panels with simple icons: a power station and pylon "generate electricity", a car and train "transport", a house with radiators "heating".
- `eres-nonrenew`: a "non-renewable" panel with four icons: coal lumps, an oil barrel, a gas flame (natural gas), a nuclear fuel rod. A bracket over the first three "fossil fuels". A fading fuel gauge "will run out one day".
- `eres-renew`: a "renewable" panel with seven icons: Sun, wind turbine, waves, hydro-electric dam, bio-fuel plant, tide (moon and water), geothermal (hot rocks). A refilling gauge "replaced as it is used".
- `eres-compare`: two columns "non-renewable" and "renewable". Non-renewable: a tick "reliable", crosses "will run out", "damage the environment". Renewable: ticks "never run out", "less harmful", crosses "some damage", "some unreliable (weather)".
- `eres-fuels`: an oil barrel with an arrow to a petrol pump and diesel pump and then to a car. Label "made from oil: non-renewable".
- `eres-biofuel`: a plant with an arrow to a fuel pump labelled "bio-fuel" and a car, plus a small "mix with petrol or diesel" tag. Label "renewable".
- `eres-electric`: a train and a car plugged in to a pylon, with an arrow back to two small icons "renewable or non-renewable resource". Label "generated first".
- `eres-boiler`: a gas flame under a boiler, a pipe to a radiator in a room. Labels "natural gas burnt", "hot water", "radiator".
- `eres-geothermal`: a house with a pipe going down into hot rocks under the ground (warm tint). Label "geothermal heat pump: energy from hot rocks".
- `eres-solar-heater`: a solar water heater panel on a roof with a pipe to a radiator. Label "sunlight heats water".
- `eres-electric-heat`: an electric heater plugged into a socket with an arrow back to "power station: renewable or non-renewable". 
- `eres-q-cards` (question, assessment view): four numbered cards in a row: 1 coal (lumps), 2 wind turbine, 3 nuclear fuel rod, 4 tides (moon and waves). Each card shows the icon and its name; no renewable/non-renewable words or colour coding. Neutral description: "Four numbered energy resource cards."
