# Physics Lesson 17 storyboard — Investigating resistance in a wire

Chapter P2, Electricity. Folder `physics/lesson-17`, id `P-ELE-017-P`, skill `P-WIRE`, spec 6.2.1.3 (required practical). It owns the wire-length investigation: variables, the circuit (ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel, crocodile clips on a metre ruler), the method and its safety point, R = V ÷ I for each length, and the resistance–length graph. It prepares students for the practical and never claims to replace it.

Big idea: the longer the wire, the greater its resistance, and the graph is a straight line through the origin, so resistance is directly proportional to length. Good technique (a thin straight wire, opening the switch to keep the wire cool) is part of getting a trustworthy graph.

Flow note: set-up first (what changes, what is measured, where each meter goes), then the method as a short routine, then results, because the calculation and graph only make sense once students know what the readings are. Calculation flow: worked example in the frames (0.60 V, 0.30 A = 2.0 Ω for the 10 cm length, matching the results table) → near-identical guided item (1.5 V, 0.50 A) → independent calculation on your own (2.4 V, 0.60 A). R = V ÷ I is shown as a rearrangement of V = IR from the resistance lesson.

Sections:
1. Start here (P17-01): short wire or long wire.
2. What are we measuring, and how is it wired? (P17-02–04): the question and variables → series loop with ammeter → voltmeter in parallel → crocodile clips on a ruler. Checks: where the ammeter goes; the independent variable.
3. How do you take the readings? (P17-05–07): set the first length → take the readings → open the switch to cool → repeat. Checks: why open the switch; order of one reading.
4. What do the results show? (P17-08–10): R = V ÷ I → table → graph and line of best fit → straight line through the origin. Checks: R from 1.5 V and 0.50 A; meaning of the straight line.
5. On your own (P17-11–15): fair test, an independent calculation, reading a graph (assessment view), predicting a doubled length, and a written method with a safety point.

Out of scope: resistivity, cross-sectional area, uncertainty and error bars, resistors in series and parallel (own lessons), I–V graphs.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 182 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.2.1.3. All wording, numbers, results and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's aside about series and parallel wiring is left to the later series/parallel lessons.
- The heating point is stated simply (heating changes resistance, so open the switch between readings); no detail about why resistance rises with temperature.
- Only "resistance is directly proportional to length" is stated for the graph; gradient is not used.
