# Physics Lesson 29 storyboard — Internal energy and changes of state

Chapter P3, Particle model of matter. Folder `physics/lesson-29`, id `P-PRT-029-P`, skill `P-INTERNAL-ENERGY`, spec 6.3.1.2 and 6.3.2.1. It owns internal energy (kinetic and potential stores of particles), what heating does (temperature rise or change of state), the names of the changes of state, and why a change of state is physical and mass is conserved. Latent heat itself belongs to the next lesson; states of matter and physical/chemical change are also met in Chemistry.

Big idea: the internal energy of a system is the total energy its particles have in their kinetic and potential energy stores. Heating increases it, giving either a temperature rise or a change of state. A change of state changes the arrangement of the same particles, so the mass stays the same.

Flow note: internal energy first (the words kinetic and potential, then the total), then heating (two outcomes), then the names of the changes, then what is conserved. The names come after heating so students first see why a change of state happens.

Sections:
1. Start here (P29-01): an ice cube melting in a hand.
2. What is internal energy? (P29-02–04): kinetic store → potential store → the total. Checks: definition; why kinetic.
3. What does heating do? (P29-05–07): energy in → two outcomes → temperature change → change of state. Checks: effect on internal energy; where the energy goes at melting.
4. What are the changes of state called? (P29-08–10): solid and liquid → liquid and gas → solid to gas → cooling too. Checks: gas to liquid; a numbered-arrow diagram.
5. What stays the same? (P29-11–12): physical change → reversible → same number of particles → mass conserved. Check: 200 g of ice.
6. On your own (P29-13–16): mass of steam; where melting energy goes; a true statement; a written explanation.

Out of scope: latent heat and E = mL (next lesson); sublimation examples and exotic changes; the exam question on the page was not reused.
