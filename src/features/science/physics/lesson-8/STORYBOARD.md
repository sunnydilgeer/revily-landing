# Physics Lesson 8 storyboard — Reducing unwanted energy transfers

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-8`, id `P-ENE-008-P`, skill `P-INSULATION`, spec 6.1.2.1. It owns wasted and dissipated energy in detail, lubrication, conduction, thermal conductivity, thermal insulators, and the three things that set the rate of cooling of a building. The conservation of energy idea is only touched (wasted energy still exists); efficiency is the next lesson.

Big idea: some energy is always wasted, spread into thermal stores we do not want. We cannot destroy it, but we can reduce it, with lubrication (less friction) and insulation (slower transfer by heating).

Flow note: where wasted energy goes first (useful, dissipated, never destroyed), then the two remedies in order of simplicity (lubrication, then conduction and insulation), then the house as one applied case that uses the insulation ideas.

Sections:
1. Start here (P8-01): a warm phone.
2. Where does wasted energy go? (P8-02–04): useful and wasted → dissipated → never destroyed → two ways to waste less. Checks: identify the wasted transfer in a lamp; what happens to wasted energy.
3. How does lubrication help? (P8-05–06): friction wastes energy → a lubricant → less friction, less waste. Check: oil on a bicycle chain.
4. How does insulation work? (P8-07–09): conduction → thermal conductivity → conductors and insulators → insulators cut waste. Checks: the best saucepan handle; what low conductivity means.
5. How do we keep a house warm? (P8-10–12): a house cools → thicker walls → low conductivity walls → adding insulation. Checks: which change slows cooling; 20 cm against 40 cm brick.
6. On your own (P8-13–16): energy dissipated (300 − 220); a numbered house diagram (which arrow loft insulation reduces); a table of three insulating materials; a written answer on two changes.

Out of scope: how conduction works with particles; convection and radiation; U-values and cost of insulation; payback times; efficiency calculations (next lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 172 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.2.1. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original; the page's surfer-in-oil picture, mobile phone box and exam question are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Conduction is described only as energy passing gradually through an object, as on the page, with no particle explanation.
- The lamp and the phone are used for wasted energy with stores named as in the rest of the course (electrical to light useful; thermal store of surroundings wasted).
- "Energy has further to travel through thick walls" is a simple reason for the thickness rule, not a formula.
