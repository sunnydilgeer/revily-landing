# Physics Lesson 28 storyboard — Density

Chapter P3, Particle model of matter. Folder `physics/lesson-28`, id `P-PRT-028-P`, skill `P-DENSITY`, spec 6.3.1.1 including the required practical on density. It owns density as mass per volume, particle packing, ρ = m/V (one worked example, near-identical guided practice, an independent calculation), and the methods for a regular solid, an irregular solid (eureka can) and a liquid.

Big idea: density says how much mass is packed into a certain space. Denser materials have more tightly packed particles. You find it by measuring mass and volume and dividing.

Flow note: meaning and particle packing first, then the equation on its own with one calculation, then the methods, because measuring only makes sense once students know that mass and volume are the two things needed. Calculation flow: worked example (steel, kg and m³) → near-identical guided item (aluminium) → independent calculation on your own (g and cm³). Unit conversions between cm³ and m³ are avoided by using g/cm³ or given values.

Sections:
1. Start here (P28-01): feathers or nails in equal boxes.
2. What is density? (P28-02–04): mass in a space → packed particles → solids, liquids, gases → the equation. Checks: meaning; order of density in the states.
3. How do you calculate density? (P28-05–07): choose the equation → substitute → calculate → unit. Checks: aluminium block; a unit of density.
4. How do you measure a solid? (P28-08–10): regular solid → volume of a cuboid → irregular solid → eureka can. Checks: volume of a stone; equipment for a cuboid.
5. How do you measure a liquid? (P28-11–12): zero the balance → density of a liquid → taking care. Check: why zero the balance.
6. On your own (P28-13–16): a density in g/cm³; a stone in a eureka can; comparing cubes; a written method.

Out of scope: converting cm³ to m³; floating and sinking; the exam question on the page was not reused. The required practical is prepared here (method, variables, care, safety); the online lesson does not replace doing it.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 194 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.3.1.1. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Worked and guided examples use given volumes in m³; the on-your-own item uses g/cm³, so no unit conversion is required.
- The liquid method uses zeroing the balance, as on the page, and 50 cm³ (the page's 50 ml).
- Variables and care (eye level, repeat and mean, spills) are in one frame, not a full planning task.
