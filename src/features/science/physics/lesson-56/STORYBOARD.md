# Physics Lesson 56 storyboard — Refraction

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-56`, id `P-WAV-056-P`, skill `P-REFRACT`, spec 6.6.2.2. It owns refraction as a change of direction at a boundary, the parts of a ray diagram (ray, boundary, normal, incident ray, refracted ray, the two angles), the five construction steps and protractor use.

Big idea: a wave that meets a boundary at an angle can change direction. A ray diagram shows this with straight lines, and every angle is measured from the normal.

Flow note: the idea first (boundary, refraction, needs an angle, depends on the materials), then the vocabulary of the diagram before anyone draws (a student cannot follow "draw the normal" until the normal has a name), then the five drawing steps, then the protractor as one worked example, because accurate angles are the hardest part. Wavefronts and the "bends towards or away from the normal" rule are left out: the page does not ask for them.

Sections:
1. Start here (P56-01): a bent straw.
2. What is refraction? (P56-02–04): boundary → change of direction → needs an angle → depends on both materials. Checks: air to glass at an angle; ray at a right angle.
3. What do the lines mean? (P56-05–07): ray → normal → incident ray → angle of incidence → refracted ray and angle of refraction. Checks: where the angle is measured; what the normal is.
4. How do you draw a ray diagram? (P56-08–10): boundary → normal → incident ray → refracted ray → mark angles. Checks: order of the steps; why use a ruler.
5. How do you use a protractor? (P56-11–12): centre point → base line on the normal → mark 40° → draw the ray. Check: which line the base line lies along.
6. On your own (P56-13–16): a numbered ray diagram; spot the error (angle from the boundary); a right-angle ray versus an angled ray; a written method.

Out of scope: wavefronts and changes in wave speed; the rule for bending towards or away from the normal; refractive index; Higher-tier ideas. The exam question on the page (55° and 35°) was not reused; the worked angle is 40° and the practice angle 60°.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 224 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The diagrams show light going from air into glass, so the refracted ray is drawn closer to the normal. The text never states this as a rule; teachers may wish to add it.
- No worked value is given for the angle of refraction; students draw the incident ray only, as the page does.
- The straw opener is used for the idea only; the explanation is kept to "light changes direction at the surface".

## Diagram specs
Look as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, `physicsPalette`, light rays in the palette's light colour, rays as straight lines with small arrowheads, boundary as a solid line, normal as a dotted line. Text at least 12px. Air is a pale tint above the boundary, glass a pale blue tint below. Angle arcs are thin coloured arcs. All diagrams are original schematics, not to scale.

Section P56-02 (one drawing that builds up):
- `refract-boundary`: a pale rectangle of air above and glass below, the boundary line between them labelled "boundary", labels "air" and "glass". A ray arrives from the top left; nothing is drawn beyond the boundary yet.
- `refract-idea`: the same drawing; the ray continues into the glass in a new direction (bent at the boundary). Label "refraction: the ray changes direction". A small side picture of a glass of water with a straw drawn bent at the surface.
- `refract-angle`: two side-by-side panels, same boundary. Left "meets the boundary at an angle": ray bends, tick. Right "goes straight in, at a right angle": ray goes straight through with no bend, labelled "no change of direction".
- `refract-materials`: two small panels: "air to glass" with a ray bending by one amount, and "air to water" with the same incoming ray bending by a slightly different amount. Captions "two materials, one bend", "different materials, different bend". No numbers.
