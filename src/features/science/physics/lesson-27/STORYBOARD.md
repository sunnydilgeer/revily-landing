# Physics Lesson 27 storyboard — The particle model and gas pressure

Chapter P3, Particle model of matter. Folder `physics/lesson-27`, id `P-PRT-027-P`, skill `P-GAS-PARTICLES`, spec 6.3.1.1 and 6.3.3.1. It owns the three states in the particle model (arrangement, forces and energy), how gas particles create pressure, and why a hotter gas at fixed volume has a higher pressure. States of matter are met in Chemistry; this lesson focuses on the energy of the particles and on gas pressure.

Big idea: the particles are the same in every state; what differs is their arrangement, the forces between them and their energy. Gas particles hit the walls and create pressure; heating makes them faster, so they hit harder and more often (if the volume stays the same).

Flow note: the three states first (a compact comparison), then pressure as collisions (the simplest idea), then temperature, because "hotter" needs both the energy of particles (first section) and collisions (second section).

Sections:
1. Start here (P27-01): which state fills its container.
2. How do particles differ in the three states? (P27-02–05): same particles → solid → liquid → gas. Checks: fixed regular pattern; ice and water particles; most energy.
3. What makes gas pressure? (P27-06–08): free to move → collisions → force over an area. Checks: cause of pressure; what pressure is.
4. Why does a hotter gas push harder? (P27-09–11): temperature and kinetic energy → faster → harder and more often → fixed volume. Checks: speed when heated; why pressure rises.
5. On your own (P27-12–15): a particle diagram of a liquid; a can in a hot car; pressure readings; a written explanation for cooling.

Out of scope: gas laws and pressure–volume calculations (not in the Foundation content shown); the exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 193 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.3.1.1, 6.3.3.1. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "Average kinetic energy" is used for temperature, as on the page; particle speeds in a gas are described as "a range of high speeds".
- Pressure is defined as force over a given area, with no calculation.
- The liquid diagram question tests arrangement (close but irregular), the commonest confusion with solids.
