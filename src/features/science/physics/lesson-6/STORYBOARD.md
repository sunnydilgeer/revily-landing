# Physics Lesson 6 storyboard — Power

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-6`, id `P-ENE-006-P`, skill `P-POWER`, spec 6.1.1.4. It owns power as the rate of energy transfer (and of doing work), the watt, P = E ÷ t and P = W ÷ t, the seconds/kJ unit check, E = P × t rearranged once, and powerful machines. Power is deliberately taught before the specific heat capacity practical, which uses energy = power × time. P = VI belongs to a later electricity lesson and is not used here.

Big idea: power is how fast energy is transferred. One watt is one joule every second, so the energy equals power times time.

Flow note: meaning first (how fast, not how much), then the unit, then the calculation with the units checked before dividing, then the equation turned round once. Calculation flow: worked example → near-identical guided item → a new item with a unit conversion → independent calculations in "On your own".

Sections:
1. Start here (P6-01): two motors lift identical boxes; one is quicker.
2. What is power? (P6-02–04): how fast, not how much → watts → work done means the same → a powerful machine. Checks: the unit; what 40 W means each second.
3. How do you work out power? (P6-05–08): equation in words → in symbols → work version → get the units right. Worked: pump 4500 J in 30 s = 150 W. Guided: 8000 J in 40 s; 12 000 J in 2 minutes.
4. How do you find the energy? (P6-09–11): find the energy → multiply both sides by t → same units. Worked: 250 W for 40 s = 10 000 J. Guided: 60 W for 50 s.
5. On your own (P6-12–16): kJ and minutes to power; spot the minutes mistake; E = P × t; compare three motors from a table; a written explanation.

Out of scope: P = VI and electrical power (a later lesson); horsepower; efficiency (next lessons); rearranging for t.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 171 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.1.4. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original; the page's motor, stuntperson and racing-car examples are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's worked example uses lifting work; here the pump, winch and heater are original numbers, with energy and work treated as the same quantity in joules as the page says.
- The unit symbol W (watts) and the symbol W (work done) share a letter; the frame warns about it once. Teachers may prefer to leave P = W ÷ t as a light mention.
- The kilojoule to joule conversion is included because the page's practice question gives energy in kJ.
