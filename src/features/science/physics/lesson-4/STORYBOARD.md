# Physics Lesson 4 storyboard — Gravitational and elastic potential energy

Chapter P1, Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-4`, id `P-ENE-004-P`, skill `P-POTENTIAL`, spec 6.1.1.2. It owns gravitational potential energy Ep = mgh (g = 9.8 N/kg), falling objects (energy lost from the g.p.e. store = energy gained in the kinetic store, no air resistance) and elastic potential energy Ee = ½ke² by substitution, with the limit of proportionality in one clause.

Big idea: lifting an object stores energy in its g.p.e. store, worked out with Ep = m × g × h. When it falls, that energy moves to the kinetic store. Stretching a spring stores energy in its elastic potential store, worked out with Ee = ½ × k × e².

Flow note: meaning and equation first, then a worked example, then guided practice, then the falling idea (it uses the conservation of energy from the previous lesson), then springs as a second, parallel equation with its own worked example. Calculation flow for g.p.e.: worked (5 kg, 3 m, 147 J) → guided (4 kg, 5 m, 196 J; 10 kg, 3 m, 294 J) → independent (20 kg, 2 m, 392 J). For springs: worked (k 200, e 0.1, 1 J) → guided (k 100, e 0.2, 2 J) → independent (k 300, e 0.2, 6 J). The written task combines both ideas (2 kg, 5 m, 98 J lost and 98 J gained).

Sections:
1. Start here (P4-01): a book lifted onto a shelf.
2. What is g.p.e.? (P4-02–04): raised objects store energy, mass and height and gravity, the equation in words, symbols and units with g = 9.8 N/kg. Checks: mass comparison; the value of g.
3. How do you work out g.p.e.? (P4-05–07): write it down, substitute, answer with joules. Two guided calculations.
4. What happens when it falls? (P4-08–10): falling transfers energy from the g.p.e. store to the kinetic store, lost equals gained, a numbers example. Checks: which store loses and gains; a diver losing 500 J.
5. What about springs? (P4-11–13): stretching and squashing, the equation and symbols, a worked example, the limit of proportionality. Checks: a calculation; what extension means.
6. On your own (P4-14–18): an independent g.p.e. calculation, the reverse falling question, a numbered three-springs diagram, an independent spring calculation, a written combined task.

Out of scope: calculating speed from height (too hard for Foundation; the page question on it was not reused); rearranging Ep = mgh for mass or height; force-extension graphs (a later topic); Higher-tier ideas.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 168 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.1.2. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The value g = 9.8 N/kg is given in every g.p.e. question and is not rearranged; the multiplication is shown one step at a time because 9.8 is not calculator-free.
- "Extension" is explained as "how much longer than its normal length".
- The limit of proportionality is one clause only: the equation works only if the spring has not been stretched too far.
- Air resistance is ignored in every falling question and the wording says so.
