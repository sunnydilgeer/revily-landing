# Physics Lesson 13 storyboard: Bio-fuels and fossil fuels

Topic P1 Energy. Folder `physics/lesson-13`, id `P-RES-013-P`, skill `P-FUELS`, prefix `biofuel-`. It owns bio-fuels, the reliability of fossil fuels and nuclear fuel, and their environmental problems (global warming, acid rain, habitats, radiation). Greenhouse gases and air pollution are taught in Chemistry and are recalled in one clause.

Big idea: bio-fuels are renewable but can cost forests; fossil fuels and nuclear fuel are reliable but run out and harm the environment in four ways.

Flow note: bio-fuels first because they finish the list of renewables from the earlier lessons. The middle section says why non-renewables are still used (reliable, stock, quick response), so that the last section, the problems, reads as the other side of the argument. The four problems get one frame each, then a side-by-side summary.

Sections:
1. Start here (P13-01): what else can power a car.
2. What are bio-fuels? (P13-02–04): what they are → pros → forest clearing → carbon dioxide in and out. Checks: what they are made from; a disadvantage.
3. Why are fossil fuels still used? (P13-05–07): reliable → stock → running out. Checks: why quick to respond; a benefit.
4. What problems do they cause? (P13-08–11): global warming → acid rain → habitats → nuclear → summary. Checks: carbon dioxide; sulfur dioxide; nuclear waste.
5. On your own (P13-12–16): bio-fuel versus coal; a still dark evening; bio-fuel not harmless; coal to nuclear; a written answer.

Out of scope: chemistry of combustion, named greenhouse gases beyond carbon dioxide, how a nuclear reactor works, radiation types (later topic), Higher-tier content.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 177 (scope only; page 179 is the topic test, quiz ideas only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.1.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's word "clean" for nuclear power is glossed as "does not release carbon dioxide".
- The carbon-dioxide-in, carbon-dioxide-out point for bio-fuels is kept to the page's wording; the lesson does not claim they are carbon neutral.
- The page says some fossil fuels "may run out within a hundred years": kept, with its "if no new ones are found".
