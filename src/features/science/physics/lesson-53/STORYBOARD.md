# Physics Lesson 53 storyboard — Transverse and longitudinal waves

Chapter P6, Waves (starts here). Folder `physics/lesson-53`, id `P-WAV-053-P`, skill `P-WAVETYPE`, spec 6.6.1.1 and 6.6.1.2. It owns waves transferring energy not matter (twig and guitar examples), transverse and longitudinal waves with examples, compressions and rarefactions, and the words used to describe waves: displacement, rest position, crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength, frequency (Hz) and period.

Big idea: a wave passes energy along while the particles only vibrate about their place. The direction of the vibration decides whether it is transverse or longitudinal, and five words let us describe any wave.

Flow note: energy not matter first (the idea everything else rests on), then the two types, then the vocabulary, built up step by step on one wave drawing. Period is defined here but its equation is the next lesson's.

Sections:
1. Start here (P53-01): the twig on the pond.
2. What does a wave carry? (P53-02–04): energy, not matter → particles stay put → twig and guitar examples. Checks: what a wave transfers; the guitar and the wind.
3. What are the two types of wave? (P53-05–07): transverse → its examples → longitudinal → compressions and rarefactions. Checks: which is transverse; the word for squashing.
4. How do we describe a wave? (P53-08–10): displacement → amplitude (crest, trough) → wavelength → frequency → period. Checks: a wave diagram with numbered arrows (assessment view); the unit of frequency.
5. On your own (P53-11–15): a longitudinal example, what 5 Hz means, comparing amplitudes in a diagram, period, and a written comparison of the two types.

Out of scope: wave speed and T = 1/f (next lesson); electromagnetic waves (later); refraction; reflection and absorption; wave graphs with time on the axis.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 221 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.1.1–6.6.1.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Water waves are given as transverse (as on the page, and as in the specification's list).
- Amplitude is drawn from the rest position, not trough to crest.

## Diagram specs
Look as in Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, gentle tints, hand-drawn feeling, text >= 12px, readable at 360px. Use `physicsPalette` (blue water, a single wave colour, an energy arrow in one accent). Particles are simple circles.

Section 2:
- `wavetype-energy`: a row of particles in a medium with a wave pulse passing; a big arrow "energy travels this way" and small vibration arrows on the particles labelled "particles vibrate".
- `wavetype-stay`: the same row at three moments (three thin strips one above the other), one particle marked in red staying in the same place while the pulse moves along; label "the particle stays put".
- `wavetype-examples`: two panels: a twig on rippled water (rings spreading, twig bobbing in place) captioned "ripples do not carry the twig away"; a guitar with sound arcs and a small "no wind" tag.

Section 3 (spring drawn as a coil):
- `wavetype-transverse`: a spring wiggled up and down: a sine-shaped coil with up-down arrows "vibrations go up and down" and a long arrow "wave travels this way", with a right-angle mark between them.
- `wavetype-transverse-eg`: three small icons in a row with captions: light (a lamp with a wavy ray) "light and all electromagnetic waves", ripples on water, a wave on a string.
- `wavetype-longitudinal`: a coil pushed along its length: the coil with a double arrow along it "vibrations in the same direction" and a long arrow "wave travels this way".
- `wavetype-compress`: the coil with dense sections marked "compressions" and stretched sections marked "rarefactions", and a small loudspeaker with "a sound wave is longitudinal".

Section 4 (one wave on labelled axes, vertical axis "Displacement", horizontal "Distance", with the rest position line; each frame adds one feature and fades the rest):
- `wavetype-displacement`: the wave with the rest position dashed and a vertical arrow from the line to a point on the wave, label "displacement".
- `wavetype-amplitude`: adds "crest" and "trough" pointers and a vertical arrow from rest position to a crest labelled "amplitude".
- `wavetype-wavelength`: a horizontal double arrow crest to next crest labelled "wavelength", with a second faded one trough to trough.
- `wavetype-frequency`: the wave passing a fixed point marker with a small tally "waves per second" and the text "1 Hz = 1 wave per second".
- `wavetype-period`: a small clock with one wave shaded, label "period: time for one wave".

Question visuals (assessment view):
- `wavetype-q-wave` (P53-09): a wave with four numbered double arrows or pointers and no names: 1 amplitude (rest line to crest), 2 wavelength (crest to next crest), 3 pointing to a trough, 4 pointing to the rest position line. Numbers only.
- `wavetype-q-compare` (P53-13): two waves on the same axes labelled "Wave A" (small amplitude, long wavelength) and "Wave B" (larger amplitude, shorter wavelength), no numbers of amplitude given. The wavelengths differ so students must pick amplitude, not wavelength.
