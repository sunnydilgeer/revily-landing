# Physics Lesson 30 storyboard — Specific latent heat

Chapter P3, Particle model of matter. Folder `physics/lesson-30`, id `P-PRT-030-P`, skill `P-LATENT-HEAT`, spec 6.3.2.3. It owns latent heat as the energy for a change of state, heating and cooling graphs with flat sections, specific latent heat of fusion and vaporisation, and E = mL (substitution; grams to kilograms shown once). It is kept separate from specific heat capacity, which is about temperature changes.

Big idea: during a change of state the energy transferred breaks (or forms) bonds between particles, so the temperature does not change. Specific latent heat is the energy to change the state of 1 kg. Energy = mass × specific latent heat.

Flow note: the idea first (why the temperature stays the same), then the graph, because the flat parts are the evidence for the idea, then the definition and the two names, then the calculation. Calculation flow: worked example (0.50 kg of ice) → near-identical guided item → guided item with a gram-to-kilogram conversion → two independent calculations on your own.

Sections:
1. Start here (P30-01): a kettle stuck at 100 °C.
2. What is latent heat? (P30-02–04): no temperature rise → where the energy goes → cooling releases energy → the term. Checks: temperature while boiling; where the melting energy goes.
3. What do the graphs show? (P30-05–07): heating graph → sloping parts → flat parts → cooling graph. Checks: what a flat section shows; a numbered heating graph.
4. What is specific latent heat? (P30-08–10): definition → not specific heat capacity → fusion → vaporisation. Checks: what it measures; the name for liquid and gas.
5. How do you calculate it? (P30-11–13): equation → substitute → answer with unit → g to kg. Checks: 0.20 kg boiled; 300 g melted.
6. On your own (P30-14–17): two independent E = mL calculations (one with grams), a numbered cooling graph, a written explanation.

Out of scope: combined calculations with temperature rises and latent heat in one problem; rearranging E = mL; the required practical on specific latent heat (not in the Foundation content shown); the exam question on the page was not reused.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Latent heat values are given in every question; none are memorised.
- The worked example uses a familiar value for ice (334 000 J/kg) chosen to give a tidy answer; the guided and independent items use invented, friendly values.
- Graphs are shown without numbers on the axes; only the pattern up, flat, up, flat, up is required.
