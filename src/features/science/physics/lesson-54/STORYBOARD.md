# Physics Lesson 54 storyboard — Frequency, period and wave speed

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-54`, id `P-WAV-054-P`, skill `P-WAVESPEED`, spec 6.6.1.2. It owns T = 1/f, wave speed v = fλ (substitution, and one rearrangement λ = v ÷ f with a friendly standard-form example) and the two-microphone oscilloscope method for the speed of sound.

Big idea: period and frequency are two ways of describing how fast a wave repeats (T = 1/f), and the speed of any wave is its frequency times its wavelength.

Flow note: the short T = 1/f calculation first, then v = fλ, then the one rearrangement with standard form, then the practical method that uses the equation. Calculation flow for each equation: worked example in the frames → near-identical guided item → independent item on your own. T: 4 Hz gives 0.25 s → 5 Hz → 20 Hz. v = fλ: 5 Hz and 0.4 m gives 2 m/s → 3 Hz and 0.5 m → 4 Hz and 1.5 m. λ = v ÷ f: standard form 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 6.0 × 10⁷ = 5 m → 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.5 × 10⁸ → 330 ÷ 110 (no standard form).

Sections:
1. Start here (P54-01): lightning and thunder.
2. How are period and frequency linked? (P54-02–03): T = 1/f → worked example → does it make sense. Check: 5 Hz.
3. How do you calculate wave speed? (P54-04–06): v = fλ → substitute → calculate. Checks: 3 Hz and 0.5 m; which equation.
4. How do you find a wavelength? (P54-07–08): rearrange → standard form in brackets → calculator. Check: 1.5 × 10⁸ Hz.
5. How can you measure the speed of sound? (P54-09–11): set up → line up → one wavelength apart → v = fλ. Checks: why move the microphone; the traces (assessment view).
6. On your own (P54-12–16): three independent calculations, the oscilloscope recall, and a written description of the method.

Out of scope: rearranging T = 1/f; the ripple tank and vibrating string (next lesson); the page's exam question is not reused; dividing standard form beyond the one worked example.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 222 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.1.2. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:

- The page's "wave speed" definition is given as how fast the wave moves or transfers energy.
- Independent questions avoid standard form so the unit stays on the equation.
