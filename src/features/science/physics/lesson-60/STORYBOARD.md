# Physics Lesson 60 storyboard — Investigating infrared emission

Chapter P6, Waves. Folder `physics/lesson-60`, id `P-WAV-060-P`, skill `P-WAV-060-P`, spec 6.6.2.2 (required practical: infrared emission). It owns the Leslie cube method, the variables, the boiling-water hazard, and the results pattern (matt black best emitter, shiny metal worst). Absorption is the next lesson.

Big idea: how much infrared radiation an object emits depends on its temperature and its surface, and a Leslie cube lets you compare surfaces at one temperature.

Flow note: what affects emission first (so the cube has a purpose), then the method in the order a student would work with safety placed at the filling step, then how to read the results. It prepares students for the practical and never claims to complete it.

Sections:
1. Start here (P60-01): two mugs at the same temperature, one matt black and one shiny.
2. What changes the infrared? (P60-02–04): temperature → surface → the Leslie cube. Checks: the two factors; what is special about the faces.
3. How do you run it? (P60-05–07): equipment → fill safely → measure each face → repeat. Checks: the safe action; why the distance is fixed.
4. What do the results show? (P60-08–10): highest reading → black beats white → matt beats shiny. Checks: best emitter; matt vs shiny.
5. On your own (P60-11–14): bar chart of readings; a numbered set-up; an unfair distance; a written method with safety.

Out of scope: the melting-wax absorption trick (next lesson); calculations; the hand-near-the-face idea on the page is not taught (touching the hot cube is unsafe).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 228 (scope only); AQA 8464 Physics 6.6.2.2 required practical. All wording, numbers, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Detector readings in the question chart are invented, unitless detector units.
- "Matt" is explained as "dull, not shiny" at first use.
