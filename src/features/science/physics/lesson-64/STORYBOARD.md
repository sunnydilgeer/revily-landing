# Physics Lesson 64 storyboard — Electromagnetism

Chapter P7, Magnetism and electromagnetism. Folder `physics/lesson-64`, id `P-MAG-064-P`, skill `P-MAG-064-P`, spec 6.7.2.1. It owns the field around a current-carrying wire (circles), the right-hand thumb rule, reversing the current, how distance and current change strength, the solenoid field (bar-magnet shape outside, strong and uniform inside) and the iron core electromagnet.

Big idea: an electric current makes a magnetic field, and coiling the wire and adding an iron core makes it much stronger and switchable.

Flow note: the single wire first (simplest picture), then what changes the strength, then the coil and the electromagnet as the payoff (the scrapyard crane in Start here). The compass link back to the previous lesson is in words only.

Sections:
1. Start here (P64-01): the scrapyard crane magnet.
2. How does a wire make a field? (P64-02–04): current makes a field → compass → thumb rule → reversing. Checks: field shape; the thumb rule.
3. What makes the field stronger? (P64-05–07): closer → larger current. Checks: where strongest; bigger current.
4. What is an electromagnet? (P64-08–10): solenoid → its field → why a coil is stronger → iron core. Checks: field inside; what an electromagnet is.
5. On your own (P64-11–14): a numbered wire diagram; making an electromagnet stronger; a numbered solenoid; a written description.

Out of scope: the motor effect, Fleming's left-hand rule, force on a wire, and uses of electromagnets in devices (not on these pages). Reversal of the current is taught and used in the written model answer but has no separate question.
