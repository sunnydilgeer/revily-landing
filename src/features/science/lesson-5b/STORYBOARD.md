# Lesson 5b storyboard — Stem cells

B-CELL-005B-B v0.1.0 · draftNeedsTeacherReview. Split from the old lesson 5; moved screens keep their B5- ids, new screens are B5-34 upward.

Big idea: a stem cell is an undifferentiated cell that can divide to make more stem cells or differentiate into specialised cells. Where it comes from decides how many types it can become, and any medical use must be weighed on evidence, risk and ethics.

Anchor / route: start from mitosis (the previous topic), which only makes more of the same cell. Ask what a stem cell is, then where stem cells are found (embryo → bone marrow → meristem), then what they are used for (plants first, then patients), then how to judge whether a treatment works.

Why this order:
- "Stem cell" needs "differentiate" (specialised cells) and "divide by mitosis" (previous topic), so both are recalled first.
- Sources come before uses, because every use depends on the range of cells a source can form.
- Plant cloning comes before medicine: it only needs meristems and is a simpler, working use.
- Benefits come before risks and ethics, and therapeutic cloning sits between them: it answers rejection but not viral infection.
- "Judging trial evidence" is taught as its own walkthrough (B5-35) before B5-23 and B5-29 use it (the old lesson tested it without teaching it).

Kept caveats: embryonic stem cells form "most" human cell types; adult cells cannot form every type; uses are "possible uses, not guaranteed cures", benefits need evidence; therapeutic cloning avoids rejection but other risks (viral infection) remain; ethical/religious objections to embryo use; trial numbers are made up, not real treatment evidence, and do not show safety. Out of scope: technical cloning method, named diseases beyond diabetes and paralysis. No calculation is practised. Diagrams are original schematics; the trial cards are illustrative.
