# Lesson 2 storyboard — Light and electron microscopes

Big idea: a microscope makes a bigger image of a specimen, but a bigger image is not the same as a clearer one. Magnification is how many times bigger; resolution is how much detail. Electron microscopes are better at both, which let scientists see and study sub-cellular structures.

Anchor and route: one tiny cell that is too small to see by eye (from the bacteria and cell-comparison lesson). Follow the light through a light microscope → ask how much bigger the image is (magnification) → ask how much detail it shows (resolution) → switch to electrons for finer detail → what that taught scientists.

Why this order: the parts of the light microscope come first, with no numbers, so "objective lens" and "eyepiece" exist before anyone multiplies them. "Image" and "magnification" are defined in B2-04 before the first total-magnification question (B2-03); the old version used magnification in B2-02 f3 and B2-03 before defining it. "Focusing makes it sharp, not bigger" (B2-02 f5) sets up the size-versus-detail idea before resolution. Resolution (B2-06) needs magnification already defined, because its key message is "a bigger blur is still a blur". The electron microscope is taught once (B2-08), after resolution, so "higher magnification and higher resolution" makes sense on first reading; comparison and history are frames of that one walkthrough, not separate sections.
