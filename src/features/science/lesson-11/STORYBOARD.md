# Lesson 11 storyboard — Circulatory system: the heart

Big idea: the heart is a double pump. Blood goes round two loops, one to the lungs and one to the body, and passes through the heart twice on every full trip.

Anchor and route: follow one red blood cell. It has just picked up oxygen in the lungs (Lesson 10). First see the whole trip as two loops. Then look at the parts that move it: chambers, the beat, valves. Finally, trace the cell through the heart name by name, ending as it leaves the left ventricle in the aorta, ready for Lesson 12.

Why this order:
- Two loops come first, so each detailed step has a place in the big picture ("this is the lung loop").
- Chambers come before the route, so the route frames only add one vessel name each.
- The beat (pacemaker) comes after chambers because it is the chamber walls that squeeze.
- Valves come after the beat: once learners know a chamber squeezes, "what stops blood going back?" is the obvious next question.
- The detailed route comes last. It uses everything above and ends in the aorta, which hands on to Lesson 12.
- Artery and vein are defined by direction just before the caveat that the pulmonary artery is still an artery.

Hand-offs:
- Back: B11-02 frame 1, "In Lesson 10, you saw blood pick up oxygen in the lungs", and the Start here question.
- Forward: B11-13 last frame, "Blood leaves the left ventricle in the aorta. Next, you will follow the vessels that carry it around the body." (Lesson 12.)
- Coronary arteries are taught in Lesson 14; this lesson gives them one clause only.

Wording rules: one new name per frame, plain meaning first, then "is called …". Sentences of 26 words or fewer. Valve names are not assessed. Out of scope: valve names, coronary artery detail (Lesson 14), conducting tissue beyond the natural pacemaker, blood pressure numbers.
