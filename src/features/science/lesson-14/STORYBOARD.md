# Lesson 14 storyboard — Cardiovascular disease and treatments

Big idea: when the heart's own blood supply or valves fail, doctors can fix the pipe, slow the problem, or replace the part.

1. Start here (B14-01): the heart muscle gets oxygen from its own arteries, not from the blood in its chambers.
2. The heart's own blood supply (B14-02–04): coronary arteries; layers of fatty material narrow them; less oxygen reaches the muscle — coronary heart disease.
3. Fix the pipe: stents (B14-05–07): cardiovascular disease and the three kinds of treatment (side-by-side diagram); a stent holds one artery open straight away; infection and clot risks.
4. Slow the problem: statins (B14-08–09): cholesterol (plain meaning first); statins lower it and slow build-up; slow, regular, possible side effects.
5. Replace the part: valves (B14-10–12): stiff vs leaky (link to Lesson 11); biological vs mechanical replacements. Leaky-valve question with labels hidden until submission.
6. Replace the whole heart (B14-13–14): heart failure; donor heart (or heart and lungs) and immune rejection; artificial heart and clot risk.
7. On your own (B14-15–18): choose a treatment for a patient who needs flow now (diagram without treatment names); compare a stent and statins; artificial heart while waiting for a donor; teacher-reviewed comparison of stents and statins.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first. Out of scope: heart attack, aerobic respiration detail, anticoagulant names.
