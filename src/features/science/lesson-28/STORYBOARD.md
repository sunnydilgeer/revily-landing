# Lesson 33 storyboard — Respiration: aerobic and anaerobic

Third lesson of chapter B4, Bioenergetics (folder `lesson-28`, id `B-BIO-028-B`). It follows the rate of photosynthesis.

Big idea: respiration is a reaction in every living cell that transfers energy from glucose. With enough oxygen it is aerobic and transfers lots of energy; without enough oxygen it is anaerobic, only partly breaks glucose down and transfers much less.

Flow note: the lesson starts from the sunflower's glucose (it was used "for respiration" when learners met photosynthesis), then answers "what is respiration, and what kinds are there?" in four steps.
1. **What respiration is.** "Not breathing" comes first, because it is the commonest mix-up. Then energy from glucose, exothermic (contrasted with endothermic photosynthesis), and the three uses of the energy.
2. **With oxygen.** Aerobic respiration is taught before anaerobic, because it is the normal case and the comparison needs it. The word equation is shown as photosynthesis the other way round; mitochondria link back to cell structure; the symbols card mirrors the photosynthesis summary card.
3. **Not enough oxygen, in muscles.** Hard exercise is the everyday reason; lactic acid; then why much less energy is transferred (incomplete oxidation), shown as two energy bars.
4. **Yeast and plants.** Ethanol and carbon dioxide, the name fermentation, then its two uses (bread, beer and wine), ending with one card comparing all three word equations.

1. Start here (B28-01): what respiration gave the sunflower (links to the photosynthesis lesson).
2. What is respiration? (B28-02–04): not breathing → energy from glucose → exothermic → what the energy is for. Checks: a true statement about respiration; what exothermic means.
3. With oxygen (B28-05–07): aerobic respiration → the word equation → mitochondria → chemical symbols (summary card). Checks: the products; a numbered cell (where most aerobic respiration happens).
4. Not enough oxygen (B28-08–10): short of oxygen (anaerobic respiration) → lactic acid → much less energy (oxidation). Checks: the muscle word equation; why less energy.
5. Yeast and plants (B28-11–13): plants and yeast → fermentation → bread and drinks → compare the three. Checks: why dough rises; the name of anaerobic respiration in yeast.
6. On your own (B28-14–17): a mushroom that does not breathe; numbered arrows on a cell; an original yeast-and-sugar bar chart read without over-claiming; a teacher-reviewed comparison of aerobic and anaerobic respiration in muscle.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; British spelling. Symbols are recognised, not balanced. Alcohol is mentioned only as the product in beer and wine, neutrally.

Out of scope: the balanced symbol equation; the effects of exercise, oxygen debt and metabolism (next lesson); lactic acid going to the liver (Higher only); ATP.

## States in full

### B28-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** A sunflower uses some of its glucose for respiration. What does respiration give the plant?
- 0 More chlorophyll · **1 Energy to live and grow ✓** · 2 Water from the soil
- Hint: You met this when you learned what plants do with glucose.
- Explanation: Glucose is made by photosynthesis. Respiration transfers energy from glucose, which the plant uses to live and grow.

### B28-02 · teach "What is respiration?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not breathing | Respiration is not the same as breathing. | breathing = air in and out; respiration = a reaction in cells | Breathing moves air in and out of your lungs. Respiration is different: it is a chemical reaction that happens inside cells. All living things respire, including plants and yeast, which do not breathe. | `energy-resp-breathing` |
| Energy from glucose | Respiration transfers energy from glucose. | glucose broken down → energy | In respiration, cells break down glucose and transfer energy from it. You met glucose as the sugar that plants make. Respiration goes on in every cell of your body, all the time. The energy is used for all the living processes a cell carries out. | `energy-resp-energy` |
| Exothermic | Respiration gives out energy to the surroundings. | exothermic = gives energy out | Respiration transfers energy to the environment. A reaction that does this is called exothermic. Photosynthesis is the opposite: it is endothermic, because it takes energy in. | `energy-resp-exothermic` |
| What the energy is for | Organisms use the energy in three main ways. | build, move, keep warm | Organisms use the energy from respiration to build larger molecules from smaller ones. Animals also use it to move about. Mammals and birds use it to keep warm. | `energy-resp-uses` |

### B28-03 · choice
**Q:** Which sentence about respiration is true?
- 0 It only happens when you breathe in · **1 It happens in every living cell, all the time ✓** · 2 Only animals respire · 3 It is another word for breathing
- Hint: Where does respiration happen?
- Explanation: Breathing moves air; respiration is a chemical reaction in cells. It happens in every living cell, all the time, in plants, animals and yeast.

### B28-04 · choice
**Q:** Respiration is exothermic. What does this mean?
- 0 It takes in energy from the environment · 1 It needs light to happen · **2 It transfers energy to the environment ✓**
- Hint: Exothermic is the opposite of endothermic.
- Explanation: Endothermic reactions, such as photosynthesis, take energy in. Exothermic reactions, such as respiration, transfer energy to the environment.

### B28-05 · teach "With oxygen"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aerobic respiration | Respiration that uses oxygen is aerobic. | aerobic = with oxygen | Most of the time, your cells use oxygen to break down glucose. Respiration that uses oxygen is called aerobic respiration. It is the most efficient way to transfer energy from glucose. | `energy-aerobic-oxygen` |
| The word equation | Glucose and oxygen react to make carbon dioxide and water. | in: glucose + oxygen; out: CO₂ + water | The word equation is glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Glucose and oxygen go in, and carbon dioxide and water are made. It uses the same substances as photosynthesis, but the other way round. | `energy-aerobic-equation` |
| Mitochondria | Most aerobic respiration happens in mitochondria. | mitochondria = where it happens | Aerobic respiration goes on all the time in plants and animals. Most of its reactions happen inside mitochondria. You met mitochondria when you learned about the parts of a cell. | `energy-aerobic-where` |
| Chemical symbols | Each substance has a chemical symbol. | C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O | You met these symbols when you learned about photosynthesis. Glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆ and oxygen is O₂. Carbon dioxide is CO₂ and water is H₂O. | `energy-aerobic-summary` |

### B28-08 · teach "Not enough oxygen"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short of oxygen | In hard exercise, muscles may not get enough oxygen. | not enough oxygen → respire without it too | During hard exercise, your body sometimes cannot get enough oxygen to your muscles. Then the muscle cells also respire without oxygen, as well as with it. Respiration without oxygen is called anaerobic respiration. You will see what this does during exercise in the next lesson. | `energy-anaerobic-short` |
| Lactic acid | In muscles, glucose is turned into lactic acid. | glucose → lactic acid | In muscle cells, anaerobic respiration turns glucose into lactic acid. The word equation is glucose → lactic acid. The glucose is not broken down completely. | `energy-anaerobic-lactic` |
| Much less energy | Anaerobic respiration transfers much less energy. | no oxygen → incomplete → less energy | In anaerobic respiration, glucose is not combined with oxygen. Combining with oxygen is called oxidation, so the oxidation of glucose is incomplete. This means anaerobic respiration transfers much less energy than aerobic respiration. | `energy-anaerobic-less` |

### B28-09 · choice
**Q:** What is the word equation for anaerobic respiration in muscle cells?
- 0 glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water · **1 glucose → lactic acid ✓** · 2 glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide · 3 lactic acid → glucose
- Hint: Anaerobic means without oxygen. Which is made in muscles?
- Explanation: The first equation uses oxygen, so it is aerobic. Ethanol is made in plants and yeast. In muscle cells, anaerobic respiration is glucose → lactic acid.

### B28-10 · choice
**Q:** Why does anaerobic respiration transfer much less energy than aerobic respiration?
- **0 The glucose is not combined with oxygen, so it is not broken down completely ✓** · 1 It makes more carbon dioxide · 2 It only happens in plants
- Hint: What is missing in anaerobic respiration?
- Explanation: Without oxygen, the oxidation of glucose is incomplete. The glucose is only partly broken down, so much less energy is transferred.

### B28-11 · teach "Yeast and plants"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plants and yeast | Plants and yeast make ethanol and carbon dioxide. | glucose → ethanol + CO₂ | Plant cells and yeast cells can also respire without oxygen. Yeast is a fungus made of single cells. In these cells, the word equation is glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide. Ethanol is a type of alcohol. | `energy-yeast-cells` |
| Fermentation | Anaerobic respiration in yeast is called fermentation. | fermentation = yeast respiring without oxygen | When yeast respires without oxygen, the process has its own name. It is called fermentation. The food and drinks industry uses fermentation a lot. | `energy-yeast-ferment` |
| Bread and drinks | Carbon dioxide makes bread rise; ethanol is the alcohol in drinks. | CO₂ → bread rises; ethanol → beer and wine | In bread making, yeast ferments sugar in the dough. The carbon dioxide gas makes bubbles, so the dough rises. In making beer and wine, fermentation makes the ethanol, which is the alcohol in the drink. | `energy-yeast-uses` |
| Compare the three | There are three word equations to know. | with oxygen; muscles; plants and yeast | Aerobic respiration is glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. In muscles, anaerobic respiration is glucose → lactic acid. In plants and yeast, it is glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide. Only aerobic respiration uses oxygen. | `energy-yeast-compare` |

### B28-12 · choice
**Q:** A baker mixes yeast into bread dough. Why does the dough rise?
- 0 Yeast makes oxygen · 1 Yeast makes lactic acid · **2 Yeast makes carbon dioxide gas, which forms bubbles ✓**
- Hint: Which gas does fermentation make?
- Explanation: Yeast ferments the sugar in the dough: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide. The carbon dioxide gas forms bubbles, so the dough rises.

### B28-13 · choice
**Q:** Yeast is respiring in a jar of sugary liquid with no oxygen. What is this process called?
- 0 Photosynthesis · 1 Aerobic respiration · **2 Fermentation ✓** · 3 Breathing
- Hint: Anaerobic respiration in yeast has its own name.
- Explanation: There is no oxygen, so the yeast respires anaerobically. Anaerobic respiration in yeast is called fermentation.

### B28-14 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** A mushroom has no lungs and does not breathe. Which statement is true?
- 0 It cannot respire, because it does not breathe · **1 It still respires, because all living things respire ✓** · 2 It gets its energy from breathing through its skin
- Hint: Is respiration the same as breathing?
- Explanation: Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells, not breathing. All living things respire, so the mushroom respires too.

### B28-17 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration in a muscle cell.
- Hint: For each type, say whether it uses oxygen, what it makes, how much energy it transfers and when it happens.
- Model answer: Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and makes carbon dioxide and water. Anaerobic respiration does not use oxygen and makes lactic acid. Aerobic respiration transfers much more energy, because in anaerobic respiration glucose is only partly broken down. Muscles respire anaerobically as well when they cannot get enough oxygen, such as during hard exercise.
- Marking points: Aerobic respiration uses oxygen; anaerobic respiration does not. · Aerobic respiration makes carbon dioxide and water. · Anaerobic respiration in muscles makes lactic acid. · Aerobic respiration transfers much more energy than anaerobic respiration. · Anaerobic respiration happens when muscles cannot get enough oxygen, for example in hard exercise.
- Common errors: Saying anaerobic respiration in muscles makes ethanol or carbon dioxide. · Saying respiration is breathing, or that anaerobic means not breathing. · Saying anaerobic respiration transfers more energy.
