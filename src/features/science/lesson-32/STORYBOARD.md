# Lesson 37 storyboard — Reaction time practical (folder lesson-32, v1)

Third lesson of chapter B5, Homeostasis and response. It prepares learners for the reaction-time required practical; they carry out the practical itself in class.

Big idea: reaction time is how long it takes to respond to a stimulus; a ruler drop measures it as a distance, and a fair test of caffeine keeps the person, hand and drop height the same and compares mean distances before and after.

Flow note: the lesson follows one ruler-drop investigation from start to finish.
1. **What is reaction time?** A timeline from stimulus to response, milliseconds, factors (age, gender, drugs) and caffeine.
2. **The ruler drop.** One side-view scene: set up, zero at the top of the thumb, drop without warning, read the ruler, higher number = slower.
3. **Make it fair.** The plan (drops, caffeinated drink, 10 minutes, drops again, with the safety line), control variables, and the three to keep the same.
4. **Find the mean.** Why repeat, what the mean is, and comparing two means; then a worked example (12, 15, 11, 14, 13 cm → 13 cm) and near-identical practice with new numbers (→ 11 cm). The independent calculation (B32-14) uses four times in ms.

Why this order: learners need to know what is being measured before they learn how. The method comes before fairness because the control variables are parts of the method. Means come last because they are how the repeated results are compared. The lesson starts from motor neurones, met in the nervous system lesson, because the catch is a response carried out by hand muscles.

1. Start here (B32-01): which neurones carry impulses to the hand muscles (links to the nervous system).
2. What is reaction time? (B32-02–04): reaction time → milliseconds → factors → caffeine. Checks: why milliseconds; the fastest of four times.
3. The ruler drop (B32-05–07): set up → line up the zero → drop and catch → read the ruler → higher means slower. Checks: why no warning; who reacted faster, 18 cm or 9 cm.
4. Make it fair (B32-08–10): testing caffeine (with safety) → control variables → same person, hand and height. Checks: which is a control variable; why the same person.
5. Find the mean (B32-11–13): results vary → the mean → compare the means; worked example; guided practice.
6. On your own (B32-14–17): mean of four computer-test times (independent calculation); an invented before/after caffeine chart read without over-claiming; spot the problem when the catching hand changes; teacher-reviewed written method.

Safety and honesty: the caffeine test is only done with a teacher in charge; no one is pushed into having caffeine, and anyone who should avoid it can help in other ways. The lesson says the practical happens in class and does not claim the online lesson completes it.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; British spelling; units written as cm and ms.

Out of scope: converting a ruler distance into a time (not on the page), anomalous results and range, graph plotting, the effect of other drugs, and computer reaction tests beyond one practice question.

## States in full

### B32-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** Which neurones carry impulses from the CNS to the muscles in your hand?
- 0 Sensory neurones · **1 Motor neurones ✓** · 2 Relay neurones
- Hint: Which neurones carry impulses away from the CNS?
- Explanation: Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS, and relay neurones link neurones inside it. Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors, such as the muscles in your hand.

There is a short delay between the ruler starting to fall and your fingers closing. The time it takes to respond to a stimulus is called your reaction time. | `nerve-rt-time` |

### B32-05 · teach "The ruler drop"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set up | Rest your forearm on the edge of a table. | arm still, hand over the edge | The person being tested sits with their forearm resting on a table. Their hand sticks out over the edge. This keeps their arm still for every catch. | `nerve-drop-setup` |
| Line up the zero | The zero mark is level with the top of the thumb. | zero at the top of the thumb | A partner holds a ruler upright, hanging between the person’s thumb and first finger. The zero mark is level with the top of the thumb. The person must not touch or grip the ruler yet. | `nerve-drop-zero` |
| Drop and catch | The ruler is dropped without warning. | no warning → no guessing | The partner lets go without any warning. The person catches the ruler as quickly as they can. With no warning, they cannot guess when it will fall. | `nerve-drop-fall` |
| Read the ruler | Read the number at the top of the thumb. | reading = distance the ruler fell | Read the number on the ruler that is level with the top of the thumb. This shows how far the ruler fell before it was caught. Here it fell 14 cm. | `nerve-drop-read` |
| Higher means slower | A higher reading means a slower reaction. | further fall → slower | The longer someone takes to react, the further the ruler falls. So the higher the number, the slower the reaction time. | `nerve-drop-compare` |

### B32-06 · choice
**Q:** Why is the ruler dropped without any warning?
- 0 So the ruler falls faster · **1 So the person cannot guess when it will fall ✓** · 2 So the reading is always zero · 3 So the person can use both hands
- Hint: What could the person do if they knew when it was coming?
- Explanation: If the person knew when the ruler would drop, they could get ready early. With no warning, they cannot guess, so the catch shows their reaction time.

### B32-07 · choice
**Q:** Sam catches the ruler at 18 cm. Priya catches it at 9 cm. What does this show?
- 0 Sam reacted faster · 1 They reacted equally fast · **2 Priya reacted faster ✓**
- Hint: Does a higher number mean faster or slower?
- Explanation: The ruler fell further before Sam caught it. A lower number means a faster reaction, so Priya reacted faster.

### B32-08 · teach "Make it fair"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Testing caffeine | Compare catches before and after caffeine. | drops → drink → wait 10 minutes → drops | First, do several ruler drops and record each one. Then the person has a drink with caffeine, such as cola, and waits 10 minutes. Then repeat the ruler drops. Only do this with a teacher in charge, and no one should be pushed into having caffeine. | `nerve-fair-plan` |
| Control variables | Keep everything else the same. | change one thing only | For a fair test, only the caffeine should change. Everything else that could affect the result must stay the same. These are called control variables. | `nerve-fair-variables` |
| Same person, hand and height | Three things to keep the same. | same person, same hand, same height | Use the same person for every catch. They should always catch with the same hand. Drop the ruler from the same height each time. | `nerve-fair-controls` |

### B32-09 · choice
**Q:** Which of these is a control variable in the caffeine test?
- 0 Whether the person has had caffeine · 1 The distance the ruler falls · **2 The hand used to catch the ruler ✓**
- Hint: A control variable is kept the same.
- Explanation: Caffeine is what is changed, and the distance the ruler falls is what is measured. The hand used is kept the same, so it is a control variable.

### B32-10 · choice
**Q:** Why must the same person catch the ruler before and after the drink?
- **0 Different people can have different reaction times ✓** · 1 The ruler only works for one person · 2 It makes the ruler fall further
- Hint: Is everyone’s reaction time the same?
- Explanation: Reaction times differ from person to person. Using the same person means only the caffeine changes.

### B32-11 · teach "Find the mean"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Results vary | Each catch gives a slightly different reading. | one catch could be unusual | No two catches are exactly the same. One catch on its own could be unusually fast or slow. So you repeat the test several times. | `nerve-mean-vary` |
| The mean | The mean gives one typical value. | mean = total ÷ how many | To turn repeated results into one value, you find the mean. Add up all the results, then divide by how many results there are. | `nerve-mean-idea` |
| Compare the means | Compare the mean before and after caffeine. | smaller mean distance → faster | Work out one mean before the drink and one mean after it. If the mean distance is smaller after the drink, the person reacted faster. | `nerve-mean-compare` |

### B32-12 · worked example "Find a mean distance" · diagram `nerve-mean-worked`
Before the drink, one person caught the ruler five times. The distances were 12 cm, 15 cm, 11 cm, 14 cm and 13 cm. What is the mean distance?
1. Add up the results: 12 + 15 + 11 + 14 + 13 = 65 cm.
2. Count the results: there are 5.
3. Divide the total by the number of results: 65 ÷ 5 = 13.
4. Add the unit: the mean is 13 cm.

### B32-13 · choice · `calculation`
**Q:** After the drink, the same person caught the ruler five times: 10 cm, 12 cm, 9 cm, 11 cm and 13 cm. What is the mean distance?
- 0 55 cm · 1 13 cm · 2 10 cm · **3 11 cm ✓**
- Hint: Add them up, then divide by 5.
- Explanation: Add them up: 10 + 12 + 9 + 11 + 13 = 55 cm. Divide by 5: 55 ÷ 5 = 11 cm.

### B32-14 · choice · `calculation`, independent
**Q:** A student did a computer reaction test four times. Their times were 260 ms, 240 ms, 250 ms and 270 ms. What is their mean reaction time?
- 0 1020 ms · 1 250 ms · **2 255 ms ✓** · 3 270 ms
- Hint: How many results are there this time?
- Explanation: Add them up: 260 + 240 + 250 + 270 = 1020 ms. There are 4 results, so 1020 ÷ 4 = 255 ms.

### B32-15 · choice · `dataInterpretation`, independent · question diagram `nerve-caffeine-data` (assessment version hides the answer)
**Q:** The chart shows one person’s mean results. Which conclusion fits?
- 0 Caffeine speeds up everyone’s reaction time · **1 In this test, their mean distance was smaller after caffeine, so they reacted faster ✓** · 2 They reacted more slowly after caffeine · 3 Caffeine made the ruler fall faster
- Hint: Compare the two bars, and remember this is one person.
- Explanation: The mean fell from 13 cm to 11 cm after caffeine; one person cannot show what happens to everyone. So in this test, their mean distance was smaller after caffeine, so they reacted faster.

### B32-16 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** Halfway through the test, Jo switches from catching with her right hand to her left hand. Why is this a problem?
- **0 The hand is a control variable, so the test is no longer fair ✓** · 1 The ruler falls more slowly · 2 It means she has had more caffeine · 3 The ruler cannot be read
- Hint: What should stay the same in a fair test?
- Explanation: Jo may react at a different speed with each hand. The hand is a control variable, so changing it means the test is no longer fair.

### B32-17 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Describe how you would use a ruler drop to find out whether caffeine affects a person’s reaction time. Include how you would make it a fair test.
- Hint: Go in order: set up, drop, read, repeat, drink, wait, repeat. Then say what stays the same.
- Model answer: The person rests their forearm on a table with their hand over the edge. Hold a ruler with the zero level with the top of their thumb, then drop it without warning. They catch it, and you read the number at the top of their thumb. Repeat several times and find the mean. Then they have a drink with caffeine, wait 10 minutes and repeat. Use the same person, the same hand and the same drop height each time.
- Marking points: The ruler is held with zero level with the top of the thumb and dropped without warning. · The distance is read at the top of the thumb when the ruler is caught. · The test is repeated several times and the mean distance is calculated. · The test is repeated after a caffeinated drink and a 10-minute wait, and the means are compared. · Control variables are kept the same: the same person, the same hand and the same drop height.
- Common errors: Saying a higher number means a faster reaction. · Using a different person after the drink. · Giving a warning before the ruler is dropped.
