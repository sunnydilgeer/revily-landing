# Chemistry Lesson 48 storyboard — Air pollution

Chapter C9, Chemistry of the atmosphere. Folder `chemistry/lesson-48`, id `C-ATM-048-C`, skill `C-AIR-POLLUTION`. It owns complete versus incomplete combustion products, carbon monoxide, particulates and global dimming, sulfur dioxide, oxides of nitrogen and acid rain. Complete combustion itself is taught in the alkanes lesson and is linked in one clause.

Big idea: when fuels burn with plenty of oxygen they release carbon dioxide and water vapour. With too little oxygen they also release carbon monoxide, soot particles and unburnt fuel. Sulfur impurities in fuels give sulfur dioxide, and the heat makes nitrogen and oxygen from the air form oxides of nitrogen. These pollutants harm health, the environment and buildings.

Flow note: combustion first (what is released and why), then the two pollutants of incomplete combustion and their harm, then the two acid rain gases and where each comes from, so every pollutant is tied to its cause before its effect. Tone is factual and calm.

Sections:
1. Start here (C48-01): a heater with a nearly blocked air supply.
2. What is released when fuels burn? (C48-02–04): fuels contain hydrocarbons → two kinds of burning → products of both → extra products → side by side. Checks: products of complete combustion; complete versus incomplete.
3. Why are CO and particulates harmful? (C48-05–07): CO stops blood carrying oxygen → effects → hard to detect → particulates in the lungs → global dimming. Checks: why CO is dangerous; what particulates are.
4. Where do acid rain gases come from? (C48-08–10): other pollutants → sulfur dioxide → nitrogen oxides → acid rain → effects. Checks: source of SO₂; source of nitrogen oxides.
5. On your own (C48-11–15): which product needs limited oxygen; naming global dimming; two tiles read for soot; what happens to the gases in clouds; a written explanation of acid rain and two effects.

Out of scope: balanced equations for incomplete combustion; catalytic converters; how to remove sulfur; the fuel sulfur indicator test noted on the page; percentages of pollutants; Higher-tier content.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 156 (scope only; the p157 revision test is used for quiz ideas only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.9.3.1 and 5.9.3.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Carbon monoxide is described only as stopping the blood carrying enough oxygen, as the page does.
- The carbon monoxide alarm is mentioned in one sentence as everyday context, not as a spec point.
- Effects are described in the page's terms (respiratory problems, damage to buildings), without health statistics.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Course colours: purple = carbon dioxide, blue = water, teal = oxygen, yellow = Sun/energy, green = plants. Soot and particulates are dark grey specks; carbon monoxide is a pale grey-lilac puff, distinct from purple CO₂. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px.

- `pollute-fuels`: a rounded drum of crude oil and a lump of coal, each with a small hydrocarbon chain of beads, an arrow to a flame with a teal oxygen puff feeding in. Label "hydrocarbons burn in oxygen".
- `pollute-two-types`: two panels side by side. Left "Complete combustion": a bright flame with plenty of teal oxygen arrows, "plenty of oxygen: all the fuel burns". Right "Incomplete combustion": a smaller sooty flame with one teal arrow, "not enough oxygen: some fuel does not burn".
- `pollute-both`: a flame with two arrows out: a purple puff "carbon dioxide" and a blue puff "water vapour", labelled "both types".
- `pollute-incomplete`: the sooty flame with the two shared products faded, and three highlighted outputs: dark specks "soot particles (particulates)", a small fuel drop "unburnt fuel", a lilac puff "carbon monoxide, CO".
