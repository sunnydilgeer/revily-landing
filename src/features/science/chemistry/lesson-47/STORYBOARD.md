# Chemistry Lesson 47 storyboard — Carbon footprints

Chapter C9, Chemistry of the atmosphere. Folder `chemistry/lesson-47`, id `C-ATM-047-C`, skill `C-CARBON-FOOTPRINT`. It follows greenhouse gases and climate change and owns the meaning of a carbon footprint, ways to reduce one, and why reducing emissions is still difficult. Life cycles are only named in one clause; life cycle assessment has its own later lesson.

Big idea: a carbon footprint is a measure of the carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases released over the full life of a service, event or product. It is hard to measure exactly, but a rough calculation shows what releases the most. Emissions can be cut by technology, rules and cleaner energy, but cost, agreement and lifestyle make it hard.

Flow note: meaning first (nothing else makes sense without it), then the ways to reduce (the positive, concrete part), then the difficulties last, so students finish with a balanced picture. The tone is calm and factual.

Sections:
1. Start here (C47-01): two families, one burns more fuel.
2. What is a carbon footprint? (C47-02–04): meaning → service, event, product → tricky to measure → rough answer still helps. Checks: what a footprint measures; why a rough calculation is useful.
3. How can footprints be reduced? (C47-05–07): aim → capture and store → less energy and waste → tax, cap and licences → renewable and nuclear. Checks: capture and storage; what a cap is.
4. Why is it still difficult? (C47-08–10): not simple → new technology → economies and well-being → lifestyles. Checks: government worries; individuals' difficulty.
5. On your own (C47-11–15): things that have footprints; less waste means less methane; a chart of three trips read for the largest; which change reduces a footprint; a written answer (two ways, one difficulty).

Out of scope: the greenhouse effect and evidence for climate change (previous lesson); how a footprint is calculated in detail; life cycle stages; carbon offsetting; politics beyond the page; Higher-tier content.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 155 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.9.2.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Climate change is presented as many scientists' view of human causes, in the page's wording, without argument.
- "Carbon capture and storage" is described only as catch and store underground; no method detail.
- The chart in C47-13 uses invented numbers for three unnamed school trips.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Course colours: purple = carbon dioxide, yellow = Sun/energy, green = plants. Footprints are drawn as a friendly pair of soft footprints with purple puffs. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px.

- `footprint-meaning`: a soft footprint in the centre with purple puffs (CO₂) and a couple of smaller puffs labelled "methane"; a curved arrow around it from "made" to "used" to "thrown away", labelled "full life". Caption: "carbon footprint = greenhouse gases released".
- `footprint-things`: three rounded tiles in a row, each with a small footprint and a simple picture: a school bus ("service"), a stadium or flag ("event"), a toaster ("product").
- `footprint-tricky`: a toaster in the middle with a winding path around it: make, transport, shop, use, throw away, each with a small purple puff and a question mark on some; label "too many steps to count exactly".
- `footprint-rough`: a simple sketch of three items with different sized puffs (large, medium, small); the largest is circled and labelled "biggest: avoid it". Label "a rough calculation is enough".
- `footprint-reduce-aim`: a large footprint shrinking into a smaller footprint, with a downward arrow; label "release less".
- `footprint-capture`: a chimney with purple puffs going into a pipe/box marked "capture", then a pipe down into rock layers underground labelled "stored deep underground". No puffs reach the sky.
- `footprint-less`: two side-by-side simple sketches: a tap-and-bulb "less energy" and a small bin "less waste" with a tiny methane puff, each with a green tick.
- `footprint-rules`: two rounded panels: "tax" (coin with a purple puff, arrow to smaller puff) and "cap and licences" (a ceiling line labelled "cap" over a row of factory shapes, one licence card being passed on).
- `footprint-clean`: a wind turbine, a solar panel (Sun in yellow) and a small nuclear power plant with cooling tower on one side; a crossed-out coal chimney on the other. Label "renewable or nuclear instead of fossil fuels".
- `footprint-hard-intro`: a footprint with a hill or steps in front of it and a small figure climbing; label "not simple".
- `footprint-hard-tech`: a laboratory bench with a half-built machine and a "still being developed" label.
- `footprint-hard-economy`: a balance or scale with a small town/factory tile on one side and a purple puff on the other; labels "economies" and "well-being". Optionally two flags with a dotted line between them and a question mark, labelled "hard to agree".
- `footprint-hard-lifestyle`: three small people: one thinking "why?", one shrugging, one holding a bicycle; label "changing lifestyles".
- `footprint-q-trips` (question, assessment view): a simple bar chart of three trips, x labels "Trip 1", "Trip 2", "Trip 3", y axis "Greenhouse gas released in one year (kg)" with ticks; bars 40, 120, 75. No highlighting or "biggest" wording, and no caption. Neutral accessible description: "Bar chart of greenhouse gas released by three trips."
