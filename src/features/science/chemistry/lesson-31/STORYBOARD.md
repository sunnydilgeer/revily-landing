# Chemistry Lesson 31 storyboard — Rates of reaction and collision theory

Chapter C6, Rate and extent of chemical change. Folder `chemistry/lesson-31`, id `C-RAT-031-C`, skill `C-RATE-COLLISION`.

Big idea: the rate of a reaction is how fast reactants become products. A graph of product against time is steep at the start and flattens as reactants are used up. Collision theory explains rates: particles must collide with at least the activation energy, and more successful collisions per second means a faster reaction.

Flow note: meaning and the single-curve shape come first because everything else reads graphs. Comparing lines comes second, while the graph is fresh, and ends on "more product needs more reactants" (a common slip). Collision theory comes last because it explains why lines differ; the next lesson uses it for each factor.

Sections:
1. Start here (C31-01): rusting nail against a firework.
2. What rate means (C31-02–04): fast/slow → product-time graph → steeper = faster → levelling off.
3. Comparing graph lines (C31-05–07): original → faster → slower → same height → more product.
4. Collision theory (C31-08–10): collide → enough energy → activation energy → more successful collisions, faster.
5. On your own (C31-11–15): three lines, four lines, too little energy, half the collisions, written explanation.

Out of scope: the four factors and catalysts (next lesson); measuring rate and calculating mean rate (later lessons); tangents (Higher).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 136 (scope only). AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.6.1.1, 5.6.1.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

- Activation energy is defined as the minimum energy for a successful collision; no profile is drawn here.

## Diagram specs
Palette: `atomPalette`. One axes drawing (Amount of product formed up, Time along, no numbers) reused in the graph frames. Curves are soft, hand-drawn-feeling. Reactant particles blue, product particles coral.
- `rates-meaning`: two small scenes side by side: a rusty nail (labelled "Slow: weeks") and a firework burst ("Fast: a second"). Label "rate = how fast reactants change into products".
- `rates-curve`: empty axes with the single rising curve (steep then flat). Axes labelled "Amount of product formed" and "Time".
- `rates-steep`: same curve; the steep start highlighted (amber band) labelled "Steep: fast reaction"; rest faded.
- `rates-flat`: same curve; flat end highlighted labelled "Flat: reaction finished"; a short note "reactants used up".
- `rates-lines-original`: axes with line 1 only, labelled "1 original reaction".
- `rates-lines-faster`: line 1 faded, add line 2 (steeper, flat sooner, same height), labelled "2 faster".
- `rates-lines-slower`: lines 1, 2 faded, add line 3 (less steep, flat later, same height), labelled "3 slower".
- `rates-lines-same`: lines 1, 2, 3 with a dashed horizontal at the shared final height, labelled "same amount of product".
- `rates-lines-more`: lines 1 to 3 faded, add line 4 finishing higher, labelled "4 more product made", with note "needs more reactants".
- `rates-collide`: a box of moving particles; two particles about to collide with motion trails, labelled "collide".
- `rates-energy`: two collisions: a gentle one (particles bounce apart, label "too little energy: no reaction") and a hard one (particles change colour into products, "enough energy: reaction").
- `rates-activation`: a simple energy bar/hurdle (a small hill drawn as a soft mound) with a particle needing to get over it; label "activation energy = minimum energy to react". Not a full profile.
- `rates-frequency`: two boxes, "fewer collisions per second" and "more collisions per second" with the second marked "faster reaction"; small "2 × collisions = 2 × rate".

Question visuals (assessment view, no answer words):
- `rates-q-three`: axes with three lines numbered 1, 2, 3 (1 steepest, flat first; all end at the same height or 3 slightly different is fine). Answer: 1 fastest. Numbers only.
- `rates-q-four`: four lines numbered 1 to 4. Lines 1 and 3 must end at the same height; 2 lower; 4 higher. Numbers only, no "more/less" text.
