# Chemistry Lesson 40 storyboard — Cracking

Chapter C7, Organic chemistry. Folder `chemistry/lesson-40`, id `C-ORG-040-C`, skill `C-CRACKING`. It follows fractional distillation and owns cracking, alkenes as a product, the bromine water test and cracking equations. Catalysts are recalled from the rates lessons, not re-taught.

Big idea: there is more demand for short-chain hydrocarbons than crude oil supplies, so long chains are cracked (broken down by heat) into smaller alkanes and alkenes. Alkenes are more reactive and are starting materials for polymers. Bromine water tells them apart from alkanes. In a cracking equation, carbons and hydrogens balance on each side.

Flow note: why first (demand, splitting, two kinds of product), then how (steam and catalytic), then the alkene test, then the equation, because the equation ends in an alkene and students need the word first. The equation is built up frame by frame with one worked example, then near-identical practice with new numbers, then an independent calculation on your own.

Sections:
1. Start here (C40-01): a refinery with too much heavy oil and not enough petrol.
2. Why crack hydrocarbons? (C40-02–04): too many long chains → cracking → alkane plus alkene → why alkenes matter. Checks: what cracking is; why alkenes are useful.
3. How is cracking done? (C40-05–07): thermal decomposition → steam cracking → catalytic cracking → side by side. Checks: which method uses steam; the catalyst.
4. How do you test for an alkene? (C40-08–10): bromine water set-up → alkane stays orange → alkene goes colourless. Checks: a result that stays orange; what an alkene does.
5. Can you complete the equation? (C40-11–12): worked C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄ in four frames. Practice: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + C₄H₈.
6. On your own (C40-13–17): three tubes read for the alkene; which method uses hot aluminium oxide; C₈H₁₈ → C₆H₁₄ + C₂H₄; a true statement about cracking; a written explanation of why and how to test.

Out of scope: general formulae of alkanes and alkenes; naming and structure of alkenes beyond "a C=C double bond"; polymer making; conditions (temperatures, pressures); Higher-tier mechanism detail; the exam question on the page (pentane cracking) was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 147 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.7.1.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The bromine water test note says it is done by, or under close supervision of, a teacher (bromine water is harmful).
- "A C=C double bond" is mentioned once so alkenes are not just a name; students are not asked to draw them.
- The equations in questions are all one alkane into a smaller alkane plus one alkene, which is what the page's example does.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Chains are drawn as a row of small connected carbon beads (a short zig-zag is fine); an alkene has one visibly doubled link. Colours from `atomPalette`: alkanes in one tint, alkenes in a second tint, so they stay distinguishable in every frame. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px.

- `crack-demand`: two arrows into a small fuel pump: a short one labelled "small molecules: high demand" and a long one labelled "long chains: not wanted as much". Beside it a bar chart-like picture: a tall bar of long chains, a short bar of small ones labelled "what oil has" against "what we need" (schematic, no numbers).
- `crack-split`: a long chain in the middle with scissors/lightning "heat" and an arrow to two or three shorter chains. Label "cracking".
- `crack-products`: the long chain splitting into a shorter alkane (labelled "alkane: fuel, e.g. petrol") and a small alkene with one double link (labelled "alkene").
- `crack-alkene-uses`: the small alkene in the centre with three arrows: "more reactive than alkanes", "starting material for other compounds", "used to make polymers" (a small repeating chain).
- `crack-thermal`: a long chain, a flame under it and the same chain broken into pieces. Label "thermal decomposition: broken down by heating".
- `crack-steam`: a simple three-step flow: 1 "vaporise" (a gas cloud), 2 "mix with steam", 3 "heat to a very high temperature". Long chains splitting at the end.
- `crack-catalytic`: a heated tube containing powder specks (aluminium oxide catalyst), vapour of long chains entering left, smaller chains leaving right. A magnified speck with a chain splitting on its surface. Labels: "hot powdered aluminium oxide catalyst", "molecules split apart on the surface".
- `crack-methods`: two columns side by side, "Steam cracking" and "Catalytic cracking", each with its three-step flow icons; the shared steps (vaporise, heat) aligned across, the difference (steam / catalyst) highlighted.
- `crack-br-setup`: two test tubes: one of orange bromine water, one of hydrocarbon (pale). An arrow "add and shake". A small note "orange".
- `crack-br-alkane`: a tube of orange bromine water plus an alkane before and after shaking, both bright orange. Label "no reaction: stays orange".
- `crack-br-alkene`: a tube before shaking (orange) and after (colourless). Label "bromine reacts: goes colourless". Alkane and alkene may sit side by side in the last frame.
- `crack-eq-setup`: C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + ? with the missing product as an empty dashed box. A tally beside each formula: 10 C and 22 H on the left; 8 C and 18 H on the right, missing box shown with "? C, ? H".
- `crack-eq-carbon`: the same, with the carbon counts highlighted: 10 − 8 = 2, so the box shows "2 C".
- `crack-eq-hydrogen`: hydrogen counts highlighted: 22 − 18 = 4, so the box shows "2 C, 4 H".
- `crack-eq-answer`: the full equation C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄ with a tick and the check 8 + 2 = 10 and 18 + 4 = 22.
- `crack-q-tubes` (question, assessment view): three test tubes numbered 1, 2, 3 after shaking with bromine water: tubes 1 and 3 orange and tube 2 colourless. No words like "alkane" or "alkene" and no results text. Neutral accessible description: "Three numbered test tubes; two contain an orange liquid and one is colourless."
