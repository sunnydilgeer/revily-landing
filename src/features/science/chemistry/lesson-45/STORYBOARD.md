# Chemistry Lesson 45 storyboard — How the atmosphere evolved

Chapter C9, Chemistry of the atmosphere. Folder `chemistry/lesson-45`, id `C-ATM-045-C`, skill `C-ATMOSPHERE`. It owns the three phases of the atmosphere's history, carbon locked in sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels, and the composition of air today. Crude oil formation is linked in one clause (taught in the crude oil lesson). Photosynthesis is recalled from Biology and the equation shown once.

Big idea: the atmosphere has changed. Volcanoes gave out mostly carbon dioxide (phase 1). Carbon dioxide was then removed by the oceans, algae, plants and by being locked in rocks and fossil fuels (phase 2). Algae and plants made oxygen by photosynthesis (phase 3). Air today is about 80% nitrogen and 20% oxygen. It is a theory, because the evidence is very old.

Flow note: told in time order, one phase per section, because the story is a sequence and each phase leads to the next. The "theory, not certainty" idea comes early so students hold it while learning the phases. The three-phase recap ends the last walkthrough and the "On your own" section tests the order.

Sections:
1. Start here (C45-01): which gas is the biggest share of air today.
2. Phase 1: volcanoes (C45-02–04): volcanoes → a best theory → mostly carbon dioxide → other gases → oceans form. Checks: what the early atmosphere was; how oceans formed.
3. Phase 2: carbon dioxide removed (C45-05–07): dissolves → carbonates and sediments → plants and algae → carbon locked in rock → fossil fuels and limestone. Checks: what happened to the carbon dioxide; a fuel from plankton.
4. Phase 3: oxygen (C45-08–10): algae → photosynthesis → oxygen builds up → air today → other gases → all three phases together. Checks: the process that added oxygen; nitrogen fraction.
5. On your own (C45-11–16): read a bar of air today; limestone; why "oxygen was always there" is wrong; order of changes; fossil fuel pair; written explanation.

Out of scope: how oxygen levels changed in detail (banded iron, ozone); other theories of the early atmosphere; noble gas names; volumes of gas calculations; greenhouse effect and climate change (next lesson); pollution.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 153 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.9.1.1 to 5.9.1.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's own time figures are used: about 4.6 billion years for the Earth, algae about 2.7 billion years ago, proportions steady for about 200 million years.
- The symbol equation for photosynthesis appears in one frame as recall; students are not asked to balance it.
- "Precipitation" is explained once in words (an insoluble solid forming from a solution).

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Course colours: purple = carbon dioxide, blue = water, teal = oxygen, yellow = Sun/energy, green = plants. Nitrogen in a neutral grey-blue and other gases in a pale grey. Gas particles are small labelled dots, not chemical-diagram detail. Text in the SVG at least 12px; the drawing must read on a 360px phone.

- `atmos-volcanoes`: a soft landscape of several rounded volcanoes erupting into a dull sky, no oceans, no plants. Label "about 4.6 billion years ago" and "volcanoes give out gases".
- `atmos-theory`: the same landscape faded, with a small thought-bubble style note "our best theory" and a faint "?" over the sky. Label "very old, so hard to get evidence".
- `atmos-early-gases`: the sky above a volcano as a wide band of labelled gas dots: many purple CO₂, few of the others, and no teal O₂. Small labels "mostly carbon dioxide" and "little or no oxygen". A small side note "like Mars and Venus today".
- `atmos-volcano-other`: the same band, now highlighting nitrogen (N₂, grey-blue, "builds up over time"), water vapour (H₂O, blue) and small amounts of methane and ammonia (pale dots, "small amounts"), with CO₂ still the most.
- `atmos-oceans`: a cooling scene in three steps left to right: 1 "hot: water vapour in the air", 2 "cooler: vapour condenses", 3 "liquid water collects: oceans". Blue.
- `atmos-dissolve`: an ocean with purple CO₂ dots moving from the air into the water, label "carbon dioxide dissolves in the oceans". A small fading purple band in the air above.
- `atmos-sediments`: a sea cross-section: dissolved CO₂ → tiny solid grains settling to the seabed in a layer. Labels "carbonates form", "solid particles settle: sediments". A dashed callout "Precipitation: an insoluble solid forms from a solution".
- `atmos-photo-uptake`: green algae in the sea and a green plant on land, each with a purple arrow of CO₂ going in, and a yellow Sun. Label "photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide".
- `atmos-locked`: a seabed cross-section in three numbered steps, one drawing: 1 sea creatures die and sink, 2 buried under layers, 3 squashed over millions of years into rock layers containing a small oil and gas pocket. Label "carbon trapped".
- `atmos-fossil`: a labelled layered cross-section: coal seam (from "thick layers of plants"), crude oil and natural gas pocket (from "plankton"), limestone layer (from "shells and skeletons: calcium carbonate"). Small icons: fern, plankton, shell.

- `atmos-oxygen-rise`: a simple schematic two-line graph with no numbers on the axes ("time" across, "amount in the atmosphere" up): the purple CO₂ line falls, the teal O₂ line rises. A small animal icon appears at the right with label "animals can evolve".
- `atmos-air-today`: one horizontal stacked bar labelled "air today": about 80% grey-blue "nitrogen 80% (4/5)", about 20% teal "oxygen 20% (1/5)", and a very thin sliver "other gases: less than 1%". Note "about 200 million years of similar proportions".

- `atmos-timeline`: three panels left to right on one arrow of time: Phase 1 volcano with gases; Phase 2 ocean, algae/plant and rock layers with the CO₂ band shrinking; Phase 3 green plants with teal oxygen. Titles "1 Volcanoes gave out gases", "2 Carbon dioxide removed", "3 Plants and algae made oxygen".
