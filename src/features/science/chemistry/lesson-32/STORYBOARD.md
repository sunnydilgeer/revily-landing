# Chemistry Lesson 32 storyboard — What changes the rate of a reaction

Chapter C6, Rate and extent of chemical change. Folder `chemistry/lesson-32`, id `C-RAT-032-C`, skill `C-RATE-FACTORS`.

Big idea: four things change the rate: temperature, concentration (or pressure), surface area of a solid, and a catalyst. The first three all work through collision theory (how often, and how energetic); a catalyst gives a lower-activation-energy pathway.

Flow note: temperature first because it changes both frequency and energy. Concentration and pressure are then a pure "frequency" idea, and surface area is the same idea for solids. The catalyst is last because it works differently (activation energy), and its profile is only shown, not re-taught.

Sections:
1. Start here (C32-01): sugar in hot and iced tea.
2. Temperature (C32-02–04): particles move faster → more collisions → more energy → graph.
3. Concentration and pressure (C32-05–07): more particles in same space → frequent collisions → gas pressure.
4. Surface area (C32-08–10): lumps against pieces → exposed particles → more collisions.
5. Catalysts (C32-11–13): what it is → different pathway → on a profile → not in the equation, enzymes.
6. On your own (C32-14–18): four flasks, catalyst arrows, graph lines, zinc strip against powder, written concentration explanation.

Out of scope: measuring rates and required-practical methods (next lessons); drawing profiles (covered earlier); Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions (not on GCSE).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 137 (scope only). AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.6.1.2, 5.6.1.3, 5.6.1.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

## Diagram specs
Palette: `atomPalette`. Reactant A particles blue, reactant B coral, motion shown with soft curved trails. Glassware: rounded flasks or beakers, gentle tints.
- `rfac-temp-move`: two beakers side by side: "Cold" (few short trails, particles slow) and "Hot" (long trails, particles fast); warm/cool tint.
- `rfac-temp-collide`: same two beakers with collisions marked by small starbursts: 1 in cold, 4 in hot; label "more collisions per second".
- `rfac-temp-energy`: hot beaker with two collisions: one gentle (bounce), one energetic (products form); label "more collisions have enough energy".
- `rfac-temp-graph`: axes (Amount of product, Time) with two curves: "cold" original, "hot" steeper, flat sooner, same final height.
- `rfac-conc-more`: two beakers, "Low concentration" (about 4 particles) and "High concentration" (about 12), same volume drawn equal.
- `rfac-conc-collide`: same beakers with collision starbursts (1 against 5) and label "more frequent collisions".
- `rfac-pressure`: two gas syringes/cylinders with a piston: same 6 particles; low pressure (large volume, particles apart) and high pressure (piston pushed in, particles close). Labels "low pressure", "high pressure", "same number of particles".
- `rfac-sa-pieces`: two beakers: one large yellow lump ("large pieces: small surface area") and one with many small pieces ("small pieces: large surface area"), a few dissolved-reactant particles around each.
- `rfac-sa-exposed`: cut-through of a lump (surface particles highlighted, buried ones faded) beside a cluster of small pieces (nearly all highlighted); label "exposed particles".
- `rfac-sa-collide`: the two beakers again with collision starbursts (few vs many); label "more frequent collisions".
- `rfac-cat-what`: reactants arrow to products with a small catalyst shape above the arrow, and a second catalyst shape after the arrow, unchanged; labels "catalyst" and "not used up".
- `rfac-cat-pathway`: a hill drawn as a path a particle must climb; long high route (no catalyst) and a lower route (with catalyst); label "lower activation energy".
- `rfac-cat-profile`: reaction profile, two curves from the same reactant level to the same product level: high peak "Without catalyst", lower peak "With catalyst"; arrows for the two activation energies. Keep as in Lesson 30's style (Lesson 30 owns profiles).
- `rfac-cat-notes`: three small labelled tiles: "Not in the equation", "Different reactions need different catalysts", "Enzymes: biological catalysts".
