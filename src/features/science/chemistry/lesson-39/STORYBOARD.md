# Chemistry Lesson 39 storyboard — Fractional distillation

Chapter C7, Organic chemistry. Folder `chemistry/lesson-39`, id `C-ORG-039-C`, skill `C-FRACTIONAL-DISTILLATION`. It builds on the crude oil lesson, which owns how crude oil forms, that it is finite, and chain-length trends; this lesson owns the column.

Big idea: crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons. Heating it and cooling the vapour as it rises up a column that is hot at the bottom and cool at the top separates it into fractions. Long chains have high boiling points and leave low down; short chains have low boiling points and leave near the top.

Flow note: the process comes first (heat, temperature gradient, rise and condense), because the reason for the separation only makes sense once students can see the column. The boiling-point reasoning is its own section, since it is the exam step. The named fractions and uses come last and are kept light.

Sections:
1. Start here (C39-01): why crude oil cannot go straight into a fuel tank.
2. How does the column work? (C39-02–04): a mixture to separate → heat the oil → hot below, cool above → rise, cool, condense. Checks: hottest part; what a fraction is.
3. Why do the fractions separate? (C39-05–07): short chains → long chains → different heights → a fraction is similar carbon numbers. Checks: a low-boiling hydrocarbon; why long chains leave low.
4. What comes out? (C39-08–10): the six fractions top to bottom → longer chains and higher boiling points down the column (about 3, 8, 15, 20, 40 carbons) → one use each. Checks: shortest molecules; order top to bottom.
5. On your own (C39-11–15): a column with four scrambled outlet numbers, three boiling points, why short chains reach the top, a 30-carbon fraction compared with diesel oil, and a written explanation of LPG against the 40-carbon fraction.

Out of scope: how crude oil formed, that it is finite, chain-length trends in flammability and viscosity (crude oil lesson); cracking (next lesson); exact boiling temperatures and temperature values in the column; intermolecular forces; the catalogue of fraction uses beyond one clause each.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 146 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.7.1.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. The exam question on the page was not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Carbon numbers (about 3, 8, 15, 20, 40) follow the page and are given only as approximate; students are not asked to recall them.
- The liquid that does not evaporate is said to be drained from the bottom; bitumen is listed as the bottom fraction.
- Heavy fuel oil is described as fuel for large ships and bitumen for road surfaces (standard uses; teacher to confirm level).

## Diagram specs
One column drawing is reused in every frame, built up step by step. It is a soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling tower (like Lessons 17 and 18: gentle tints, no hard geometry) with a furnace and pipe at the bottom left and outlet pipes on the right. Temperature tint runs from warm orange-red at the bottom to pale cool blue-white at the top. Non-question views carry a short caption. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px. Colours from `atomPalette`.

- `frac-mixture`: a beaker or oil drum of dark crude oil, with a handful of small chain molecules of different lengths drawn inside it (short, medium, long, jumbled). Label "Crude oil: a mixture of hydrocarbons". Arrow to a faint, empty column labelled "fractionating column".
- `frac-heat`: the column outline with the furnace at the bottom left. A pipe from crude oil goes into the furnace ("Heat"), then an arrow of vapour into the lower part of the column. A small drain at the bottom with a drop labelled "liquid left behind is drained off".
- `frac-gradient`: the column with the temperature tint shown clearly. A vertical arrow beside it, thin at the top, thick at the bottom, or a bar labelled "Very hot" at the bottom and "Cool" at the top.
- `frac-rise`: same column. Wavy vapour arrows rise. At three heights a few droplets form on trays and short outlet arrows show liquid draining off. Labels: "vapour rises and cools", "condenses to a liquid", "liquid drained off".
- `frac-short`: same column with the top region highlighted. A short 3-carbon chain (about 3 tiny beads) sits at the top, with a label "Short chains: low boiling point, stay gas until cool".
- `frac-long`: the bottom region highlighted. A long, curly chain (about 40 beads) in the bottom part. Label "Long chains: high boiling point, condense while hot".
- `frac-heights`: both. Five chains of increasing length in a column on the left, shortest at the top and longest at the bottom, each level aligned with an outlet. Arrow up the side labelled "chains get shorter", arrow down labelled "boiling points get higher".
- `frac-similar`: one band of the column enlarged in a magnifier: a few chains of nearly the same length together. Label "One fraction: similar chain lengths, similar boiling points".
- `frac-list`: the full column with all six outlets on the right, each named in order from the top: LPG (liquefied petroleum gas), petrol, kerosene, diesel oil, heavy fuel oil, bitumen (bottom). Bitumen leaves at the very bottom.
- `frac-trend`: same column, outlets labelled with approximate carbon numbers ("about 3", "about 8", "about 15", "about 20", "about 40", bitumen "longest") and a small chain drawn beside each, growing longer downwards. Arrows down: "longer chains, higher boiling points".
- `frac-uses`: same column, each outlet gets a small simple icon: a gas flame (LPG), a car (petrol), an aeroplane (kerosene), a lorry (diesel oil), a ship (heavy fuel oil), a road (bitumen). Icons are original, soft and simple.
- `frac-q-column` (question, assessment view): the same column drawn with the tint but no words on it: no "hot", "cool", fraction names or carbon numbers. Four outlets with numbered pointers scrambled top to bottom: the top outlet is 3, the next is 1, the next is 4 and the bottom outlet is 2. Nothing else may show the answer. The accessible description is neutral: "A fractionating column with four numbered outlets".
