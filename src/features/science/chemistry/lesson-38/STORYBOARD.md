# Chemistry Lesson 38 storyboard: Crude oil

Chapter C7, Organic chemistry. Folder `chemistry/lesson-38`, id `C-ORG-038-C`, skill `C-CRUDE-OIL`. It builds on the hydrocarbons lesson (alkanes, chain of carbon atoms) and is followed by the fractional distillation lesson.

Big idea: crude oil is a finite, non-renewable fossil fuel formed from ancient plankton. It is a mixture of hydrocarbons of many chain lengths, used as fuels and as feedstock. Short-chain hydrocarbons are runnier, boil at lower temperatures and are more flammable than long-chain ones.

Flow note: where it comes from and why it runs out first (the story frames one picture of sea, mud and rock); then what it is used for (fuels, then the more surprising feedstock use, ending with the organic-compound and homologous-series idea that explains the variety); last the property trends, which need the idea of hydrocarbons of different sizes. The trend section ends with a short-versus-long summary frame that hands on to the next lesson (the column that separates the chains).

## Diagram specs (diagram worker: `components/CrudeOilVisuals.tsx`, prefix `crude-`)

Frames:
- `crude-fossil`: cross-section scene: sea at the top, layers of rock below, a dark pool labelled "crude oil" trapped in the rock and a small oil rig above. Labels: "fossil fuel", "found in rocks", "source of energy" (a small spark/heat wave icon).
- `crude-formed`: a three-stage strip left to right: 1 "Plankton die and sink" (tiny plankton in water), 2 "Mud buries the remains" (layers), 3 "Millions of years later: crude oil" (dark pool). Clock/"millions of years" arrow between 2 and 3.
- `crude-rate`: two bars or arrows: a very long "takes millions of years to form" arrow versus a very short "used in minutes" arrow, with an oil drop and a car exhaust/tank. Label "used faster than it forms = non-renewable".
- `crude-finite`: a large barrel or tank being drained to a low level with an empty warning mark; label "finite: it will run out one day".
- `crude-fuels`: a row of five transport icons (car, lorry, train, plane, ship) each tagged with a fuel: petrol, diesel oil, diesel oil, kerosene, heavy fuel oil; plus a gas cylinder "LPG".
- `crude-feedstock`: oil barrel on the left, an arrow into a factory/flask labelled "petrochemical industry", the tag "feedstock = raw material".
- `crude-products`: the factory in the centre, four outputs around it: polymers (a plastic bottle/bag), solvents, lubricants, detergents (small simple icons, each labelled).
- `crude-organic`: a barrel labelled "crude oil" with a soft ring around chain drawings and the text "contain carbon atoms = organic compounds" and "most are hydrocarbons".
- `crude-homologous`: two families side by side, alkanes (three chains of different lengths) and alkenes (one chain with a double line shown between two carbons, label only "alkenes", no more detail), banner "each family = a homologous series: similar compounds, similar properties".

- `crude-viscosity`: two beakers: short chain runs quickly (drip streaming), long chain thick and slow (slow drip); labels "runny, less viscous" / "thick, more viscous"; a small arrow "shorter chain".
- `crude-boiling`: two test tubes over flames, short chain bubbling at a lower thermometer reading (e.g. "lower temperature") and long chain needing a higher reading. Use words not numbers.
- `crude-flammable`: short chain lights easily (big flame on a splint), long chain barely burns (small flame); "more flammable" / "harder to ignite".
- `crude-compare`: a two-column summary: short chain (runny, low boiling point, flammable) and long chain (thick, high boiling point, harder to ignite) with a chain-length arrow from short to long.
