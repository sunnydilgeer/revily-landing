# Chemistry Lesson 50 storyboard — Reuse and recycling

Chapter C10, Using resources. Folder `chemistry/lesson-50`, id `C-RES-050-C`, skill `C-RECYCLING`. It owns sustainable development, recycling metals (blast furnace in one clause only) and reusing and recycling glass. Renewable and finite resources come from the previous lesson.

Big idea: sustainable development meets today's needs without harming people in the future. Using fewer finite resources, by reusing and recycling, saves energy, raw materials and waste.

Flow note: the idea of sustainability first, then metals (the biggest energy story), then glass as a second, simpler example of reuse versus recycling.

Sections:
1. Start here (C50-01): an empty glass bottle.
2. What is sustainable development? (C50-02–04): thinking ahead → why using resources can be unsustainable → use less → reuse and recycle. Checks: definition; a sustainable action.
3. Why recycle metals? (C50-05–07): metals cost energy → three benefits → separate, melt, recast → steel and iron together. Checks: source of the energy; a benefit.
4. Reusing and recycling glass (C50-08–10): why → reusing bottles → crushing, melting, reshaping. Checks: what is done to non-reusable glass; why reuse saves energy.
5. On your own (C50-11–15): aluminium cans; a bar chart read without over-claiming; reuse or recycling; steel and iron together; a written explanation.

Out of scope: blast furnace chemistry; life cycle assessments (later lessons); costs and economics; Higher-tier content.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 159 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.10.1.1, 5.10.2.2. All wording, examples and data are original (energy figures in the chart are invented for reading practice). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher: the chart supports only the energy claim; the question tests that students do not claim cost or waste from it.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Yellow lightning bolts = energy, green = recycling arrows, glass in pale blue-green, metal in soft grey. Text in the SVG at least 12px.

- `recycle-sustain`: a balance or two-person scene: "people today" and "people in the future" both under one umbrella arrow labelled "needs of both".
- `recycle-unsustain`: three small panels with numbers: 1 "finite raw materials" (a shrinking pile), 2 "extraction: energy and waste" (digger, energy spark, waste heap), 3 "processing: energy from finite fuels" (furnace, fuel drum).
- `recycle-less`: two piles of a resource: a large one used up quickly and a larger remainder where less is used; label "use less: saves the resource and the energy to make it".
- `recycle-loop`: a loop of arrows: product, used, collected, processed (recycled), new product; a small side arrow "reuse: use it again as it is".
- `recycle-metal-energy`: a mine, a furnace with a flame and yellow energy sparks, and a fossil-fuel drum labelled "most energy from burning fossil fuels".
- `recycle-metal-benefits`: three labelled icons: "much less energy" (small spark), "saves some of the limited metal" (ore pile), "less waste to landfill" (small heap).
- `recycle-metal-steps`: three steps left to right: "separate" (metal items sorted from waste), "melt down" (molten metal in crucible), "recast" (mould producing a new object).
- `recycle-metal-mixed`: steel and iron scrap going together into a blast furnace with "less iron ore needed"; other metals in a separate bin labelled "separate if the product needs it".
- `recycle-glass-benefits`: two icons: "less energy to make new glass" and "less glass thrown away".
- `recycle-glass-reuse`: a bottle cycle: filled, used, collected, washed, refilled. Label "reuse: same shape".
- `recycle-glass-recycle`: crushed glass (cullet), molten glass, new jars. Label "crush, melt, reshape".
- `recycle-q-energy` (question, assessment view): a simple bar chart, "Energy used to make 1 tonne of metal (units)": bar "from ore" 100, bar "from recycled scrap" 10, labelled axis. No conclusion words. Description: "A bar chart comparing the energy used to make a metal from ore, 100 units, and from recycled scrap, 10 units."
