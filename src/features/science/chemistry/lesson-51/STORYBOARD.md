# Chemistry Lesson 51 storyboard: Life cycle assessments

Chapter C10, Using resources. Folder `chemistry/lesson-51`, id `C-RES-051-C`, skill `C-LCA`. It owns what an LCA is and its four stages. Comparing LCAs (plastic and paper bags) and the problems and bias of LCAs belong to the next lesson.

Big idea: a life cycle assessment looks at every stage of a product's life to judge its impact on the environment. The four stages are getting the raw materials, manufacture and packaging, using the product, and product disposal. Each stage can use energy or resources, or cause pollution or waste.

Flow note: what and why first (whole life, total environmental cost, the four names), then the stages in two halves in the order they happen, so each teaching walkthrough is 4 to 5 frames and not a list of 16 points. The last frame puts the cycle back together. Guided questions check each half. "On your own" tests order, sorting a scenario into a stage, a numbered diagram, one effect in the using stage, and a written plan for a new product.

Sections:
1. Start here (C51-01): what to check before selling a new bottle.
2. What is an LCA? (C51-02 to 04): whole life, why a company does one, the four stages. Checks: what an LCA looks at; what impact means.
3. Getting materials and making it (C51-05 to 07): stage 1 raw materials, processing needs energy, stage 2 manufacture, waste from making. Checks: which belongs to stage 1; a stage 2 problem.
4. Using it and getting rid of it (C51-08 to 10): stage 3 use, how long it is used for, stage 4 landfill, burning, the cycle put together. Checks: long life; a disposal problem.
5. On your own (C51-11 to 15): the stages in order; a factory scenario; a numbered cycle diagram; burning fuel in use; a written plan for a metal bottle (teacher marked).

Out of scope: comparing LCAs and bias (next lesson); recycling and reuse in detail (earlier lesson); numerical LCA data; ISO standards; Higher-tier detail. The page's cartoons, jokes and its paint example were not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 160 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.10.2.1. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Extraction of metals from ores and fractional distillation of crude oil are named in one clause as examples of energy-hungry processing, not re-taught.
- "Greenhouse gases" is recalled from the greenhouse-gas lesson; carbon monoxide is named in one clause and taught in the air pollution lesson.

## Diagram specs
Same look as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, gentle tints, hand-drawn feeling, no more detail than Foundation needs, text in the SVG at least 12px, viewBox about 600 wide. Course colours: green = plants, blue = water, purple = carbon dioxide, teal = oxygen, yellow = Sun/energy. Use the same four stage colours in every frame (stage 1 blue-grey, stage 2 amber, stage 3 purple-lilac, stage 4 teal-grey) and keep a small numbered badge 1 to 4 on each stage. One shared drawing is built up: a ring of four rounded panels, one per stage, joined by soft arrows going clockwise; the current stage is bold and the others faded, as in Lesson 18.

- `lca-whole`: a simple product (a plastic-free everyday object such as a drinks bottle) in the centre with a soft loop arrow around it from "ground" to "bin", label "every stage of its life". Labels: "life cycle assessment (LCA)", "impact = effect on the environment".
- `lca-why`: a company building with a new product on a table and a soft scale or tick-list beside it: "total environmental cost". Shows a factory and a bin and the ground so it is clear the whole life is counted, not just the factory.
- `lca-four`: the ring of four numbered stage panels, all full colour: "1 Raw materials", "2 Manufacture and packaging", "3 Using the product", "4 Product disposal", each with a small icon (digger or rock; factory; person using the object; bin or landfill).
- `lca-raw`: ring with stage 1 highlighted. Inside, a quarry or mine with a digger, labels "extracted from the ground", "can damage local environment", "uses energy", "can cause pollution" (a small grey cloud).
- `lca-process`: stage 1 still highlighted. A lump of rock or a barrel labelled "raw material" going through a machine and coming out as a shaped plate or pipe labelled "useful material", with a small yellow energy bolt labelled "lots of energy". Optional two small tags: "metal from ore", "crude oil split by distillation".
- `lca-make`: stage 2 highlighted. A factory with a chimney, a box of packaging, a yellow energy bolt and a small cloud. Labels "energy and other resources", "pollution", "packaging".
- `lca-waste`: stage 2. A factory with a chemical reaction flask; an arrow to a "waste" bin and a second arrow from the waste to a "useful chemical" bottle. Labels: "waste made by reactions", "some waste turned into useful chemicals: less pollution".
- `lca-use`: stage 3 highlighted. A car-like or stove icon burning fuel with a purple cloud labelled "greenhouse gases and other harmful substances", and a small field with a stream: fertiliser draining into a river with a plant and a fish icon, label "fertiliser drains into rivers: harms plants and animals". Purple = carbon dioxide.
- `lca-lifespan`: stage 3. Two timelines side by side: a short one for a product used a few times, a long one for a product used for years. The long one has fewer bins at the end. Labels "used a short time: more waste", "used for ages: less waste in the long run". Do not give numbers.
- `lca-landfill`: stage 4 highlighted. A landfill hill with a lorry driving in; labels "takes up space", "pollutes land and water" (a small stream), and the lorry with a small cloud "transport uses energy and gives out pollutants".
- `lca-burn`: stage 4. A furnace or bonfire of products with a grey smoke cloud, label "burnt (incinerated): air pollution".
- `lca-cycle`: the full ring with all four stages full colour and, under each, one short effect word: "energy, damage", "energy, waste", "fuel, uses", "landfill, burning". Centre text "total environmental cost".
- `lca-q-cycle` (question, assessment view): the ring of four panels with numbers 1 to 4 only and small unlabelled icons (rock, factory, person, bin) that do not name the stages; arrows show the direction. Nothing gives away which stage a landfill lorry belongs to beyond the order. Neutral accessible description: "A ring of four numbered stages joined by arrows, in order from 1 to 4."
