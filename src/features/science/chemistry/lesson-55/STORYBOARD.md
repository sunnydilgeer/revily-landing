# Chemistry Lesson 55 storyboard — Waste water treatment

Chapter C10, Using resources. Folder `chemistry/lesson-55`, id `C-RES-055-C`, skill `C-SEWAGE`. It owns the sources of waste water, why it is treated, screening, sedimentation, aerobic and anaerobic digestion, methane and fertiliser, and the trade-offs. Desalination is recalled from the potable water lesson in one clause.

Big idea: waste water from homes, farms and factories holds pollutants (organic matter, harmful microbes), so a sewage treatment plant cleans it in stages before it returns to rivers and lakes: screening, sedimentation, then bacteria treat the effluent with oxygen and the sludge without oxygen, giving methane and fertiliser.

Flow note: why first (sources, pollutants, back to rivers), then the two physical stages, then the two biological stages, because "effluent" and "sludge" come out of sedimentation and are needed to follow the digestions. Trade-offs and toxic waste come last as they compare the whole process with desalination. The last stage is one whole-plant picture.

Sections:
1. Start here (C55-01): where bath water goes.
2. Why treat waste water? (C55-02–04): homes → farms and factories → pollutants → back to rivers. Checks: a source; why treat.
3. Screening and sedimentation (C55-05–07): route overview → screening → sedimentation → effluent and sludge. Checks: what screening removes; what sinks.
4. Aerobic and anaerobic digestion (C55-08–10): aerobic digestion → released → anaerobic digestion → methane and fertiliser → whole plant. Checks: meaning of aerobic; what happens to effluent.
5. Extra treatment and trade-offs (C55-11–12): more stages but less energy than desalination → an option where fresh water is scarce, though some people dislike it → toxic substances. Check: extra stages for toxic waste.
6. On your own (C55-13–17): numbered plant diagram; twig and plastic bag; anaerobic products; reasoning about a town short of fresh water; a written ordered description.

Out of scope: named bacteria and the chemistry of digestion; treatment plant design details; costs and regulation; Higher-tier content; the exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 164 (scope only; page 165 is the topic test, used only as quiz ideas); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.10.1.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page lists grit under screening, so the lesson keeps it there.
- "Organic matter" is glossed as carbon compounds from the remains and waste of living things, as on the page.
- The opinion point (people dislike drinking water that used to be sewage) is stated neutrally, as an attitude, not a reason to reject the option.

## Diagram specs
Organic, soft and slightly hand-drawn, as in Lessons 17 and 18: rounded tanks, gentle tints, no engineering detail. One town drawing is reused through "Why treat waste water?" and one plant drawing through the screening, sedimentation and digestion walkthroughs; the part under discussion is in full colour and the rest faded. Numbered stage badges 1 screening, 2 sedimentation, 3 aerobic digestion, 4 anaerobic digestion, with a key of the four stage names under the plant (the current stage bold). Course colours: blue = water (effluent lighter blue, sludge in a brown tint), green = plants/fertiliser, yellow = energy, teal = oxygen. Text in the SVG ≥ 12px.

- `sewage-homes`: a small house with a bath/sink/toilet, a pipe going into the ground to a sewer that leads right to a plant icon. Labels "washing-up, toilet, bath", "sewer", "sewage treatment plant".
- `sewage-sources`: three sources feeding one sewer arrow towards the plant: house "homes", a field with a tractor or crops "agriculture (farming)", a factory with a chimney "industrial processes". Label "waste water".
- `sewage-pollutants`: a glass or tank of murky waste water with two callouts: "organic matter" (small leaf/food-scrap flecks, note "carbon compounds from the remains and waste of living things") and "harmful microbes" (a few simple bacteria and virus blobs). Small note "pollutants".
- `sewage-return`: from the plant a tidy pipe to a river with a lake; a tick and the label "treated first, so no health problems". A faded murky pipe with a cross to the river marks the untreated route.
- `sewage-route`: the whole plant as a walkthrough, all four numbered stages drawn and named, arrows in order, all in light form (screen, settling tank, aerobic tank, sealed sludge tank, river, cooker, fertiliser sacks faded), so later frames can highlight one.
- `sewage-screening`: the plant drawing with stage 1 highlighted and a round close-up of the screen (bars) catching a twig and a plastic bag, with small stones and sand grains at the bottom. Labels "screen", "large bits: twigs, plastic bags", "grit: small bits of stone and sand".
- `sewage-sediment`: stage 2 highlighted: a settling tank cut-away. Heavier solids sinking to a brown layer labelled "sludge" at the bottom, lighter liquid on top labelled "effluent". Small arrows show sinking. Labels "heavier solids sink", "lighter liquid floats on top". "Effluent" is left to the next frame (one new term per frame).
- `sewage-split`: the settling tank with two exits: effluent pipe leaving from the top toward stage 3, sludge pipe leaving from the bottom toward stage 4. Labels "effluent (liquid waste) to stage 3" and "sludge to stage 4" (the key names the stages).
- `sewage-aerobic`: stage 3 highlighted: a tank of effluent with air bubbles rising (teal) and small bacteria shapes among organic flecks that are shrinking. Labels "air (oxygen) bubbled through", "bacteria break down organic matter and other microbes", and a note "aerobic = with oxygen".
- `sewage-released`: the aerobic tank with an arrow out to a river scene. Label "treated water released into the environment".
- `sewage-anaerobic`: stage 4 highlighted: a sealed rounded tank containing sludge and bacteria, no air arrows, a gas arrow leaving from the top labelled "methane gas". Label "sealed: no oxygen gets in", note "anaerobic = without oxygen" with a small crossed-out O2 sign. The cooker and fertiliser are hidden until the next frame.
- `sewage-products`: the sealed tank with two arrows out: one gas to a cooker ring labelled "methane: an energy source, e.g. cooking" (yellow tint), one solid to sacks with plants labelled "remaining waste: fertiliser" (green).
- `sewage-whole`: the full plant drawing, all four stages in full colour and all labels: screening, sedimentation, aerobic digestion, released to river, anaerobic digestion, methane, fertiliser. Effluent and sludge routes clearly distinct.
- `sewage-compare`: two schematic panels, no numbers. "Stages": fresh water (2 stage pills) beside waste water (4 pills), label "more stages". "Energy needed": waste water (short bar, "less energy") beside desalination (tall bar with a flame). Only the two comparisons the page makes are drawn.
- `sewage-option`: a dry landscape with a small reservoir low, and the plant returning treated water; two speech-free icons: a tick "an option where there is little fresh water" and a thoughtful face "some people dislike the idea of drinking it". Keep it neutral.
- `sewage-toxic`: a factory pipe of toxic waste water (warning triangle, "toxic substances") entering three extra treatment icons in a row: a chemical dropper "adding chemicals", a UV lamp "UV radiation", a filter sheet "membranes". Label "extra stages".
- `sewage-q-plant` (question, assessment view): the whole plant with the four stages shown only as numbers 1–4 in circles on their tanks (1 screen, 2 settling tank, 3 aerobic tank with bubbles, 4 sealed tank), house at the start and river at the end. No stage names, no "effluent", "sludge", "aerobic", "anaerobic" or "methane" words and no product labels.
