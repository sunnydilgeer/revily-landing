# Chemistry Lesson 41 storyboard — Purity and formulations

Chapter C8, Chemical analysis. Folder `chemistry/lesson-41`, id `C-ANA-041-C`, skill `C-PURITY`. Owns the chemical meaning of pure, the melting and boiling point test, and formulations.

Big idea: in chemistry, pure means one element or compound only, unlike the everyday meaning. A pure substance melts and boils at a specific temperature, so comparing a measured value with the data book value shows how pure a sample is. A formulation is a mixture made to a recipe with exact amounts, so it has the properties needed for a job.

Flow note: the everyday meaning first (students already hold it), then the chemical meaning as a contrast, then the test that makes purity measurable, then formulations as the "useful mixtures" idea, because formulations only make sense once students know a mixture is not pure. Mixtures and separation are recalled from earlier lessons.

Sections:
1. Start here (C41-01): "pure apple juice" as a chemist would see it.
2. What is a pure substance? (C41-02–04): everyday meaning → chemical meaning → pure or mixed → impure sample. Checks: definition; which is pure.
3. How pure is it? (C41-05–07): fixed temperature → data book → closer is purer → impurities and melting → impurities and boiling. Checks: pure substances melt at one temperature; which result means purer.
4. What is a formulation? (C41-08–10): designed mixture → recipe → right properties → paint → everywhere. Checks: definition; the binder.
5. On your own (C41-11–15): melting range read from a diagram; two boiling points; why amounts are measured; spot the formulation; written explanation of how to test purity.

Out of scope: naming the parts of specific medicines or foods; how impurities change melting points at particle level; chromatography as a purity test (next lessons); the aspirin exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 148 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.8.1.1, 5.8.1.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "Impurities raise the boiling point" is stated as the page does; no particle explanation is given.
- Pure water is used as the worked example (0 °C, 100 °C); lesson 54 later uses the 100 °C test.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Particles are simple circles; one substance = one colour from `atomPalette`. Text in the SVG at least 12px.

- `pure-everyday`: a carton labelled "pure juice, nothing added", with a magnified bubble showing several different coloured particle types mixed (sugar, water, flavour). Caption "still a mixture".
- `pure-chemistry`: a beaker with one kind of particle only (one colour), labelled "pure: one substance only".
- `pure-sort`: three small beakers side by side. "Pure water": one particle type. "Tap water": water particles plus a few dissolved specks. "Orange juice": several types. Each labelled "pure" or "mixture".
- `pure-impure`: a flask of a pale solid with a few odd-coloured specks mixed in, labelled "wanted compound" and "impurity".
- `pure-fixed`: a thermometer beside a beaker of ice melting at 0 °C and one boiling at 100 °C, labelled "pure water: melts at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C".
- `pure-compare`: a small data book page with "melting point: 136 °C" arrow to a thermometer reading of a measured value; label "compare".
- `pure-gap`: a number line of temperatures with the pure value marked; two measured samples: one very close, one far away, labelled "purer" and "less pure".
- `pure-melting`: a simple temperature-against-time line: pure sample as a flat step at one temperature; impure as a lower, sloping, wider step. Labels "pure: one temperature" and "impure: lower, across a range".
- `pure-boiling`: same idea for boiling: pure flat at 100 °C; impure higher and sloping. Labels "impure: higher, across a range".
- `pure-formulation`: a tube of hand cream, a paint tin and a tablet blister, each with a small mixture bubble; label "mixtures designed for a job".
- `pure-recipe`: a recipe card "formula" listing three measured parts with balance scale and measuring spoon; arrow to a finished product.
- `pure-properties`: two paint tins: one with correct amounts (smooth brush) and one with too much solvent (runny); labels "right amounts: right properties".
- `pure-paint`: a paint pot cut-away with four labelled parts: pigment (colour), solvent (dissolves, makes it runny), binder (holds pigment on), additives (change properties).
- `pure-everyday-formulations`: a tidy row of small icons with labels: cleaning spray, fuel, medicine, cosmetics, fertiliser, alloy, food and drink.
- `pure-q-melting` (question, assessment view): three thermometers or a small table, numbered Sample: "melts 118 to 124 °C", data book "128 °C". No words like "pure" or "impure". Neutral description: "A small table comparing a sample's melting range with a data book value."
