# Chemistry Lesson 46 storyboard — Greenhouse gases and climate change

Chapter C9, Chemistry of the atmosphere. Folder `chemistry/lesson-46`, id `C-ATM-046-C`, skill `C-GREENHOUSE`. It owns the greenhouse effect, human activities that add greenhouse gases, the evidence and its limits (peer review, models, bias) and the possible consequences of climate change. Carbon footprints and reducing emissions belong to the next lesson.

Big idea: greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour) keep the Earth warm by absorbing thermal radiation and giving it out in all directions, so some returns to the surface. Human activities add to them. Scientists agree the extra carbon dioxide from human activity has raised temperatures. The evidence is peer-reviewed, but the climate is complex and some media stories are biased. Possible effects include rising sea levels, changing rainfall, stronger storms and effects on food.

Flow note: mechanism first (four numbered steps, then all four together), because the human activities only make sense once students know what greenhouse gases do. Then the evidence and how to judge it, before the consequences, so the effects are read as possible outcomes backed by evidence. Tone stays factual and calm, using "may" and "could" as the page does.

Sections:
1. Start here (C46-01): why the Earth is warm enough for life.
2. The greenhouse effect (C46-02–04): gases that keep us warm → step 1 Sun → step 2 Earth → step 3 all directions → step 4 warming → all four together. Checks: a greenhouse gas; what the gases do with the radiation.
3. What people add (C46-05–07): overview → deforestation → burning fossil fuels → agriculture → waste. Checks: deforestation; which activity releases carbon dioxide.
4. Climate change and the evidence (C46-08–10): temperature rise → peer review → complex climate and models → media stories and bias. Checks: what has happened to temperature; why peer review helps.
5. Possible effects (C46-11–12): ice and sea level → rain and storms → food. Check: melting ice.
6. On your own (C46-13–17): a numbered greenhouse diagram; what biased means; why models are hard; which pair releases methane; written explanation of the greenhouse effect and human activities.

Out of scope: named wavelengths or the electromagnetic spectrum; ozone layer; global warming potential; carbon dioxide levels data; ways to cut emissions and why that is hard (next lesson); pollution (later lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 154 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.9.2.1 to 5.9.2.3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The four-step radiation explanation uses "short wavelength" and "long wavelength" as the page does, with "thermal (heat) radiation" explained in words.
- "Scientists agree" follows the page; the lesson then teaches how to judge evidence (peer review, models, bias) so it stays balanced without hedging the scientific consensus.
- Consequences are written as things that are happening or may happen, in a neutral tone; no pictures of disasters.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Course colours: yellow = Sun/radiation, purple = carbon dioxide, blue = water, green = plants, teal = oxygen. Methane in a pale orange dot. Radiation arrows: yellow short-wavelength arrows in, warm orange-red wavy arrows for thermal radiation out. The Earth is a rounded globe with a thin atmosphere band of greenhouse gas dots. Text in the SVG at least 12px; it must read on a 360px phone.

- `ghg-gases`: the globe with its thin atmosphere band containing labelled dots: CO₂ (purple), CH₄ (orange), H₂O (blue). Label "greenhouse gases" and "keep the Earth warm enough for life".
- `ghg-step1`: numbered walkthrough drawing shared with steps 2 to 4: Sun at top left, globe bottom right with atmosphere band. Step 1 highlighted: yellow short-wavelength (tight zig-zag) arrows from the Sun to the Earth, numbered pointer 1 "short wavelength radiation". Rest faded.
- `ghg-step2`: same drawing, step 2 highlighted: a wavy longer-wave orange arrow leaving the Earth's surface upward into the atmosphere band, pointer 2 "absorbed by the Earth, given out as long wavelength thermal (heat) radiation". Step 1 kept in place but lighter.
- `ghg-step3`: same drawing, step 3 highlighted: from the greenhouse gas dots, several small orange arrows radiating outwards in all directions. Pointer 3 "given out in all directions".
- `ghg-step4`: same drawing, step 4 highlighted: one or two of those arrows pointing back down at the surface, with warm glow on the surface. Pointer 4 "some heads back and warms the surface: the greenhouse effect".
- `ghg-effect`: the full drawing with all four numbered pointers at full strength, a key list 1 to 4 under the drawing, and a note "original schematic, not to scale".
- `ghg-human`: a 2 by 2 set of small rounded icons with the globe in the middle: cut trees ("deforestation"), a chimney and car ("burning fossil fuels"), a cow and a rice paddy ("agriculture"), a landfill heap ("creating waste"). Arrows from each into the atmosphere band.
- `ghg-deforest`: a forest on the left with purple CO₂ arrows going into the trees, and the same area with stumps on the right and the arrows crossed out / fewer, more purple dots left in the air. Labels "trees take in carbon dioxide" and "fewer trees: less taken in".
- `ghg-fossil`: a power station chimney, a car and a house chimney, with purple CO₂ dots rising. Label "burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide".
- `ghg-agri`: a cow with orange CH₄ dots leaving and a flooded rice paddy with orange CH₄ bubbles. Labels "farm animals digesting food" and "rice paddies".
- `ghg-waste`: a landfill heap with purple CO₂ and orange CH₄ dots rising. Label "waste breaks down: carbon dioxide and methane".
- `ghg-temp`: a simple schematic line, no numbers on the axes ("time" across, "average temperature" up), rising overall with small wiggles. Label "average temperature of the Earth’s surface has gone up recently". A small tag "extra carbon dioxide from human activity" beside it.
- `ghg-peer`: three small figures in a row: a scientist writing "evidence", a second scientist checking with a tick, then a page labelled "published". Label "peer review: other scientists check it" and "more reliable".
- `ghg-model`: a big swirl of many small factor icons (Sun, clouds, oceans, ice, forests, gases) linked around a globe, next to a plain simple model drawing (a globe with just one arrow) with a small "?" . Labels "the real climate: very complex" and "an oversimplified model may leave things out".
- `ghg-media`: a newspaper or phone-screen icon with a thumbs-up and a "?" beside it, and two small boxes: "based on good evidence" (tick) and "biased: favours one view / only some of the facts" (warning triangle, calm colour).
- `ghg-ice`: a two-part sea scene, before and after, showing polar ice shrinking with a small sea-level line rising against a coastal town. Labels "ice melts" and "sea level rises: more coastal flooding".
- `ghg-rain`: a split panel: one side dry cracked ground with a small cloud, the other side heavy rain and a swollen river, then a storm cloud icon. Labels "too little water", "too much water", "storms may be more frequent and more severe".
- `ghg-food`: a crop field with a small sun and cloud, and a question label. Labels "temperature and rainfall changes" and "may affect food production in some places". Calm, no dramatic imagery.
- `ghg-q-steps` (question, assessment view): the four-step greenhouse drawing with four numbered arrows (1 Sun's radiation in, 2 heat out of the Earth, 3 arrows from the greenhouse gases in all directions, 4 one arrow back to the surface) and no descriptive labels or key; only the numbers. No words like "short wavelength" or "greenhouse effect". Neutral accessible description: "A drawing of the Sun, the Earth and a band of gas around it, with four numbered arrows."
