# Chemistry Lesson 33 storyboard: Measuring rates using gas

Chapter C6, Rate and extent of chemical change. Folder `chemistry/lesson-33`, id `C-RAT-033-C`, skill `C-RATE-MEASURE-GAS`. Required practical preparation (rate and concentration): it teaches the method, variables and data handling, and does not claim to complete the practical.

Big idea: if a reaction makes a gas, you can measure its rate by measuring the gas: the volume collected in an upturned cylinder or a gas syringe, or the mass lost on a balance. More gas (or a quicker mass fall) in the same time means a faster reaction. To test concentration, change only the concentration and keep every other variable the same.

Flow note: gas volume first, with the subtraction worked example, then the balance as a second way that measures mass lost and leaks gas, then the fair test that uses either. Graph plotting and mean rate belong to a later lesson; the cross method to the next.

Sections:
1. Start here (C33-01): comparing two fizzing reactions.
2. Collecting the gas (C33-02–04): a gas-making reaction → the cylinder set-up → timing → syringe → volume produced (worked subtraction; guided C33-04).
3. Using a balance (C33-05–07): flask gets lighter → cotton wool → quicker fall = faster → choosing a method.
4. A fair test on concentration (C33-08–10): different concentrations → variables → reading results.
5. On your own (C33-11–15): numbered apparatus, a mass table, a subtraction, a toxic-gas drawback, a written plan.

Out of scope: graphs, tangents and mean-rate calculations (later lesson); the cross method (next lesson); reaction equations; measuring temperature or catalyst effects.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 138 (scope only). AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.6.1.1 and 5.6.1.2. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The starting reading (2 to 6 cm³) is kept, as on the page, so students subtract it; no explanation of why an upturned cylinder may not start at zero.
- Toxic-gas advice defers to the teacher (fume cupboard or collecting the gas).
- C33-12 fastest interval is read from mass falls (1.2 g, then smaller falls).

## Diagram specs
Drawing style: Chemistry palette (`atomPalette`), soft rounded glassware, gentle tints, hand-drawn-feeling curves, minimal detail. Reuse one apparatus drawing across the frames of a section. Marble chips are small grey lumps; acid is a pale blue-grey; gas bubbles are small circles.
- `gasrate-why`: a flask with marble chips and acid fizzing, bubbles rising, an arrow out labelled "carbon dioxide gas"; caption "more gas each second = faster reaction".
- `gasrate-set`: conical flask (chips, acid) with a delivery tube running to an upturned water-filled measuring cylinder in a trough of water; numbered labels 1 conical flask, 2 delivery tube, 3 measuring cylinder full of water, 4 water; bubbles entering the cylinder.
- `gasrate-start`: same drawing, stopwatch beside it; steps 1 add chips 2 attach tube and start the stopwatch together 3 read every 10 s; a small blank readings table.
- `gasrate-syringe`: flask with a tube to a horizontal gas syringe with a plunger marked in cm³; label "reads to the nearest cm³"; warning note "plunger can be pushed out if the reaction is too fast".
- `gasrate-table`: table: time 0, 10, 20, 30 s; reading 2, 14, 22, 27 cm³; volume produced 0, 12, 20, 25 cm³, with an arrow showing "reading − 2". Highlight the 10 s column: 14 − 2 = 12.
- `gasrate-balance`: flask on a top-pan balance (display showing a mass in g), bubbles leaving the mouth, an arrow "gas escapes", stopwatch.
- `gasrate-cotton`: neck of the flask enlarged with a plug of cotton wool; gas arrows pass through; acid drops stopped below it. Labels "gas escapes", "acid stays in".
- `gasrate-balance-fall`: balance readings table falling with time, e.g. 0 s 150.0 g, 30 s 148.8, 60 s 148.2, 90 s 148.0, with a downward arrow "mass falls as gas escapes".
- `gasrate-methods`: two-column comparison: "Collect the gas" (drawing of cylinder/syringe, "measures volume, keeps the gas in") and "Balance" (drawing, "measures mass lost, very accurate, gas released into the room").
- `gasrate-fair`: three flasks in a row with acid labelled low, medium, high concentration; same chips in each; stopwatches; caption "same method each time".
- `gasrate-vars`: table "Change" (concentration of acid) versus "Keep the same" (volume of acid, mass of marble chips, temperature); highlight the change row.
- `gasrate-read`: two upturned measuring cylinders side by side after 30 s: one about a third full of gas (weaker acid), one about two thirds full (stronger acid); caption "more gas in the same time = faster".
- Question visuals (assessment view, no words that answer): `gasrate-q-set` same apparatus as `gasrate-set` with numbered pointers only: 1 flask, 2 delivery tube, 3 upturned measuring cylinder, 4 water trough. `gasrate-q-table` a table: time 0, 30, 60, 90 s with mass 150.0, 148.8, 148.2, 148.0 g, no arrows or comment.
