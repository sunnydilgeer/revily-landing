# Chemistry Lesson 44 storyboard — Tests for gases

Chapter C8, Chemical analysis. Folder `chemistry/lesson-44`, id `C-ANA-044-C`, skill `C-GAS-TESTS`. It owns the four gas tests: chlorine, oxygen, carbon dioxide and hydrogen.

Big idea: many gases are colourless, so each is identified by a test with its own result. Chlorine bleaches damp litmus paper white; oxygen relights a glowing splint; carbon dioxide turns limewater cloudy; hydrogen gives a squeaky pop with a lit splint.

Flow note: the tests are taught in two pairs, grouped by what is used (paper and liquid, then splints), because the two splint tests are the ones most often swapped and are easiest to contrast side by side. A third section puts all four together, goes from result back to gas, and shows how to write up a test (test, observation, conclusion).

Sections:
1. Start here (C44-01): a colourless gas is given off; how do you find out which?
2. How do you test for chlorine and carbon dioxide? (C44-02–04): chlorine → limewater set-up → cloudy result → side by side. Checks: damp litmus in chlorine; limewater staying clear.
3. How do you test for oxygen and hydrogen? (C44-05–07): glowing splint → lit splint → the two compared. Checks: relighting splint; the squeaky pop.
4. Can you name the gas? (C44-08–10): the four tests together → results back to gases → writing up. Checks: which test for chlorine; a pop means which gas.
5. On your own (C44-11–15): four numbered results (which contains oxygen); two-clue gas; matching results to gases; what a full answer needs; a written description of two tests.

Out of scope: tests for ions and other gases; word or symbol equations for the reactions; why limewater goes cloudy (calcium carbonate); the exam question on the page was not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 151 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.8.2.1–5.8.2.4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Chlorine is toxic; frames say only a teacher makes or tests it.
- Damp litmus is described as bleached white, as on the page; no mention of it first turning red.
- The pop is explained in one clause (hydrogen burning quickly with oxygen from the air).
