# Chemistry Lesson 52 storyboard: Comparing life cycle assessments

Chapter C10, Using resources. Folder `chemistry/lesson-52`, id `C-RES-052-C`, skill `C-LCA-COMPARE`. It follows the four stages of an LCA and owns comparing two products (plastic and paper bags), reaching a conclusion, and the problems and bias of LCAs.

Big idea: LCA information can be compared stage by stage to decide which of two similar products is the least harmful overall. Each product has good and bad points, and the answer can be surprising: plastic bags are not usually biodegradable but may be less harmful because they take less energy to make and can be reused. LCAs have limits: some effects are easy to measure, others need judgement, and a selective LCA can be biased.

Flow note: comparison first (a table built one row at a time), then the conclusion (weigh, verdict, back each reason with a stage), then the problems (measurable, hard to measure, bias, selective), because bias only makes sense once students have seen a conclusion being drawn. The four stages are recalled, not re-taught. "On your own" reads a small invented table, spots a selective LCA, picks a fair comparison, spots bias, and ends with a written explanation.

Sections:
1. Start here (C52-01): paper or plastic, what should a shop compare?
2. Comparing two bags (C52-02 to 04): the task, raw materials and making them, using the bag, throwing it away, the table. Checks: paper's raw material; what biodegradable means.
3. Reaching a conclusion (C52-05 to 07): weigh up, the surprising verdict, back each reason with a stage. Checks: why plastic may be less harmful; a two-bag comparison.
4. Problems with LCAs (C52-08 to 10): easy to measure, hard to measure, judgement causes bias, selective LCAs. Checks: what is easy to measure; why results differ.
5. On your own (C52-11 to 15): read a numbered table of invented data; selective LCA; a fair comparison; bias in a student's comparison; a written explanation (teacher marked).

Out of scope: the full cracking and polymerisation chemistry (named in one clause); numerical LCAs; other product comparisons; Higher-tier content. The page's cartoons, jokes and slogan were not reused.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 161 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.10.2.1. All wording, questions and the invented Cup P and Cup Q data are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The bag comparison is given as the page gives it: "may be less harmful", not "is less harmful". The lesson shows it as one conclusion from stated information, not a universal rule.
- Paper bags are described as "non-toxic" and "biodegradable" as on the page.
- Bias is defined in plain words and then applied twice, in personal judgement and in selective LCAs.

## Diagram specs
Same look as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, gentle tints, hand-drawn feeling, text in the SVG at least 12px, viewBox about 600 wide, readable at phone width. Keep colours consistent: plastic bag = cool pale blue-grey, paper bag = warm tan. Two columns, one per bag, and four rows for the four stages, so the table builds up frame by frame. Course colours: yellow = energy, blue = water, purple = carbon dioxide, green = plants (wood).

- `lcause-task`: a plastic bag on the left, a paper bag on the right, a question mark and a scale between. Below, an empty table frame of four rows with labels "Raw materials", "Manufacture and packaging", "Using the product", "Product disposal". Label "least harmful to the environment?".
- `lcause-make`: table rows 1 and 2 highlighted. Plastic column: a barrel (crude oil), then a small factory with tags "fractional distillation, cracking, polymerisation" and "other fractions have other uses: less waste". Paper column: a tree (wood), then a factory with a yellow energy bolt and a waste bin, tags "pulping uses lots of energy" and "lots of waste".
- `lcause-use`: row 3 highlighted (rows 1 and 2 faded but visible). Plastic: the bag with three or four small reuse arrows and a bin liner icon, "reused several times, other uses". Paper: the bag with one arrow and "usually used once".
- `lcause-dispose`: row 4 highlighted. Plastic: landfill icon with "many types not biodegradable", "takes up space, pollutes land", small recycling arrow "recyclable". Paper: recycling arrow, a leaf, "recycled", "biodegradable", "non-toxic". A small speech bubble beside: "biodegradable: broken down naturally by microorganisms".
- `lcause-table`: all four rows full colour, short phrases only, same icons as previous frames: Raw materials: crude oil | wood. Making: less waste | lots of energy and waste. Using: reused several times | usually once. Disposal: recyclable, not usually biodegradable | recyclable, biodegradable, non-toxic.
- `lcause-weigh`: the same table faded, with a balance in front: a green up-tick or "good" tag and a red "bad" tag on each bag column. Label "good points and bad points: weigh them up". Nothing says which wins.
- `lcause-verdict`: the balance tilted towards the plastic bag column, with the two reasons highlighted on the table rows: "takes less energy to make" (row 2) and "longer lifespan: reused" (row 3), and the small bad point "not usually biodegradable" still visible. Label "may be less harmful overall".
- `lcause-evidence`: two sentence stems as linked arrows from the table rows to conclusion boxes: row 2 to "less energy to make", row 3 to "longer lifespan", each with a numbered stage badge. Label "name the stage each reason comes from".
- `lcause-measure`: a list of three measurable effects each with a small icon and a number-like ruler mark or bar: "energy used to make it" (yellow bolt), "natural resources used" (rock), "waste produced" (bin). Label "easy to measure: give as numbers".
- `lcause-hard`: a hedge or street with litter (a bag caught on a fence) and a question mark ruler: "how unattractive does it look?", with a person thought bubble "my judgement".
- `lcause-bias`: two people each doing an LCA on the same bag, with two different verdict tags (one leans left, one right), label "results could change depending on who does the assessment". Small box: "biased = favours one point of view, not backed by facts".
- `lcause-selective`: a report with four impact bars of which only two are shown in full colour and the rest greyed out or hidden by a sheet; a company logo shape (generic, not a real brand) with a speech bubble "our product is green". Labels "selective LCA: only some impacts shown", "can support a company's claims".
- `lcause-q-table` (question, assessment view): a small table with column headings "Stage", "Cup P", "Cup Q" and rows: "Energy to make (units)" 3 | 8; "Number of uses" 1 | 40; "Recycled after use?" No | Yes. Plain table, no conclusion text, no highlighting. Neutral accessible description: "A table comparing two cups on energy to make, number of uses and whether they are recycled."
