# Chemistry Lesson 36 storyboard — Reversible reactions and equilibrium

Chapter C6, Rate and extent of chemical change. Folder `chemistry/lesson-36`, id `C-RAT-036-C`, skill `C-REVERSIBLE`, AQA 5.6.2.1, 5.6.2.2, 5.6.2.3. It reuses endothermic and exothermic (recalled by topic, not re-taught).

Big idea: in a reversible reaction the products can turn back into the reactants (⇌). In a closed system the reaction reaches equilibrium when the forward and backward reactions go at exactly the same rate, so the amounts stop changing (they need not be equal). If there are more products the reaction is going forwards; changing the conditions can change the direction. If one direction is endothermic the other is exothermic, with the same amount of energy.

Flow note: idea first (with a real example), then equilibrium (the hardest part, by rates), then direction, then energy last because it needs the earlier ideas. Copper sulfate appears at the start and again at the end so the student sees the same reaction from two angles. No Le Chatelier's principle (Higher).
1. **Start here** (C36-01): an everyday change that can be undone (melting wax).
2. **What is a reversible reaction?** (C36-02–04): reactions that go backwards → forward/backward and ⇌ → hydrated copper sulfate. Checks: definition; which is the backward reaction.
3. **How does a reaction reach equilibrium?** (C36-05–08): only reactants at first → rates change → equal rates → not equal amounts → closed system. Checks: what is true of the two reactions; what it says about amounts; why closed.
4. **Which way is it going?** (C36-09–10): more products = forwards → conditions change direction → ammonium chloride heat/cool. Check: heating ammonium chloride.
5. **What happens to the energy?** (C36-11–12): opposite changes → same amount → copper sulfate. Check: backward reaction when forward is endothermic.
6. **On your own** (C36-13–16): copper sulfate colour changes (reversible); data table in a closed flask (no over-claiming); overall direction with more products; written: explain how equilibrium is reached.

Numbers: data table for C36-14 (time s → mass of product g): 0→0, 10→2.0, 20→3.0, 30→3.4, 40→3.5, 50→3.5, 60→3.5.

Out of scope: Le Chatelier's principle, effect of pressure, temperature or concentration on the position of equilibrium (Higher); equilibrium constants; industrial examples (Haber process); the book's signpost cartoon and jokes; the pressure and concentration parts of "changing conditions" beyond naming them.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The lesson says "the amounts of reactants and products stop changing" at equilibrium, and says the reaction carries on in both directions (dynamic).
- Ammonium chloride is described as breaking down when heated and re-forming when cooled, as on the page; the real reaction goes both ways at once in a closed tube and the sublimation-looking effect is not discussed.
- Copper sulfate: forward (heating, losing water) endothermic; backward (adding water to white powder) exothermic, "the powder gets warm".
- C36-14 (data) asks students to accept "stopped changing, fits equilibrium" and reject "reaction stopped" and "all the reactant used up".

## Diagram specs (for the diagram worker)

House style: like Science Lessons 17 and 18. Organic, gentle tints, rounded glassware; readable on a phone; text at least 12 px. Colour code: reactants in one tint (blue family) and products in another (coral family); keep them the same in every rev- diagram. Use generic labelled dots or blobs for A, B, C, D (A and B blue-tinted, C and D coral-tinted) rather than atoms. Copper sulfate: hydrated = blue, anhydrous = white or very pale grey with an outline. Temperature: warm = coral/amber, cool = pale blue.

Teaching frames:
- `rev-ab`: two soft-cornered boxes, "reactants" (A + B) and "products" (C + D), joined by a forward arrow along the top (A + B → C + D) and a backward arrow along the bottom (C + D → A + B), both drawn, so it reads as "both ways". Labels "reactants" and "products".
- `rev-arrow`: the equation "A + B ⇌ C + D" large, with the forward reaction shown as a curved arrow above labelled "forward reaction" (over A + B → C + D) and the backward arrow below labelled "backward reaction". The ⇌ symbol highlighted in amber.
- `rev-copper`: a test tube or evaporating dish with blue crystals on the left (label "blue hydrated copper sulfate") and white powder plus a few water droplets on the right (label "white anhydrous copper sulfate + water"). Top arrow labelled "heat" pointing left to right, bottom arrow labelled "add water" pointing right to left.
- `rev-eq-start`: a sealed flask full of blue A + B particles (about 8), no products; a long thick forward arrow labelled "forward: fast" and no backward arrow (or a faint dotted one labelled "backward: none yet").
- `rev-eq-middle`: the same flask with a mix, fewer reactants (about 4) and some products (about 4); forward arrow medium, backward arrow medium-short, labelled "forward: slowing" and "backward: speeding up".
- `rev-eq-balance`: the flask with a steady mix; two arrows of equal length, one forward and one backward, labelled "same rate"; a tag "equilibrium" in green. Optionally a small rate-time graph is NOT wanted (keep it simple).
- `rev-eq-amounts`: three sealed flasks side by side, all at equilibrium: mostly reactants; equal; mostly products. Each with the caption "equilibrium" and a shared caption "amounts not changing (not always equal)". Each flask has the equal double arrow.
- `rev-closed`: two containers side by side: left a sealed flask with a stopper ("closed system: nothing escapes or gets in", green tick) and right an open beaker with gas particles leaving the top ("open: gas escapes", red cross).
- `rev-direction`: two flask pictures with a signpost feel: left mostly products (coral) labelled "more products: going forwards →"; right mostly reactants (blue) labelled "more reactants: going backwards ←".
- `rev-conditions`: the equation "A + B ⇌ C + D" in the middle with three small icons around it: a thermometer "temperature", a pressure gauge "pressure", a beaker with dots "concentration". Caption "change the conditions to change the direction".
- `rev-ammonium`: "ammonium chloride ⇌ ammonia + hydrogen chloride" as a word equation with "heat" over the forward arrow and "cool" under the backward arrow; a test tube with white solid on the left, and gas puffs on the right; a small flame under the forward and an ice cube by the backward. Label "forwards when heated", "backwards when cooled".
- `rev-energy`: A + B ⇌ C + D with two labelled arrows: forward "endothermic: takes in heat" (arrow into the reaction from a thermometer/flame icon) and backward "exothermic: gives out heat" (arrow out).
- `rev-energy-equal`: two equal-height bars or two equal-length arrows: "energy taken in" and "energy given out", with an "=" between them. No numbers.
- `rev-copper-energy`: the copper sulfate picture as in `rev-copper`, with "endothermic (heat taken in)" on the heating arrow and "exothermic (heat given out, powder gets warm)" on the water arrow.

### C36-01 · choice · `understanding, priorKnowledge, diagnostic`
**Q:** Which of these changes can be undone by cooling?
- 0 Frying an egg · 1 Burning a piece of paper · **2 Melting wax into a liquid ✓** · 3 Baking a cake
- Hint: Which change makes something that turns back into what it was?
- Explanation: Melted wax turns back into solid wax when it cools. Frying, burning and baking make new substances that do not simply turn back.

### C36-02 · teach "What is a reversible reaction?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reactions can go backwards | In a reversible reaction, the products can turn back into the reactants. | reactants → products → reactants again | Most reactions go one way only. In some reactions, the products can react together to make the reactants again. These reactions can go backwards as well as forwards. They are called reversible reactions. | `rev-ab` |
| Forward and backward | The two directions have names, and the ⇌ symbol shows both. | A + B → C + D is forward; C + D → A + B is backward | This equation shows a reversible reaction: A + B ⇌ C + D. The symbol ⇌ means the reaction goes both ways. A and B reacting to make C and D is the forward reaction. C and D reacting to make A and B is the backward reaction. | `rev-arrow` |
| A real example | Heating blue crystals of copper sulfate gives a white powder and water. | heat → white + water; add water → blue | Blue hydrated copper sulfate crystals contain water. When you heat them, they lose their water and turn into a white powder called anhydrous copper sulfate. Add water to the white powder, and it turns blue again. | `rev-copper` |

### C36-03 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** What is a reversible reaction?
- **0 A reaction where the products can react to make the reactants again ✓** · 1 A reaction that only goes forwards until the reactants run out · 2 A reaction that has to be done twice before it works · 3 A reaction that gives out heat in both directions
- Hint: Can the products turn back into the reactants?
- Explanation: In a reversible reaction, the products can react together to make the reactants again. So the reaction can go both forwards and backwards.

### C36-04 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** In the reversible reaction P + Q ⇌ R + S, which is the backward reaction?
- 0 P and Q reacting to make R and S · 1 P and S reacting to make Q and R · 2 R changing into S · **3 R and S reacting to make P and Q ✓**
- Hint: The forward reaction reads left to right. Which way does the backward one read?
- Explanation: The forward reaction is P + Q → R + S. The backward reaction goes the other way: R + S → P + Q.

### C36-05 · teach "How does a reaction reach equilibrium?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starting out | At the start there are only reactants, so only the forward reaction happens. | many reactants → forward fast | Imagine a reversible reaction in a sealed flask that starts with only reactants. At first, the forward reaction is fast. There are no products yet, so the backward reaction cannot happen. | `rev-eq-start` |
| Rates change | The forward reaction slows down and the backward reaction speeds up. | reactants fall → forward slows; products rise → backward speeds up | As the reactants are used up, their concentrations fall. So the forward reaction slows down. As more products are made, the backward reaction speeds up. | `rev-eq-middle` |
| Equilibrium | At equilibrium the forward and backward reactions have exactly the same rate. | same rate → amounts stop changing | After a while, the forward and backward reactions go at exactly the same rate. Reactants are made as fast as they are used up. We say the reaction is at equilibrium. | `rev-eq-balance` |
| What equilibrium does not mean | It does not mean equal amounts. It means the amounts are not changing. | steady, not necessarily equal | Equilibrium does not mean there are equal amounts of reactants and products. There can be more products, more reactants, or equal amounts. It only means the amounts are not changing any more. | `rev-eq-amounts` |
| A closed system | Equilibrium is only reached if nothing can escape or get in. | closed = sealed | A reaction can only reach equilibrium in a closed system. In a closed system, none of the reactants or products can escape, and nothing else can get in. A sealed flask is a closed system. An open beaker is not. | `rev-closed` |

### C36-06 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** A reversible reaction is at equilibrium in a closed system. What is true about its two reactions?
- 0 The forward reaction has stopped · **1 They happen at exactly the same rate ✓** · 2 The backward reaction is faster · 3 The backward reaction has stopped
- Hint: Think about how fast the reactants are used up and made again.
- Explanation: At equilibrium, the forward and backward reactions carry on at exactly the same rate. Neither has stopped. That is why the amounts stay the same.

### C36-07 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** A reaction is at equilibrium. What does this tell you about the amounts of reactants and products?
- 0 They are exactly equal · 1 All the reactants have been used up · **2 They are not changing any more ✓** · 3 They are still changing quickly
- Hint: Does equilibrium mean equal amounts, or steady amounts?
- Explanation: Equilibrium means the amounts of reactants and products are not changing any more. They do not have to be equal. There can be more of either side.

### C36-08 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** Why must a reversible reaction be in a closed system to reach equilibrium?
- **0 Because none of the substances can escape and nothing else can get in ✓** · 1 Because a closed system makes the reaction go faster · 2 Because reversible reactions only work with solids · 3 Because it needs extra heat from outside
- Hint: What would happen to a gas made in an open beaker?
- Explanation: In a closed system, none of the reactants or products can escape, and nothing else can get in. In an open beaker a gas could escape, so the backward reaction could not keep up with the forward one.

### C36-09 · teach "Which way is it going?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall direction | If there are more products than reactants, the reaction is going forwards. | more products → forwards; more reactants → backwards | When a reaction is at equilibrium, one side may have more. If there are more products than reactants, we say the reaction is going in the forwards direction. If there are more reactants, it is going in the backwards direction. | `rev-direction` |
| Changing the conditions | Changing the conditions can change the direction. | temperature, pressure, concentration | You can change the direction of a reversible reaction by changing the conditions. The conditions are the temperature, the pressure and the concentration. Here we look at temperature. | `rev-conditions` |

### C36-10 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** Ammonium chloride breaks down into ammonia and hydrogen chloride when heated. What do you get more of when you heat it?
- 0 More ammonium chloride · **1 More ammonia and hydrogen chloride ✓** · 2 No change in the amounts · 3 Only ammonia
- Hint: Which direction does heating make this reaction go?
- Explanation: Heating makes the forward reaction go, so ammonium chloride breaks down. You get more ammonia and hydrogen chloride. Cooling would make the backward reaction go instead.

### C36-11 · teach "What happens to the energy?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opposite energy changes | If one direction takes in heat, the other gives out heat. | endothermic one way → exothermic the other | You met endothermic and exothermic reactions when you learned about energy changes. In a reversible reaction, the two directions have opposite energy changes. If the forward reaction takes in heat, the backward reaction gives out heat. | `rev-energy` |
| The same amount | The energy taken in one way equals the energy given out the other way. | energy in = energy out | The amount of energy is the same in both directions. | Copper sulfate again | Heating drives off the water (endothermic). ### C36-12 · choice · `understanding, guided, practice`
**Q:** A reversible reaction is endothermic in the forward direction. What is true of the backward reaction?
- 0 It is also endothermic, and takes in the same energy · 1 It is exothermic, and gives out more energy · 2 It does not involve any energy change · **3 It is exothermic, and gives out the same amount of energy ✓**
- Hint: If one direction takes in heat, what does the other do?
- Explanation: The two directions have opposite energy changes, so the backward reaction is exothermic. The energy given out is exactly the same amount as the energy taken in.

### C36-13 · choice · `application, independent, independent`
**Q:** Blue copper sulfate crystals turn white when heated. The white powder turns blue again when water is added. What does this show?
- 0 The change is not a chemical reaction · **1 The reaction is reversible ✓** · 2 The reaction only goes forwards · 3 The reaction needs no energy
- Hint: Can the change be undone?
- Explanation: The change goes one way when heated and the other way when water is added. A reaction that can go in both directions is a reversible reaction.

### C36-14 · choice · `dataInterpretation, independent, independent` · visual `rev-question-data`
**Q:** The table shows the mass of product in a closed flask over time. Which conclusion do the data support?
- 0 The reaction stopped at 40 s · 1 All the reactant was used up by 40 s · **2 The amount of product stopped changing from 40 s, which fits equilibrium ✓** · 3 The forward reaction was faster than the backward reaction at 60 s
- Hint: What does the mass do after 40 s, and what does that not tell you?
- Explanation: From 40 s the mass of product stays at 3.5 g. In a closed flask, this fits a reaction at equilibrium. The reaction has not stopped, because both reactions carry on. And the data cannot show that all the reactant was used up.

### C36-15 · choice · `application, independent, independent`
**Q:** At equilibrium, a mixture has much more product than reactant. In which direction is the reaction said to be going?
- 0 The backwards direction · 1 It is not going in either direction · **2 The forwards direction ✓** · 3 Both directions at once
- Hint: Which side has more?
- Explanation: More products than reactants means the reaction is going in the forwards direction. More reactants than products would mean the backwards direction.

### C36-16 · written · teacherOnly
**Q:** Explain how a reversible reaction reaches equilibrium in a closed system.
- Hint: Start with only reactants. Say what happens to the forward reaction, then the backward reaction, then what is true at the end.
- Model answer: At the start there are only reactants, and the forward reaction is fast. As the reactants are used up, their concentrations fall and the forward reaction slows down. As products build up, the backward reaction speeds up. Eventually both reactions go at exactly the same rate. The system is at equilibrium, and the amounts of reactants and products stop changing.
- Rubric:
  - The forward reaction slows down as the reactants are used up and their concentrations fall.
  - The backward reaction speeds up as more products are made.
  - Eventually the forward and backward reactions have exactly the same rate.
  - At equilibrium the amounts of reactants and products are not changing (in a closed system).
- Reject:
  - Saying the reaction stops at equilibrium.
  - Saying the amounts of reactants and products are equal.
  - Only saying that the reaction goes both ways, with no idea of rates changing.
