# Chemistry Lesson 53 storyboard: Potable water

Chapter C10, Using resources. Folder `chemistry/lesson-53`, id `C-RES-053-C`, skill `C-POTABLE-WATER`, prefix `potable-`. It owns potable versus pure, sources (surface and ground water), desalination and the treatment of fresh water. Testing purity and lab distillation belong to the next lesson; waste water to the one after.

Big idea: potable water is water that is safe to drink, not water that is pure. How it is made safe depends on where you live: fresh water is filtered and sterilised, and sea water is desalinated only when nothing else is available.

Flow note: meaning first (potable is not pure, three safety rules), then where the water comes from, then the problem case (sea water and desalination), then the everyday treatment of fresh water, ending with the whole route. Desalination comes before treatment because it explains why fresh water is preferred.

Sections:
1. Start here (C53-01): a clear stream is not automatically safe.
2. What is potable water? (C53-02–04): safe to drink; potable is not pure; salts, pH 6.5 to 8.5, microbes. Checks: the meaning; a true statement.
3. Where does it come from? (C53-05–07): rain and freshwater; surface and ground water; location decides; very dry countries. Checks: ground water example; why the south-east uses ground water.
4. What if there is only sea water? (C53-08–10): too much salt; distillation; reverse osmosis; cost. Checks: meaning of desalination; why it is not always used.
5. How is fresh water treated? (C53-11–13): why treat; wire mesh; filter beds; sterilisation; the whole route. Checks: filter beds; purpose of sterilising.
6. On your own (C53-14–17): choose and order treatments for a lake; source for a dry coastal country; potable but not pure; a written description of treatment.

Out of scope: reverse osmosis mechanism beyond "a membrane that only lets water molecules through"; other chemicals used in treatment; water hardness; costs in numbers.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 162 (scope only); AQA 8464 Chemistry 5.10.1.2. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The page's "pH between 6.5 and 8.5" is taught as given.
- Ozone is only named as a gas used for sterilising.

## Diagram specs
Soft, rounded, hand-drawn-feeling shapes, gentle tints, as in Lessons 17 and 18. Blue = water. Yellow for the Sun where it appears. Text in the SVG at least 12px. All "original schematic, not to scale".

- `potable-meaning`: a glass of clear water with a tick and the label "potable: safe to drink", and a small person drinking. No chemistry labels.
- `potable-notpure`: two glasses side by side. Left "pure water: only H₂O molecules" (plain blue dots all the same). Right "potable water: H₂O plus safe dissolved substances" (blue dots with a few small other-coloured dots). Both ticked "safe" is not needed; keep labels as given.
- `potable-rules`: a checklist card with three rows with icons: "low levels of dissolved salts", "pH between 6.5 and 8.5" (a small pH strip with the range highlighted), "no harmful microbes".
- `potable-rain`: a cloud raining onto land, labelled "rainwater: freshwater, little dissolved in it".
- `potable-sources`: the same landscape: a lake, a river and a reservoir highlighted and labelled "surface water"; rock layers underground with water trapped in them labelled "ground water".
- `potable-uk`: a simple map-like outline of the UK (soft blob, no borders) with the south-east marked and a note "warmer: surface water dries up first, so more ground water". Small tap-water drop icons only; no data.
- `potable-dry`: a dry landscape with cracked ground and a dry river bed, and the sea beside it, arrow "sea water used instead".
- `potable-salty`: a beaker of sea water with salt particles dotted through it, label "too much dissolved salt: not potable. Desalination removes it".
- `potable-distill`: a simple distillation flow: boil sea water in a flask, steam, condense in a cooled tube, collect fresh water; salt left behind labelled. Few labels.
- `potable-membrane`: a membrane across the middle of a tank; salty water on one side, water molecules (blue) passing through to the other side, salt (other colour) and bigger molecules blocked. Labels "membrane" and "salty water", "fresh water".
- `potable-cost`: a big energy symbol (a lightning bolt in yellow) over a purse or £ sign, labelled "lots of energy = expensive", with two small pictures "distillation" and "reverse osmosis".
- `potable-treat`: a simple flow strip of two boxes, "Filtration" then "Sterilisation", between a river and a tap.
- `potable-mesh`: a wire mesh with water flowing through and twigs stopped on top. Label "wire mesh stops large things".
- `potable-beds`: a tank with layers of sand and gravel, grains catching tiny solid bits; labels "filter bed: sand and gravel".
- `potable-sterilise`: a beaker of water with microbes (small bugs) being crossed out, and three small icons labelled "chlorine gas", "ozone", "ultraviolet light".
- `potable-route`: the whole route: rain, surface/ground water, wire mesh, filter beds, sterilisation, tap; a side branch "sea water: desalination" joins before the mesh. Numbered steps.
- `potable-q-flow` (question, assessment view): a lake on the left with twigs and bugs in the water, a tap on the right, and three empty numbered boxes between them with no treatment names. Description: "A lake containing twigs and microbes, three unlabelled stages and a tap."
