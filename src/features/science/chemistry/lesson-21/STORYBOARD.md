# Chemistry Lesson 21 storyboard — Concentration of solutions

Chapter C3, Quantitative chemistry. Folder `chemistry/lesson-21`, id `C-QNT-021-C`, skill `C-CONCENTRATION`. Visuals: `components/ConcentrationVisuals.tsx` (focus prefix `conc-`).

Big idea: concentration is how much solute is dissolved in a certain volume of solution. In g/dm³ it is the mass of solute (g) divided by the volume of solution (dm³). Rearranged, mass = concentration × volume. Volumes in cm³ are divided by 1000 to give dm³.

Flow note: picture first (crowded particles), then the unit, then the formula, then the rearrangement.
1. **What is concentration?** Same volume, more solute (more crowded) → same solute, more water (more spread out) → g/dm³ and the 1 dm³ cube (= 1000 cm³). Checks: which beaker is the most concentrated (assessment visual); which unit is a concentration.
2. **How do you calculate it?** The formula with unit tags → cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³ (bar of 1000 cm³) → three steps (24 g in 400 cm³ = 60 g/dm³). Worked: 40 g in 0.50 dm³ = 80 g/dm³. Practice: 9 g in 300 cm³ = 30 g/dm³; converting 400 cm³.
3. **How do you find the mass?** Multiply both sides by the volume → formula triangle → three steps (30 g/dm³, 200 cm³ = 6 g). Worked: 50 g/dm³ × 0.30 dm³ = 15 g. Practice: 20 g/dm³, 250 cm³ = 5 g; choose the correct rearrangement.
4. **On your own**: 6 g in 400 cm³ (15 g/dm³); 12 g/dm³ in 500 cm³ (6 g); spot the mistake in a working (÷100 instead of ÷1000, assessment view, numbered lines); data table of four solutions read without over-claiming (S = 60 g/dm³ is highest; R holds more than P but has the same concentration); written task (30 g is not always more concentrated than 10 g).
