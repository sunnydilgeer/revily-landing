# Lesson 12 storyboard — Circulatory system: blood vessels

Big idea: blood leaves the heart in arteries, swaps substances with cells in capillaries, and comes back in veins. Each vessel's walls suit the pressure of the blood inside it.

Anchor route: follow blood from the aorta to a working leg-muscle cell and back — aorta → artery → capillary beside a muscle cell → vein → vena cava. Each vessel is taught once, in the order the blood meets it.

Why this order:
- Arteries come first because blood meets them first, and "high pressure" (from the heartbeat in Lesson 11) explains their thick walls.
- Capillaries come next, at the muscle cell. Oxygen and glucose going in, then carbon dioxide coming out, are both taught before any question asks about them.
- Veins come after the capillaries because the blood has lost most of its pressure by then. Low pressure explains thinner walls, a wide lumen and valves. "Lumen" is first needed here, so it is taught here.
- Rate of blood flow comes last. It starts with a reason (a working muscle needs more blood each minute), then the worked example, then practice with new numbers, then reuse in "On your own".

Scientific boundaries: an artery or vein is named by direction, not oxygen — the pulmonary artery is still an artery because it carries blood away from the heart. Vessel drawings are simplified original schematics, not to scale and not micrographs. Flow-rate numbers are invented illustrative values. Out of scope: arterioles, venules and tissue fluid by name, blood pressure values, how muscles squeeze veins.
