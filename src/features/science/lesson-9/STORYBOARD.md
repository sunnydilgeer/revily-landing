# Lesson 9 storyboard — Digestion and food tests

Big idea: enzymes break a meal into small soluble molecules, which are absorbed into the blood. A food test shows what a meal contained in the first place.

Anchor and route: one cheese sandwich. We break it down (which enzymes, and where), finish it in the small intestine (bile, then absorption into the blood), then test a sample of it in the lab. Digestion and food tests are one story, not two parts.

Why this order: the enzyme groups and their products come first, because the sites, bile and the written task all use them. Enzyme sites follow the meal down the gut, and they introduce stomach acid before bile has to neutralise it. Absorption comes straight after bile, because it needs the finished products (sugars, amino acids, glycerol and fatty acids). It is also the first step of the lessons 9→12 transport story. The food tests come last and reuse the sandwich. "Observation before conclusion" is taught (B9-14 f4) before any question asks about it (B9-15, B9-17).

Practical boundary: "This lesson prepares you for required practical 3. It does not replace doing it." No home experiment is suggested. Heating, ethanol and reagents stay behind school supervision, eye protection and the school risk assessment. The ethanol-and-water method from the current AQA handbook is the main lipid test. Sudan III is given as an alternative school method, and learners follow the method named by the teacher or question.

Ownership: this lesson owns bile (made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, neutralises stomach acid, emulsifies fat). Out of scope: enzyme names beyond amylase, villi detail (Lesson 6), how absorbed molecules are used in detail, and the heart and vessels (Lessons 11–12).
