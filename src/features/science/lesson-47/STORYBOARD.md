# Lesson 52 storyboard — Abiotic and biotic factors, and adaptations

Second lesson of chapter B7, Ecology. Folder `lesson-47`, id `B-ECO-047-B`, skill `B-ECO-FACTORS`.

Big idea: non-living (abiotic) and living (biotic) factors in an environment affect population sizes, and organisms survive where they live because of adaptations: structural, behavioural and functional.

Flow note: the lesson moves from the environment to the organism.
1. **What non-living things matter?** One field-and-pond scene with each abiotic factor labelled, then grouped: light, temperature, moisture and wind; the soil (pH and mineral content); gases (carbon dioxide for plants, oxygen in water). It ends with one change spreading: fewer mineral ions → less grass → less food for rabbits.
2. **What living things matter?** One woodland scene: food, new predators, new pathogens and competition, ending with the spec example of grey squirrels outcompeting red squirrels.
3. **Built to survive.** Once learners know what an environment throws at organisms, adaptations answer "how do they cope?": structural (Arctic fox; large surface area to lose heat in hot places), behavioural (swallows), functional (desert animals saving water), then extremophiles.

Sections:
1. Start here (B47-01): which of four things is not living (prior knowledge for "abiotic").
2. What non-living things matter? (B47-02–04): checks which is an abiotic factor; a dry spell in a field.
3. What living things matter? (B47-05–07): checks which is a biotic factor; why red squirrels have declined.
4. Built to survive (B47-08–10): checks the type of adaptation of the Arctic fox's fur; what bacteria at hot vents are called.
5. On your own (B47-11–14): a desert gerbil's burrow (behavioural); a numbered fennec fox, which feature loses heat (assessment view hides names); an invented red-squirrel count read without over-claiming; teacher-reviewed written answer on warm water, fish and herons.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; communities, photosynthesis and pathogens are referred to by topic; British spelling.

Out of scope: detailed pathogen biology; metabolism beyond one clause; surface area to volume ratio calculations; named extremophile species; the book's cartoons, the penguin question and the camel drawing.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide pages 80–81 (scope only); AQA 8464 sections 4.7.1.2, 4.7.1.3 and 4.7.1.4. The spec's own examples (red and grey squirrels, Arctic fox) are used; the field and pond, wood, gerbil, fennec fox, squirrel data, lake scenario, all questions and all wording are original. ## States in full

### B47-01 · choice · `understanding, priorKnowledge, diagnostic`
**Q:** Which of these is not a living thing?
- 0 A fungus · 1 An earthworm · **2 Rain ✓** · 3 Grass
- Hint: Living things feed, grow and reproduce.
- Explanation: A fungus, an earthworm and grass are all living organisms. Rain is water falling from clouds, so it is not living.

### B47-02 · teach "What non-living things matter?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-living factors | Abiotic factors are the non-living parts of an environment. | abiotic = non-living | You met ecosystems when you learned about communities. The non-living parts of an ecosystem can change, and this affects the living things. The non-living factors in an environment are called abiotic factors. | `eco-abiotic-all` |
| Light, warmth, water and wind | Light, temperature, moisture and wind are abiotic factors. | bright? warm? wet? windy? | How bright the light is, is called the light intensity. Temperature and moisture level are abiotic factors too. So are how strong the wind is and which way it blows. | `eco-abiotic-weather` |
| The soil | Soil pH and mineral content are abiotic factors. | pH, mineral ions | Plants take in mineral ions from the soil, so the mineral content of the soil matters. Soil pH matters too. It tells you how acidic or alkaline the soil is. | `eco-abiotic-soil` |
| Gases | Carbon dioxide and oxygen levels are abiotic factors. | CO₂ for plants, O₂ for water animals | Plants need carbon dioxide for photosynthesis, so its level is an abiotic factor for plants. Animals that live in water need the oxygen dissolved in it. So the oxygen level matters for them. | `eco-abiotic-gases` |
| A change spreads | A change in one abiotic factor can affect many species. | soil ↓ → grass ↓ → rabbits ↓ | Suppose the mineral content of the soil falls. The grass grows less well, so there is less of it. The rabbits that eat the grass then have less food, so their population may fall too. | `eco-abiotic-chain` |

### B47-03 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** Which of these is an abiotic factor?
- 0 A new predator · **1 Soil pH ✓** · 2 A new pathogen · 3 The amount of food
- Hint: Abiotic means non-living.
- Explanation: Predators, pathogens and food are all living factors. Soil pH is non-living, so it is an abiotic factor.

### B47-04 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** A long dry spell makes the soil in a field much drier. What is most likely to happen?
- **0 Plants grow less well, so animals that eat them have less food ✓** · 1 Plants grow more, so animals that eat them have more food · 2 Nothing changes, because the soil is not alive
- Hint: What do plants take in from the soil?
- Explanation: Moisture is an abiotic factor, and plants need water from the soil. So the plants grow less well, and animals that eat them have less food.

### B47-05 · teach "What living things matter?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Living factors | Biotic factors are the living parts of an environment. | biotic = living | Living things affect each other too. The living factors in an environment are called biotic factors. A change in one can change the population size of a species, and of the species that depend on it. | `eco-biotic-all` |
| Food | Less food means a smaller population. | less food → fewer survive | If less food is available, fewer animals survive and breed. So the population size goes down. In a wood, a poor year for nuts and acorns means less food for squirrels. | `eco-biotic-food` |
| New predators | A new predator can reduce the animals it eats. | new predator → prey ↓ | An animal that hunts and kills other animals for food is called a predator. If a new predator arrives in a habitat, the population of the animals it eats may go down. | `eco-biotic-predators` |
| New pathogens | A new disease can spread quickly. | new pathogen → population ↓ | Pathogens are microorganisms that cause disease. You met them when you learned about infectious diseases. A new pathogen can spread quickly, so the population of the species it infects may fall fast. | `eco-biotic-pathogens` |
| Competition between species | One species can outcompete another. | grey wins food and shelter → red ↓ | Red and grey squirrels live in the same woods and eat the same food. Grey squirrels are better at getting food and shelter, so we say they outcompete red squirrels. In many places, the red squirrel population has fallen. | `eco-biotic-competition` |

### B47-06 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** Which of these is a biotic factor?
- 0 Temperature · 1 Wind direction · 2 Light intensity · **3 A new pathogen ✓**
- Hint: Biotic means living.
- Explanation: Temperature, wind direction and light intensity are non-living. A pathogen is a living microorganism, so it is a biotic factor.

### B47-07 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** Why has the number of red squirrels fallen in many woods where grey squirrels live?
- **0 Grey squirrels outcompete them for food and shelter ✓** · 1 Red squirrels need more light than grey squirrels · 2 Red squirrels hunt grey squirrels
- Hint: Both species eat the same food.
- Explanation: Red and grey squirrels need the same food and shelter. Grey squirrels are better at getting them, so they outcompete red squirrels.

### B47-08 · teach "Built to survive"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptations | Special features help organisms survive where they live. | feature → suits its environment | Every organism, including microorganisms, has features that help it survive in its environment. These special features are called adaptations. There are three types: structural, behavioural and functional. | `eco-adapt-all` |
| Structural | Body shape and colour can be adaptations. | a feature of the body | Structural adaptations are features of an organism’s body, such as its shape or colour. The Arctic fox has white fur in winter, so it is hard to see against the snow. This helps it hide from predators and creep up on prey. Animals in hot places often have a large surface area compared with their volume, which helps them lose heat. | `eco-adapt-structural` |
| Behavioural | The way an organism behaves can be an adaptation. | what it does | Behavioural adaptations are ways that an organism behaves. Many swallows fly thousands of kilometres to warmer places before winter. This helps them avoid the cold and find food. | `eco-adapt-behavioural` |
| Functional | Processes inside the body can be adaptations. | what happens inside the body | Functional adaptations are things that happen inside the body, linked to processes such as metabolism. Many desert animals lose very little water. They make very little sweat, and only small amounts of concentrated urine. | `eco-adapt-functional` |
| Extremophiles | Some organisms live in extreme conditions. | very hot, very salty, high pressure | Some organisms, mostly microorganisms, live where most living things could not survive. Organisms like this are called extremophiles. Some live in very hot water near volcanic vents. Others live in very salty lakes, or at high pressure deep in the sea. | `eco-adapt-extreme` |

### B47-09 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** The Arctic fox has white fur in winter. What type of adaptation is this?
- 0 Behavioural · 1 Functional · **2 Structural ✓**
- Hint: Is it something it does, something inside its body, or a feature of its body?
- Explanation: Fur colour is part of the body that you can see. A feature of the body, such as its colour, is a structural adaptation.

### B47-10 · choice · `understanding, guided`
**Q:** Some bacteria live in very hot water near volcanic vents. What are organisms like this called?
- **0 Extremophiles ✓** · 1 Predators · 2 Producers · 3 Pathogens
- Hint: The conditions are extreme.
- Explanation: Very hot water is an extreme condition, where most living things could not survive. Organisms that live in extreme conditions are called extremophiles.

### B47-11 · choice · `application, independent`
**Q:** A desert gerbil stays in its cool burrow by day and comes out at night. What type of adaptation is this?
- 0 Structural · **1 Behavioural ✓** · 2 Functional · 3 Abiotic
- Hint: Is this a body feature, a process inside it, or something it does?
- Explanation: Staying in a burrow by day is something the gerbil does. A way of behaving that helps it survive is a behavioural adaptation.

### B47-12 · choice · `application, independent` · question diagram `eco-fennec-question`
**Q:** The fennec fox lives in hot deserts. Which numbered feature helps it lose heat?
- 0 Feature 1 · 1 Feature 2 · **2 Feature 3 ✓**
- Hint: Heat is lost from a surface. Which feature has a large surface?
- Explanation: Feature 1 is its sandy fur, which gives camouflage. Feature 2 is its furry soles, which protect its feet from hot sand. Feature 3 is its very large ears, which have a large surface area, so they help it lose heat.

### B47-13 · choice · `dataInterpretation, independent` · question diagram `eco-squirrel-data`
**Q:** The graph shows the red squirrels counted in one wood. Grey squirrels arrived in year 3. Which conclusion fits?
- 0 Grey squirrels killed all the red squirrels · **1 In this wood, red squirrel numbers fell after grey squirrels arrived ✓** · 2 Red squirrels will soon be extinct everywhere · 3 Red squirrel numbers rose after year 3
- Hint: Only say what this one wood shows.
- Explanation: The count fell from 47 in year 3 to 16 in year 8, but red squirrels were still there. So in this wood, red squirrel numbers fell after grey squirrels arrived. One wood cannot show what happens everywhere.

### B47-14 · written · `explanation, transfer`
**Q:** Warmer water in a lake holds less oxygen. Explain how this could affect the fish and the herons that eat them.
- Hint: Start with the abiotic change, then follow it to the fish, then to the herons.
- Model answer: The oxygen level in water is an abiotic factor. Fish need oxygen dissolved in the water, so with less of it, fewer fish survive and the fish population may fall. The herons then have less food, so the heron population may fall too.
- Teacher-reviewed (not auto-marked). Rubric points:
  - The oxygen level in the water is an abiotic factor that fish depend on.
  - With less oxygen, fewer fish survive, so the fish population may fall.
  - Herons then have less food, so their population may fall too.
- Reject:
  - Saying fish take in carbon dioxide from the water to survive.
  - Saying the herons need oxygen from the water.
  - Saying the warmer water is a biotic factor.
