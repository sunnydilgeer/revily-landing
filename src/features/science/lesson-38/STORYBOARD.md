# Lesson 43 storyboard — Reproduction and meiosis (folder lesson-38)

Second lesson of chapter B6.

Big idea: sexual reproduction mixes genes from two parents, so gametes must carry half a set; meiosis makes four genetically different gametes, and after fertilisation mitosis builds the embryo.

Flow note: the lesson answers "why half?" before "how?".
1. **Two ways to reproduce.** Two parents, gametes, half a set (23), fertilisation (23 + 23 = 46), then the contrast: one parent, mitosis, clones. One gamete drawing builds frame by frame; asexual reproduction gets its own drawing.
2. **How are gametes made?** Meiosis on one left-to-right tree with two chromosome pairs (rose from the mother, blue from the father): where it happens, pairs, copying (X shapes), first division, second division, four gametes.
3. **From one cell to an embryo.** Fertilisation, mitosis again and again, differentiation into specialised cells.

Why this order: learners need the reason for halving (fertilisation) before the mechanism. Asexual reproduction sits in the first section because it is the contrast that defines "sexual". Mitosis and stem cells are referred to by topic only. The next lesson uses the 23 pairs to explain sex chromosomes.

1. Start here (B38-01): what mitosis makes (links to cell division).
2. Two ways to reproduce (B38-02–05): two parents → sex cells → half a set → fertilisation → one parent → clones. Checks: chromosomes in an egg cell; why asexual offspring are identical; why sexual offspring vary.
3. How are gametes made? (B38-06–08): where it happens → pairs → copy the DNA → first division → second division → four gametes. Checks: what one cell makes; where meiosis happens.
4. From one cell to an embryo (B38-09–11): a new cell → many cells → specialised cells. Checks: which division grows the embryo; what differentiation is.
5. On your own (B38-12–16): a strawberry runner; sperm chromosomes in a dog (78 → 39); a chromosome-number table read without over-claiming; the numbered meiosis diagram (which stage is the gametes); teacher-reviewed written answer.

Accuracy notes: only two pairs (meiosis) and three chromosomes per gamete (fertilisation) are drawn, and each drawing says so. The meiosis drawing gives the chromatids small swapped tips so that all four gametes really are different, as the specification states; the swap itself (crossing over) is not named or taught.

Out of scope: naming the phases of meiosis, crossing over, independent assortment by name, advantages and disadvantages of each type of reproduction (separate Biology only), the stem-cell detail beyond one link.

Source boundary: supplied pages 62–63 used for scope only; AQA 8464 sections 4.6.1.1 and 4.6.1.2. The strawberry, dog and chromosome table examples, all questions and all diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

## States in full

### B38-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** A body cell divides by mitosis. What are the two new cells like?
- **0 Genetically identical to the parent cell ✓** · 1 Each has half the chromosomes · 2 Genetically different from each other · 3 Each is a sex cell
- Hint: You met mitosis when you learned how cells divide for growth and repair.
- Explanation: In mitosis, the chromosomes are copied and shared out equally. So the two new cells are genetically identical to the parent cell.

### B38-02 · teach "Two ways to reproduce"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two parents | In sexual reproduction, genes from two parents are mixed. | two parents → one mix of genes | Most animals and plants reproduce with two parents, a male and a female. The offspring get a mixture of genes from both parents. This is called sexual reproduction. | `inherit-repro-sexual` |
| Sex cells | Each parent makes sex cells called gametes. | gametes: sperm and egg | Each parent makes special sex cells. In animals, the male makes sperm cells and the female makes egg cells. In flowering plants, the sex cells are pollen and egg cells. Sex cells are called gametes. | `inherit-repro-gametes` |
| Half a set | Each gamete has half the number of chromosomes. | body cell 46 → gamete 23 | A normal human body cell has 46 chromosomes. Each gamete has only half as many, so it has 23. Gametes are made by a special kind of cell division called meiosis. | `inherit-repro-half` |
| Fertilisation | A sperm and an egg join, making a full set again. | 23 + 23 = 46 | A sperm cell and an egg cell join together. The new cell has 23 + 23 = 46 chromosomes, the full number again. The joining of a male and a female gamete is called fertilisation. The new mix of genes makes each offspring different from its parents. | `inherit-repro-fertilise` |
| One parent | Asexual reproduction needs only one parent. | one parent, mitosis, no mixing | Some organisms can reproduce with just one parent. The parent cell divides in two by mitosis. There are no gametes, so no genes are mixed. This is called asexual reproduction. | `inherit-repro-asexual` |
| Clones | Asexual offspring are genetically identical to the parent. | same genes = clone | Each new cell has exactly the same genes as its parent cell. ### B38-03 · choice
**Q:** A normal human body cell has 46 chromosomes. How many chromosomes are in a human egg cell?
- 0 46 · 1 92 · **2 23 ✓** · 3 12
- Hint: A gamete has half the normal number.
- Explanation: Gametes have half the number of chromosomes of a body cell. Half of 46 is 23.

### B38-04 · choice
**Q:** Why is asexual offspring genetically identical to its parent?
- 0 Two gametes fuse and mix their genes · **1 The parent divides by mitosis, so no genes are mixed ✓** · 2 Each offspring gets half the parent’s chromosomes · 3 The offspring are made by meiosis
- Hint: How many parents are there, and which kind of division happens?
- Explanation: In asexual reproduction there are no gametes and no fertilisation. The parent divides by mitosis, so the offspring has exactly the same genes: it is a clone.

### B38-05 · choice
**Q:** Why do offspring from sexual reproduction show variation?
- 0 They are clones of one parent · 1 They are made by mitosis only · **2 They get a mixture of genes from two parents ✓** · 3 Their cells have no chromosomes
- Hint: Where do the offspring’s genes come from?
- Explanation: A sperm and an egg from two different parents fuse at fertilisation. The offspring gets a mixture of genes from both parents, so there is variation.

### B38-06 · teach "How are gametes made?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Where it happens | In humans, meiosis happens only in the ovaries and testes. | reproductive organs only | Meiosis is the cell division that makes gametes. In humans, it only happens in the reproductive organs. These are the ovaries in females and the testes in males. | `inherit-meiosis-where` |
| Pairs | The starting cell has its chromosomes in pairs. | one from the mother, one from the father | The cell that starts meiosis has its chromosomes in pairs. | Copy the DNA | First, the DNA in the cell is copied. | copy → X shapes | First, the cell copies all of its DNA. Each chromosome now has two identical copies joined in the middle. This makes X-shaped chromosomes. | `inherit-meiosis-copy` |
| First division | The cell divides, and each new cell gets half the chromosomes. | split up the pairs | Next, the cell divides into two. The pairs are split up, so each new cell gets one chromosome from each pair. So each new cell has half the number of chromosomes. | `inherit-meiosis-first` |
| Second division | Each cell divides again, and the X shapes are pulled apart. | split up the X shapes | Then each of the two new cells divides again. This time the X-shaped chromosomes are pulled apart. One copy of each goes into each new cell. | `inherit-meiosis-second` |

### B38-07 · choice
**Q:** One cell divides by meiosis. What does it make?
- 0 Two cells, identical to each other · **1 Four gametes, all genetically different ✓** · 2 Four gametes, all genetically identical · 3 One cell with the full number of chromosomes
- Hint: How many times does the cell divide in meiosis?
- Explanation: The cell divides twice, so one cell makes four. Each gamete gets a different mix of chromosomes, so all four are genetically different.

### B38-08 · choice
**Q:** In humans, where does meiosis happen?
- **0 Only in the ovaries and testes ✓** · 1 In every body cell · 2 In the skin, to repair cuts · 3 In the blood
- Hint: Meiosis makes gametes. Where are gametes made?
- Explanation: Body cells divide by mitosis for growth and repair. Meiosis happens only in the reproductive organs: the ovaries and testes.

### B38-10 · choice
**Q:** After fertilisation, which type of cell division makes the embryo grow?
- 0 Meiosis · **1 Mitosis ✓** · 2 Fertilisation · 3 Differentiation
- Hint: Should the new cells be identical, or have half the chromosomes?
- Explanation: Meiosis only makes gametes, and differentiation is cells becoming specialised. The fertilised cell divides again and again by mitosis to form the embryo.

### B38-11 · choice
**Q:** What happens when the cells of an embryo differentiate?
- **0 They become specialised to do different jobs ✓** · 1 They halve their chromosomes · 2 They fuse with more gametes · 3 They turn back into a single cell
- Hint: Think of nerve cells and muscle cells.
- Explanation: Early embryo cells are all alike. As they differentiate, they become specialised cells that do different jobs.

### B38-12 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** A strawberry plant grows a side shoot called a runner. The runner grows into a new plant, with no gametes involved. What is the new plant like?
- 0 A mixture of genes from two parents · 1 It has half the parent’s chromosomes · 2 It is genetically different from the parent · **3 Genetically identical to the parent: a clone ✓**
- Hint: No gametes means which type of reproduction?
- Explanation: No gametes means no fertilisation and no mixing of genes, so this is asexual reproduction. The new plant is made by mitosis, so it is genetically identical to its parent: a clone.

### B38-16 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Explain why a baby is not genetically identical to either of its parents.
- Hint: Think about how gametes are made, then what happens at fertilisation.
- Model answer: Each parent makes gametes by meiosis. Each gamete has half the number of chromosomes, 23 in humans, and every gamete is genetically different. At fertilisation, a sperm from the father and an egg from the mother fuse. So the baby gets a mixture of genes from both parents, which causes variation.
- Marking points: Gametes are made by meiosis. · Each gamete has half the number of chromosomes (23 in humans). · The gametes are genetically different from each other. · At fertilisation a sperm and an egg fuse, so the baby gets genes from both parents. · This mixture of genes causes variation.
- Common errors: Saying gametes are made by mitosis. · Saying each gamete has 46 chromosomes, or that the baby has 92. · Saying the baby is a clone of one parent.
