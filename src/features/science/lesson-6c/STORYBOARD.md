# Lesson 6c storyboard — Active transport and exchange surfaces

Big idea: diffusion and osmosis only go "downhill", from more to less. Cells can also move things "uphill", but that costs energy. Big organisms have too little outside surface for their size, so they grow special exchange surfaces that are large, thin and well supplied.

Anchor / route: downhill (diffusion and osmosis, from the osmosis practical) → uphill in one root hair cell, then the gut → name the process with three questions → why a big body has a problem (SA:V) → work SA:V out → what a good exchange surface looks like.

Why this order:
- Active transport is taught against the downhill processes learners already know, so "against the gradient" has something to be against. "Concentration gradient" is recapped in B6-13 frame 1 before frame 3 uses it.
- Mineral ions are named (B6-13 frame 2) before "moving uphill" uses them; energy from respiration (frame 4) comes before the gut example reuses it.
- The three-process comparison comes once, only after all three processes are known (B6-15), so B6-17 and B6-61 can mix them.
- SA:V is explained with the 1 cm cube (B6-18) before the worked 2 cm cube (B6-19), so the worked example shows a new number, not a repeat. The practice (B6-63) uses a 6 cm cube; "On your own" uses a 3 cm cube.
- "Exchange surface" is defined at the end of the size section (B6-18 frame 4), so the next section can ask what makes one good.
- Ventilation and stomata are each defined in their own frame (B6-21 frames 3 and 4) before any question uses them.

Hand-offs:
- Back: "In the osmosis practical, water moved into or out of the potato by itself." (B6-13 frame 1), and the Start here question.
- On: "Next, you will see how cells build up into tissues, organs and organ systems." (B6-21 final frame).

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first, then "is called …". Alveoli appear in one clause only (B6-21 frame 5) and are never tested; the lungs lesson owns them.

Caveats kept: diffusion and osmosis need no energy from respiration; blood carries absorbed sugar to cells for respiration; SA:V ratios use the given cm measurements; plants have no blood, so no claim that every exchange surface has a blood supply; ventilation applies to gas surfaces; the dye data are invented and one test cannot show what happens in every animal.

Out of scope: carrier proteins, calculating SA:V for non-cube shapes, the detail of alveoli and breathing, xylem and phloem.
