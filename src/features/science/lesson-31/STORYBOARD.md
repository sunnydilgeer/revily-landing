# Lesson 36 storyboard — The nervous system and reflexes (folder lesson-31, v1)

Second lesson of chapter B5, Homeostasis and response.

Big idea: receptors detect a stimulus, sensory neurones carry electrical impulses to the CNS, the CNS coordinates a response and motor neurones carry impulses to effectors; impulses cross synapses as chemicals, and reflexes use a relay neurone and skip the thinking brain, so they are fast.

Flow note: the lesson builds one picture of the nervous system, then uses it twice.
1. **What is the nervous system?** On one body: the CNS, receptors, neurones, sensory neurones, motor neurones and effectors, one per frame.
2. **From stimulus to response.** A goalkeeper sees a ball and saves it, traced step by step along a seven-tile chain.
3. **Crossing the gap.** A close-up of one synapse: the gap, chemicals released, diffusion across, a new impulse.
4. **Why are reflexes fast?** A rose thorn pricks a finger: sensory neurone, relay neurone in the spinal cord, motor neurone, arm muscle; then the whole reflex arc.

Why this order: the parts must be named before a pathway can be traced. The goalkeeper's thinking response comes before the reflex so the difference (no thinking, a relay neurone in the spinal cord) is clear. Synapses come before reflexes because the reflex frames mention impulses crossing synapses. The lesson starts from the three parts of a control system met in the homeostasis lesson.

1. Start here (B31-01): which part of a control system detects a stimulus (links to homeostasis).
2. What is the nervous system? (B31-02–04): CNS → receptors → neurones → sensory neurones → motor neurones → effectors. Checks: numbered body (which two numbers are the CNS); which neurones carry impulses to a muscle.
3. From stimulus to response (B31-05–07): stimulus and receptor → sensory neurone → CNS decides → motor neurone and effectors → put together. Checks: the right order; the effectors in the save.
4. Crossing the gap (B31-08–10): a tiny gap → chemicals released → diffusion across → a new impulse. Checks: how an impulse crosses; what a synapse is.
5. Why are reflexes fast? (B31-11–13): reflexes → into the spinal cord → relay neurone → out to the muscle → the reflex arc. Checks: what a relay neurone does; why reflexes are quick.
6. On your own (B31-14–18): a cyclist hears a horn (which part detects); a numbered reflex arc in a new order (which is the relay neurone); an invented response-time chart read without over-claiming; spot the error in a student's sentence; teacher-reviewed written answer (stepping on a sharp stone).

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; British spelling; "thinking part of the brain" is used for the conscious brain, and "a part of the brain you are not aware of" for the unconscious part.

Out of scope: the structure of a neurone in detail (met in the specialised cells lesson), myelin, how an impulse travels, named neurotransmitters, the brain's regions and the eye (separate Biology only), and hormones in detail (a later lesson).

## States in full

### B31-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** In a control system, which part detects a stimulus?
- 0 An effector · **1 A receptor ✓** · 2 A coordination centre
- Hint: You met the three parts when you learned about homeostasis. Which one detects?
- Explanation: Receptors detect a stimulus. Coordination centres organise a response, and effectors carry it out.

### B31-02 · teach "What is the nervous system?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The CNS | Your brain and spinal cord form the CNS. | CNS = brain + spinal cord | Your nervous system lets you react to your surroundings and coordinates your behaviour. At its centre are your brain and your spinal cord. Together they are called the central nervous system, or CNS. | `nerve-ns-cns` |
| Receptors | Different receptors detect different stimuli. | eyes → light; ears → sound | Receptors detect stimuli, as you saw when you learned about homeostasis. Different receptors detect different stimuli. Receptors in your eyes detect light, and receptors in your ears detect sound. Receptors in your skin detect touch. | `nerve-ns-receptors` |
| Neurones | Nerve cells carry electrical impulses. | neurone = nerve cell | Long, thin nerve cells connect the CNS to the rest of your body. Information travels along them as electrical impulses. Nerve cells are called neurones. | `nerve-ns-neurones` |
| Sensory neurones | Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS. | sensory: receptor → CNS | Some neurones carry impulses from receptors to the CNS. These are called sensory neurones. | `nerve-ns-sensory` |
| Motor neurones | Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS. | motor: CNS → effector | Other neurones carry impulses from the CNS out to effectors. These are called motor neurones. | `nerve-ns-motor` |
| Effectors | Muscles and glands are effectors. | muscles contract; glands release hormones | Effectors respond to impulses and bring about a change. Muscles respond by contracting, which means getting shorter. Glands respond by releasing hormones. | `nerve-ns-effectors` |

### B31-03 · choice · question diagram `nerve-ns-question` (assessment version hides the answer)

- 0 1 and 2 · **1 2 and 4 ✓** · 2 3 and 4 · 3 1 and 3
- Hint: The CNS is in the head and runs down the back.
- Explanation: Number 1 is an eye, which contains receptors, and number 3 is a muscle, an effector. Numbers 2 and 4 are the brain and the spinal cord, which make up the CNS.

### B31-04 · choice
**Q:** Which neurones carry impulses from the CNS to a muscle?
- 0 Sensory neurones · 1 Receptor cells · **2 Motor neurones ✓**
- Hint: Which neurones carry impulses away from the CNS?
- Explanation: Sensory neurones carry impulses from receptors to the CNS. Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors, such as muscles.

### B31-05 · teach "From stimulus to response"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The stimulus | Receptors in the eyes detect the ball. | stimulus → receptor | A football flies towards a goalkeeper. Light from the moving ball is the stimulus. Light receptors in the goalkeeper’s eyes detect it. | `nerve-path-receptor` |
| To the CNS | Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS. | receptor → sensory neurone → CNS | The receptors send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the brain. The brain is part of the CNS. | `nerve-path-sensory` |
| The CNS decides | The CNS coordinates a response. | CNS = coordination centre | The CNS receives the information and decides what to do. This is called coordinating a response. Here, the brain decides to move the goalkeeper’s arms. | `nerve-path-cns` |
| Out to the effectors | Motor neurones carry impulses to the muscles. | CNS → motor neurone → effector | Impulses travel from the CNS along motor neurones to the arm muscles. The muscles are the effectors. They contract, and the arms move up. | `nerve-path-motor` |
| Put it together | Stimulus to response, one step at a time. | stimulus → receptor → sensory → CNS → motor → effector → response | The goalkeeper catches the ball. This is the response. Responses like this follow the same route: stimulus, receptor, sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, effector, response. | `nerve-path-all` |

### B31-06 · choice
**Q:** Which is the right order?
- **0 Stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector ✓** · 1 Stimulus → effector → motor neurone → CNS → sensory neurone → receptor · 2 Stimulus → receptor → motor neurone → CNS → sensory neurone → effector
- Hint: Which neurones lead into the CNS?
- Explanation: Receptors detect the stimulus, and sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS. Motor neurones then carry impulses from the CNS to the effector.

### B31-07 · choice
**Q:** When the goalkeeper saves the ball, what are the effectors?
- 0 The eyes · 1 The brain · 2 The sensory neurones · **3 The arm muscles ✓**
- Hint: Effectors produce the response.
- Explanation: The eyes contain receptors, and the brain is part of the CNS. The arm muscles contract to produce the response, so they are the effectors.

### B31-08 · teach "Crossing the gap"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A tiny gap | Two neurones meet at a synapse. | synapse = where two neurones connect | Where one neurone ends and the next one starts, there is a tiny gap. The connection between two neurones is called a synapse. The electrical impulse cannot jump across the gap. | `nerve-syn-gap` |
| Chemicals released | The impulse makes the neurone release chemicals. | impulse arrives → chemicals out | An impulse arrives at the end of the first neurone. This makes the neurone release chemicals into the gap. | `nerve-syn-release` |
| Across the gap | The chemicals diffuse across the gap. | chemicals spread across | The chemicals spread out across the gap. They move by diffusion, which you met when you learned how substances move into and out of cells. | `nerve-syn-diffuse` |
| A new impulse | The chemicals set off a new impulse. | chemicals → new impulse | The chemicals reach the start of the next neurone. They set off a new electrical impulse there. The impulse then carries on along the next neurone. | `nerve-syn-new` |

### B31-09 · choice
**Q:** How is an impulse passed from one neurone to the next?
- 0 The impulse jumps across the gap as electricity · 1 The two neurones join together · **2 Chemicals diffuse across the gap and start a new impulse ✓** · 3 Blood carries it across the gap
- Hint: What is released into the gap?
- Explanation: At a synapse there is a tiny gap between the two neurones. Chemicals diffuse across it and start a new impulse in the next neurone.

### B31-10 · choice
**Q:** What is a synapse?
- **0 The connection between two neurones ✓** · 1 A type of receptor · 2 The middle of the brain
- Hint: Think about where one neurone meets the next.
- Explanation: A synapse is where one neurone ends and the next begins. So it is the connection between two neurones, with a tiny gap.

### B31-11 · teach "Why are reflexes fast?"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reflexes | A reflex is a fast, automatic response. | reflex = automatic, no thinking | You grab a rose stem and a thorn pricks your finger. Your hand pulls away before you even think about it. An automatic response like this is called a reflex. Reflexes are very quick, so they help stop you getting hurt. | `nerve-reflex-quick` |
| Into the spinal cord | A sensory neurone carries impulses to the spinal cord. | receptor → sensory neurone | Receptors in the skin of your finger detect the prick of the thorn. Impulses travel along a sensory neurone, up your arm to your spinal cord. | `nerve-reflex-sensory` |
| The relay neurone | A relay neurone passes the impulses on. | relay: sensory → motor | In the spinal cord, the impulses cross a synapse to a relay neurone. Relay neurones connect sensory neurones to motor neurones. They are found in the CNS. | `nerve-reflex-relay` |
| Out to the muscle | A motor neurone carries impulses to a muscle. | motor neurone → effector | The impulses cross another synapse to a motor neurone. The motor neurone carries them to a muscle in your arm, the effector. The muscle contracts and pulls your hand away. | `nerve-reflex-motor` |
| The reflex arc | The path of a reflex is called a reflex arc. | receptor → sensory → relay → motor → effector | The path from receptor to effector in a reflex is called a reflex arc. It goes through the spinal cord or a part of the brain you are not aware of. The thinking part of your brain is not involved, so the response is fast. | `nerve-reflex-arc` |

### B31-12 · choice
**Q:** What does a relay neurone do in a reflex arc?
- 0 Detects the stimulus · **1 Carries impulses from the sensory neurone to the motor neurone ✓** · 2 Contracts to move the hand · 3 Releases hormones into the blood
- Hint: Relay means passing something on.
- Explanation: Receptors detect the stimulus, and the muscle contracts. The relay neurone, in the CNS, passes impulses from the sensory neurone to the motor neurone.

### B31-13 · choice
**Q:** Why is a reflex so quick?
- **0 It does not involve the thinking part of the brain ✓** · 1 It uses only one neurone · 2 Impulses travel through the blood · 3 The muscle does not need impulses
- Hint: Which part of the brain is left out?
- Explanation: A reflex arc goes through the spinal cord or a part of the brain you are not aware of. No time is spent thinking about it, so the response is very quick.

### B31-14 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** A cyclist hears a car horn and brakes. Which part detects the stimulus?
- 0 The leg muscles · 1 The motor neurones · 2 The spinal cord · **3 The receptors in the ears ✓**
- Hint: Which receptors detect sound?
- Explanation: The leg muscles are effectors, and the motor neurones and spinal cord carry and coordinate impulses. The sound is detected by receptors in the ears.

### B31-15 · choice · `understanding`, independent · question diagram `nerve-reflex-question` (assessment version hides the answer)
**Q:** Look at the numbered parts of the reflex arc. Which number is the relay neurone?
- 0 Number 1 · 1 Number 2 · **2 Number 3 ✓** · 3 Number 4
- Hint: The relay neurone sits inside the spinal cord.
- Explanation: 1 is the receptors, 4 is the sensory neurone and 2 is the motor neurone. Number 3 is inside the spinal cord and joins the other two neurones, so it is the relay neurone.

### B31-16 · choice · `dataInterpretation`, independent · question diagram `nerve-reflex-data` (assessment version hides the answer)
**Q:** The chart shows two response times for one person. Which conclusion fits?
- 0 Reflexes are always five times faster, for everyone · **1 In this test, the reflex was faster than the response that needed thinking ✓** · 2 Pressing the button did not use the nervous system · 3 Both responses took the same time

### B31-17 · choice · `understanding`, independent
**Q:** A student writes: “In a reflex, you think about the danger, then your brain tells the muscle to move.” What is wrong?
- 0 Reflexes do not use any neurones · 1 The muscle is the receptor in a reflex · 2 Nothing, this is correct · **3 Reflexes are automatic, so you do not think about the response ✓**
- Hint: Do you decide to pull your hand away from a thorn?
- Explanation: A reflex does not involve the thinking part of the brain. Reflexes are automatic, so you do not think about the response.

### B31-18 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** You step on a sharp stone and your foot lifts before you think. Describe this reflex, from stimulus to response.
- Hint: Start at the receptors, then name each neurone in order.
- Model answer: The sharp stone is the stimulus. Receptors in the skin of the foot detect it. Impulses travel along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord. A relay neurone passes the impulses to a motor neurone. The motor neurone carries the impulses to a muscle in the leg, the effector, which contracts and lifts the foot.
- Marking points: Receptors in the skin detect the stimulus, the sharp stone. · A sensory neurone carries impulses to the spinal cord, part of the CNS. · A relay neurone passes the impulses to a motor neurone. · The motor neurone carries impulses to the effector, a muscle. · The muscle contracts and lifts the foot.
- Common errors: Saying the thinking part of the brain decides to move the foot. · Putting the motor neurone before the sensory neurone. · Saying a receptor or a neurone moves the foot, rather than a muscle.
