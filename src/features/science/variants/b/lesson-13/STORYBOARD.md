# Lesson 13 storyboard — Blood

Big idea: blood is a tissue — a liquid carrying three kinds of cell part, each with one job. The lesson starts with a whole drop of blood, then takes each part in turn and ends with plasma as the carrier.

1. Start here (B13-01): blood is mostly a pale liquid with cells floating in it.
2. What is in blood? (B13-02–03): plasma first, then red cells, white cells and platelets; blood is a tissue (link to Lesson 7). Diagram: magnified drop of blood.
3. Red blood cells (B13-04–07): haemoglobin carries oxygen from the lungs; biconcave ("dished in on both sides") gives a large surface area; no nucleus leaves room for haemoglobin. One highlighted part of the red-cell diagram per frame.
4. White blood cells (B13-08–09): pathogens (plain meaning first); engulfing; antibodies. Brief, with a forward link to Lesson 19.
5. Platelets (B13-10–11): tiny pieces of cells; a clot seals a cut and keeps microorganisms out.
6. Plasma carries everything (B13-12–14): dissolved substances; glucose, amino acids and hormones; urea to the kidneys and carbon dioxide to the lungs.
7. On your own (B13-15–18): name numbered part 2 (labels hidden until submission); identify a red blood cell from its features; few platelets → longer bleeding; teacher-reviewed explanation of red-cell adaptations.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first. Out of scope: phagocytosis, antitoxins, fibrin, plasma proteins.
