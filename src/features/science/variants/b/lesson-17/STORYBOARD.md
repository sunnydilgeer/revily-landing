# Lesson 17 storyboard — Plant tissues and the leaf

Big idea: a leaf is an organ made of layers of tissue, and each layer has a job. The lesson zooms in (whole plant → growing tips → inside a leaf → vein) and back out to the whole plant, ready for Lesson 18.

1. Start here (B17-01): the stomach is an organ made of tissues, which are made of cells (links to Lesson 7).
2. The whole plant (B17-02–03): roots, stem and leaves are organs; a tissue is similar cells doing one job; organs together form an organ system. Diagrams: whole plant; cell → tissue → organ → organ system ladder.
3. Where a plant grows (B17-04–05): meristem at shoot and root tips makes new cells (brief link to Lesson 5).
4. Inside a leaf (B17-06–10): one layer per screen, top to bottom — clear upper epidermis; palisade mesophyll near the top for most light; spongy mesophyll with air gaps; lower epidermis with stomata and guard cells; then light from above and carbon dioxide from below meet in the middle. Checks: why the top is clear, why palisade is near the top, what air gaps do, name a numbered layer (labels hidden until submission).
5. Back to the whole plant (B17-11–13): veins contain xylem (water and mineral ions in) and phloem (sugar out); cell structure is a link back to Lesson 4, not retaught.
6. On your own (B17-14–16): spot the error in a student's drawing (palisade at the bottom); stomata + air gaps; teacher-reviewed explanation of two layers.

Wording rules: one new term per screen, plain meaning first. Out of scope: waxy cuticle, companion cells, lignin detail, vascular-bundle terminology.
