# Lesson 26 storyboard — Photosynthesis and what plants do with glucose (v2)

First lesson of chapter B4, Bioenergetics.

Big idea: in its chloroplasts, a plant uses energy from light to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen, then uses that glucose to live, grow and store food.

Flow note: the lesson follows one sunflower through a summer. It opens with something familiar: a small seed grows taller than you in one summer, so where does all that new plant come from? The lesson answers in three steps.
1. **What goes in and out.** The word equation comes first, with each input traced to where it enters: carbon dioxide through the stomata and water from the roots (both brief links to Lesson 18).
2. **Where and how it happens.** Chloroplasts and chlorophyll, why photosynthesis is endothermic, and the chemical symbols, all shown on one summary card.
3. **Where the glucose goes.** Respiration, cellulose for the tall stem, amino acids made with nitrate from the soil, oil stored in the seeds (sunflower oil), and starch for the night. It ends with why starch, and not glucose, is stored.

The equation comes before the chloroplast because learners need to know what is being made before they learn where. Glucose's uses come last because they only make sense once glucose exists. Starch comes last of the five uses, because "why starch?" builds on osmosis (Lesson 6).

1. Start here (B26-01): which gas enters a leaf through the stomata? (links to Lesson 18).
2. Making glucose (B26-02–04): a sunflower grows → the word equation → carbon dioxide in → water in. Checks: the products; a numbered arrows diagram (which arrow is oxygen).
3. Inside the leaf (B26-05–07): chloroplasts → chlorophyll → endothermic → chemical symbols (the one summary card). Checks: what an endothermic reaction is; the symbol for glucose.
4. Where the glucose goes (B26-08–10): respiration → cellulose → amino acids and proteins → oils in seeds → starch → why starch (insoluble). Checks: a numbered sunflower diagram (where oil is stored); what nitrate ions are needed for.
5. On your own (B26-11–15): two days in the dark; root cells without chloroplasts; why starch is stored; an original starch-over-a-day graph read without over-claiming; teacher-reviewed written answer.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; words written out in full; British spelling. The symbols are written as CO₂, H₂O, C₆H₁₂O₆ and O₂. Learners recognise the symbols; they do not balance them.

Out of scope: the rate of photosynthesis and limiting factors (Lesson 27); respiration in detail and metabolism (Lesson 28); the balanced symbol equation; the practical test for starch; other mineral ions.

Source boundary: supplied page 44 (reference only); AQA 8464 sections 4.4.1.1 and 4.4.1.3. The sunflower story, all questions and the starch graph are original. Draft pending teacher review.

- 0 Oxygen · **1 Carbon dioxide ✓** · 2 Nitrogen · 3 Water vapour

### B26-03 · choice
**Q:** What are the products of photosynthesis?
- 0 Carbon dioxide and water · 1 Oxygen and water · **2 Glucose and oxygen ✓** · 3 Glucose and carbon dioxide
- Hint: The products are what is made, on the right of the arrow.
- Explanation: Carbon dioxide and water go in; they are the reactants. Glucose and oxygen are made; they are the products.

### B26-06 · choice
**Q:** Photosynthesis takes in energy from the environment. What is this type of reaction called?
- **0 Endothermic ✓** · 1 Exothermic · 2 Osmosis · 3 Respiration
- Hint: Think about which way the energy moves.
- Explanation: Energy is transferred from the environment into the chloroplasts. A reaction that takes in energy like this is endothermic.

### B26-07 · choice
**Q:** Which is the chemical symbol for glucose?
- 0 CO₂ · 1 H₂O · 2 O₂ · **3 C₆H₁₂O₆ ✓**
- Hint: Glucose is the largest molecule of the four.
- Explanation: CO₂ is carbon dioxide, H₂O is water and O₂ is oxygen. Glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆.

### B26-09 · choice · question diagram `photo-parts-question` (assessment version hides the answer)
Diagram: the sunflower with four numbered parts: 1 the roots, 2 the stem, 3 a leaf, 4 the seeds in the flower head. The names stay hidden until the answer is submitted.

**Q:** Look at the numbered parts. Where does the sunflower store most of its oil?
- 0 Part 1 · 1 Part 2 · 2 Part 3 · **3 Part 4 ✓**
- Hint: Think about where sunflower oil for cooking comes from.
- Explanation: Parts 1, 2 and 3 are the roots, stem and a leaf, which store starch. Part 4 is the seeds, where the sunflower stores oil.

### B26-10 · choice
**Q:** What must the roots absorb from the soil so the sunflower can turn glucose into amino acids?
- **0 Nitrate ions ✓** · 1 Starch · 2 Oxygen · 3 Cellulose
- Hint: Glucose is combined with something from the soil.
- Explanation: Amino acids need more than glucose. Glucose is combined with nitrate ions, absorbed from the soil, to make amino acids.

### B26-11 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** A sunflower is kept in the dark for two days. What does it use to keep living?
- 0 Glucose it makes by photosynthesis in the dark · **1 Starch it stored in its leaves, stems and roots ✓** · 2 Chlorophyll from its leaves
- Hint: Can photosynthesis happen without light?
- Explanation: Photosynthesis needs light, so no new glucose is made in the dark. The sunflower uses the starch it stored earlier.

### B26-12 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** Cells in a sunflower's roots have no chloroplasts. Why can these cells not photosynthesise?
- **0 They have no chlorophyll to absorb light ✓** · 1 They have no cell walls · 2 They take in too much water
- Hint: What does a chloroplast contain?
- Explanation: Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts, where chlorophyll absorbs light. Root cells have no chloroplasts, so they have no chlorophyll to absorb light.

### B26-13 · choice · `understanding`, independent
**Q:** Why do plants store glucose as starch?
- 0 Starch dissolves easily in water · **1 Starch is insoluble, so it does not draw water into cells by osmosis ✓** · 2 Starch is a gas that can leave the plant
- Hint: Think about what glucose does when it dissolves in a cell.
- Explanation: Lots of dissolved glucose would draw water into a cell by osmosis, and the cell would swell. Starch is insoluble, so it does not draw water into cells by osmosis.

### B26-14 · choice · `dataInterpretation`, independent · question diagram `photo-starch-data` (assessment version hides the answer)
Diagram: an original line graph of the amount of starch in one sunflower leaf over 24 hours. There is little starch at 06:00; it rises through the day to a peak at 18:00, then falls through the night. A day-and-night band runs along the time axis. There is no conclusion text.

**Q:** The graph shows the starch in one leaf over a day. Which conclusion fits it?
- 0 The leaf made most of its starch at night · 1 The amount of starch stayed the same all day · **2 Starch built up in the light and was used up in the dark ✓** · 3 All plants store starch only in their leaves
- Hint: Compare the line during the day with the line at night.
- Explanation: The starch rose while it was light and fell at night; one leaf cannot show what all plants do. Starch built up in the light and was used up in the dark.

### B26-15 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Describe how a sunflower makes glucose, and give two ways it uses the glucose.
- Hint: Say what goes in, where it happens and what comes out, then give two uses.
- Model answer: The sunflower takes in carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil. In the chloroplasts, chlorophyll absorbs light, and the energy is used to turn them into glucose and oxygen. The sunflower uses some glucose for respiration, and it turns some into starch, which it stores for the night.
- Marking points: Carbon dioxide and water are the reactants. · Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light energy. · Glucose and oxygen are made. · One correct use of glucose, such as respiration or cellulose for cell walls. · A second correct use, such as starch or oil stored, or amino acids for proteins.
- Common errors: Saying the plant takes in oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. · Saying light is a reactant, or that photosynthesis gives out energy. · Saying glucose is stored as glucose. · Saying amino acids are made from glucose alone, without nitrate.
