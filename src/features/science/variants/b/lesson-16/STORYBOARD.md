# Lesson 16 storyboard — Risk factors and cancer

Big idea: a risk factor makes a disease more likely, not certain. Cancer starts when cells divide out of control.

1. Start here (B16-01): crossing a busy road without looking — more likely to be hit, not certain.
2. Risk means chance (B16-02–03): risk factor (plain meaning first); lifestyle, environment and genes; several factors act together. Diagram: 2 in 20 vs 8 in 20 ill.
3. Link or cause? (B16-04–05): correlation; a link is not proof (ice cream and sunburn both rise in sunny weather); for some factors the causal mechanism is known (smoke damaging lung cells).
4. The main risk factors (B16-06–09): smoking; diet and exercise → cardiovascular disease (link to Lesson 14); obesity → Type 2 diabetes; alcohol → liver and brain; smoking and alcohol in pregnancy; carcinogens including ionising radiation. One matching check replaces four single-fact checks.
5. How cancer starts and spreads (B16-10–13): controlled division (reuses the Lesson 5 cell-cycle diagram); a tumour forms when division is out of control; benign vs malignant; secondary tumours; lifestyle and genetic risk factors for some cancers.
6. Lowering risk and cost (B16-14–15): some risks can be lowered, not every risk; human and financial costs.
7. On your own (B16-16–19): example scatter data (correlation, not cause); name the tumour type from a spread diagram (labels hidden until submission); a non-smoker with lung cancer; teacher-reviewed benign vs malignant explanation.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first; non-moralising health language. Out of scope: insulin, lymph, screening.
