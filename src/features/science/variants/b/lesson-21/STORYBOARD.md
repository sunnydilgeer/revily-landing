# Lesson 21 storyboard — Plant diseases and malaria (v2)

Big idea: plant diseases damage leaves, so the plant makes less food and cannot grow well; malaria shows how an animal vector carries a pathogen from person to person. The lesson stays in one gardener's garden until both plant diseases are finished: tomatoes first, then roses. The tomatoes introduce the chain *less chlorophyll or fewer leaves → less photosynthesis → less food → poor growth*, and the roses reuse it rather than reteaching it. The lesson then leaves the garden for malaria. Malaria reuses the vector idea from Lesson 19 and the four-question disease card from Lesson 20. It ends by completing a grid of all seven diseases.

1. Start here (B21-01): what leaves use light for (links to Lesson 17).
2. A patchy tomato plant (B21-02–04): TMV → mosaic pattern → pale patches have less chlorophyll → less photosynthesis → less food → poor growth → spread by touch (named only). Checks: why the plant grows poorly; fill the missing step in the chain.
3. Spotted roses (B21-05–07): rose black spot is a fungus → leaves yellow and drop → the same chain → spread by water and wind → fungicides and destroying leaves. Checks: why leaves are destroyed; which actions control it.
4. Malaria and the mosquito (B21-08–10): a protist with part of its life cycle in mosquitoes → picked up in one bite → passed on in the next (vector) → fever that keeps coming back → stop breeding, use nets. Checks: numbered bite cycle (labels hidden); why a mosquito is a vector.
5. All seven diseases (B21-11–12): a grid grouped by pathogen type. Check: name a numbered column (headings hidden).
6. On your own (B21-13–16): mosquito nets; original fungicide data without over-claiming; which diseases cut photosynthesis; teacher-reviewed comparison of malaria and black spot.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first. Out of scope: TMV control, the stages of the malaria protist's life cycle, named antimalarial drugs, plant defences (Biology only), and how fungicides work.

Source boundary: supplied pages 38–39; AQA 8464 sections 4.3.1.2 (TMV), 4.3.1.4 and 4.3.1.5. Draft pending teacher review.

### B21-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** Leaves use light to make the plant's food. What is this process called?
- 0 Respiration · **1 Photosynthesis ✓** · 2 Transpiration
- Hint: You met the leaf's main job in Lesson 17.
- Explanation: Transpiration is water loss from leaves, and respiration releases energy. Making food using light is photosynthesis.

### B21-03 · choice
**Q:** Why does a tomato plant with TMV grow poorly?
- 0 The virus eats the plant's roots · **1 Its leaves have less chlorophyll, so less photosynthesis happens ✓** · 2 It loses too much water through its leaves
- Hint: Follow the chain from pale leaves to growth.
- Explanation: TMV makes pale patches with less chlorophyll, so less light is absorbed. Less photosynthesis means less food for growth.

- 0 More chlorophyll · 1 Faster transpiration · **2 Less photosynthesis ✓** · 3 More food is made

- Explanation: Less chlorophyll means less light is absorbed. So less photosynthesis happens, and less food is made.

### B21-06 · choice
**Q:** Why must a gardener destroy spotted rose leaves, not leave them on the ground?
- **0 So the fungus cannot spread to other roses ✓** · 1 So new leaves grow faster · 2 So the leaves make more chlorophyll
- Hint: What is still living on the fallen leaves?
- Explanation: The removed leaves still carry the fungus. Water or wind could carry it from them to other plants.

### B21-07 · choice
**Q:** Which pair of actions would help control rose black spot?
- 0 Mosquito nets and insecticide · 1 Vaccination and isolation · **2 Fungicide spray and destroying spotted leaves ✓** · 3 Antiretroviral drugs and condoms
- Hint: Rose black spot is caused by a fungus.
- Explanation: Fungicides kill the fungus on the plant. Destroying spotted leaves removes fungus that could spread.

### B21-09 · choice · question diagram `malaria-cycle-question` (assessment: four stages numbered, captions hidden)
**Q:** Which numbered stage shows the mosquito passing the protist to a new person?
- 0 Stage 1 · 1 Stage 2 · 2 Stage 3 · **3 Stage 4 ✓**
- Hint: Look for a bite on someone who is not ill yet.
- Explanation: Stage 2 shows the mosquito picking the protist up from an infected person. Stage 4 shows it biting someone new and passing the protist on.

### B21-10 · choice
**Q:** Why is a mosquito called a vector for malaria?
- 0 It causes malaria by itself · **1 It carries the protist from person to person ✓** · 2 It makes toxins in the blood
- Hint: What does the word "vector" mean?
- Explanation: The protist causes malaria, not the mosquito. The mosquito carries the protist between people, so it is a vector.

### B21-13 · choice · `application`, independent
**Q:** A charity gives mosquito nets to families. How do the nets reduce malaria?

### B21-15 · choice · `understanding`, independent
**Q:** Which two diseases both reduce photosynthesis in plants?
- 0 Measles and malaria · 1 Salmonella and TMV · **2 TMV and rose black spot ✓**
- Hint: Which diseases affect plants?
- Explanation: TMV makes leaves pale, and rose black spot makes leaves fall. Both mean less photosynthesis.

### B21-16 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Compare how malaria and rose black spot spread. Give one way to reduce the spread of each.
- Hint: Take one disease at a time. What carries the pathogen, and what would block that route?
- Model answer: Malaria is spread by mosquitoes. They pick up the protist in one bite and pass it on in the next. Mosquito nets, or stopping mosquitoes breeding, reduce its spread. Rose black spot is spread by water or wind. Spraying fungicide, or removing and destroying spotted leaves, reduces its spread.
- Marking points: malaria is carried by mosquitoes (vectors) · malaria: nets or stopping breeding · black spot is spread by water or wind · black spot: fungicide or destroying spotted leaves.
- Common errors: says the mosquito causes malaria · says black spot spreads by insects · suggests antibiotics or vaccination.
