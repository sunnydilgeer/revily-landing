# Lesson 22 storyboard — How your body defends itself (v2)

Big idea: your body keeps most pathogens out at every entrance; any that get in are attacked by white blood cells in three ways. The lesson follows one set of bacteria on a school door handle. It tries each entrance in turn: skin, nose, airways (mucus and cilia), then the stomach, where swallowed mucus meets acid. A cut then lets a few bacteria in, and the lesson follows them to the end. White blood cells swallow them (phagocytosis), make antibodies that lock onto their antigens, and make antitoxins against their toxins. Antigens are taught before antibodies, because an antibody only makes sense once you know what it locks onto. Antitoxins come last, because they rely on toxins from Lesson 19.

1. Start here (B22-01): which part of the blood fights pathogens? (links to Lesson 13).
2. Keeping pathogens out (B22-02–05): door handle → skin → nose (mucus defined) → trachea and bronchi (links to Lesson 10) → cilia → stomach acid. Checks: numbered body diagram (labels hidden); why swallowed mucus is safe; what cilia do.
3. One gets in (B22-06–07): a cut breaks the barrier → immune system → white blood cells → phagocytosis. Check: what happens in phagocytosis.
4. Antibodies and antitoxins (B22-08–10): antigens → antibodies lock on → one antibody, one antigen → antitoxins → the three ways together. Checks: numbered antigen/antibody diagram (labels hidden); what stops toxins.
5. On your own (B22-11–15): damaged cilia; why one antibody can't fit all; the antibody order; an original infected-cut graph read without over-claiming; teacher-reviewed three ways.

Wording rules: one new term per frame, plain meaning first. Out of scope: how immunity lasts and vaccination (Lesson 23), antibiotics (Lesson 24), names of white blood cell types (phagocyte, lymphocyte), and specific versus non-specific defence as terms.

### B22-15 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Describe three ways white blood cells defend the body against pathogens.
- Hint: Name each way, then say what it does to the pathogen or its toxins.
- Model answer: Some white blood cells surround pathogens and digest them. This is phagocytosis. Some white blood cells make antibodies, which lock onto antigens on a pathogen so it can be destroyed. White blood cells also make antitoxins, which stop the toxins made by bacteria working.
- Marking points: Phagocytosis: white blood cells surround and digest pathogens. · Antibodies lock onto antigens so the pathogen is destroyed. · Antitoxins stop toxins working.
- Common errors: Antibodies and antigens are mixed up. · Skin, mucus or stomach acid are given as white blood cell actions. · Antitoxins are said to kill the bacteria themselves.
