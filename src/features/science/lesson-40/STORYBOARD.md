# Lesson 45 storyboard — Inherited disorders and embryo screening (folder lesson-40)

Last lesson of the DNA and reproduction part of chapter B6.

Big idea: some disorders are passed on through faulty alleles; genetic diagrams and family trees show the chances, and embryo screening can find disorders early, which raises questions that people answer differently.

Flow note: two disorders that mirror each other, then reading families, then screening.
1. **Cystic fibrosis.** Inherited disorders, a recessive allele of cell membranes, carriers, and the Ff × Ff cross (1 in 4).
2. **Polydactyly.** Extra fingers or toes, a dominant allele, and the Dd × dd cross (1 in 2).
3. **Read a family tree.** The key, why carriers show the allele is recessive, and the chances for a new baby (the same Ff × Ff cross).
4. **Embryo screening.** Testing IVF embryos and embryos in the womb, the decisions it can lead to, then arguments for and against in equal space.

Why this order: the recessive disorder comes first because it needs the idea of a carrier, which the family tree then uses. Polydactyly contrasts dominant with recessive while the Punnett method is fresh. Screening comes last because it depends on knowing what can be detected. Calculations: the frames model 1 in 4 (cystic fibrosis) and 1 in 2 (polydactyly); guided practice B40-04 (carrier chance) and B40-07 (Dd × Dd); independent calculation B40-14 (ff × Ff).

Sensitive topic: the language is factual and neutral. The decisions frame states what the page states (embryos not used and destroyed; a decision to end a pregnancy) without judgement. The for and against lists get equal space and neutral colours, and no question asks the learner for their own view. The hand drawing is a simple outline, not a medical image.

1. Start here (B40-01): the alleles needed for a recessive characteristic (links to genetic diagrams).
2. Cystic fibrosis (B40-02–04): inherited disorders → cystic fibrosis → carriers → two carriers. Checks: why a carrier does not have the disorder; the chance of a carrier child (calculation).
3. Polydactyly (B40-05–07): extra fingers or toes → a dominant allele → one parent. Checks: why one parent can pass it on; Dd × Dd (calculation).
4. Read a family tree (B40-08–09): the key → reading the tree → a new baby. Check: which numbered person is a carrier.
5. Embryo screening (B40-10–12): testing an embryo → embryo screening → hard decisions → arguments for → arguments against. Checks: what embryo screening is; which argument is against.
6. On your own (B40-13–16): a new family tree (the genotype of a parent); ff × Ff (independent calculation); an invented opinion survey read without over-claiming; teacher-reviewed written answer with one argument on each side.

Out of scope: IVF as a fertility treatment (Higher), symptoms and treatment of cystic fibrosis, gene therapy, the law beyond the one example on the page, sex-linked disorders.

Source boundary: supplied pages 66–67 used for scope only; AQA 8464 section 4.6.1.7. The book's named family is replaced by an original, unnamed family. All wording, questions, survey data and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

## States in full

### B40-01 · choice · `priorKnowledge`, `diagnostic`
**Q:** For an organism to show a recessive characteristic, what must its alleles be?
- **0 Both recessive, such as bb ✓** · 1 One dominant and one recessive, such as Bb · 2 Both dominant, such as BB · 3 Any mix, as long as there is one recessive allele
- Hint: You met dominant and recessive alleles when you learned about genetic diagrams.
- Explanation: A dominant allele shows even when there is only one copy. So a recessive characteristic only shows when both alleles are recessive, such as bb.

### B40-02 · teach "Cystic fibrosis"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inherited disorders | Some health conditions are caused by faulty alleles. | faulty allele → passed on | Some health conditions are passed from parents to their children. They are caused by inheriting faulty alleles. Conditions like this are called inherited disorders. | `inherit-cf-intro` |
| Cystic fibrosis | Cystic fibrosis is a disorder of cell membranes. | recessive allele f; ff has it | Cystic fibrosis is an inherited disorder of cell membranes. It is caused by a recessive allele, written f. The dominant allele, F, does not cause the disorder. So a person must have two f alleles, ff, to have cystic fibrosis. | `inherit-cf-allele` |
| Carriers | People with one f allele do not have the disorder. | Ff = carrier | A person with one F allele and one f allele does not have cystic fibrosis. But they can pass the f allele on to their children. A person like this is called a carrier. | `inherit-cf-carrier` |
| Two carriers | Two carriers have a 1 in 4 chance of a child with cystic fibrosis. | Ff × Ff → FF, Ff, Ff, ff | For a child to have cystic fibrosis, both parents must be carriers or have the disorder. Here, two carriers (Ff) have a child. The four possible genotypes are FF, Ff, Ff and ff. So there is a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of the child having cystic fibrosis. | `inherit-cf-cross` |

### B40-03 · choice
**Q:** Why does a carrier of cystic fibrosis not have the disorder?
- 0 They have no f alleles · 1 Cystic fibrosis is caused by a dominant allele · **2 They have one F allele, and the f allele is recessive ✓** · 3 Carriers have two f alleles
- Hint: What is a carrier’s genotype?
- Explanation: A carrier is Ff, with one of each allele. The f allele is recessive, so one F allele means they do not have the disorder.

### B40-04 · choice · `calculation`
**Q:** Two carriers (Ff) have a child. What is the chance that the child is a carrier?
- 0 1 in 4 (25%) · 1 3 in 4 (75%) · 2 0 (0%) · **3 1 in 2 (50%) ✓**
- Hint: Count the Ff outcomes out of four.
- Explanation: The four possible genotypes are FF, Ff, Ff and ff. 2 of the 4 are Ff, so the chance is 2 in 4 = 1 in 2 (50%).

### B40-05 · teach "Polydactyly"
| Label | Summary | Think: | Text | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extra fingers or toes | Polydactyly means being born with extra fingers or toes. | extra digits | Some babies are born with an extra finger or toe. This inherited disorder is called polydactyly. | `inherit-poly-hand` |
| A dominant allele | Polydactyly is caused by a dominant allele, D. | D: one copy is enough | Polydactyly is caused by a dominant allele, written D. The other allele, d, is recessive. Because D is dominant, one copy is enough. So a person who is Dd has polydactyly, and just one parent can pass it on. | `inherit-poly-allele` |
| One parent | One Dd parent gives a 1 in 2 chance. | Dd × dd → Dd, dd, Dd, dd | Here, one parent has polydactyly (Dd) and the other does not (dd). The four possible genotypes are Dd, dd, Dd and dd. Two of the four have a D allele. So there is a 1 in 2 (50%) chance of the child having polydactyly. | `inherit-poly-cross` |

### B40-06 · choice
**Q:** Why can polydactyly be inherited from just one parent?
- 0 It needs two recessive alleles · 1 Only carriers can pass it on · 2 It is not inherited · **3 It is caused by a dominant allele, so one copy is enough ✓**
- Hint: Is the polydactyly allele dominant or recessive?
- Explanation: Polydactyly is caused by the dominant allele D. One copy of a dominant allele is enough, so it can come from just one parent.

### B40-07 · choice · `calculation`
**Q:** Both parents have polydactyly and are Dd. What is the chance their child will not have polydactyly?
- 0 3 in 4 (75%) · **1 1 in 4 (25%) ✓** · 2 1 in 2 (50%) · 3 0 (0%)
- Hint: Only dd does not have polydactyly. How many squares are dd?
- Explanation: The four possible genotypes are DD, Dd, Dd and dd. Only dd does not have polydactyly: 1 of 4, a 1 in 4 (25%) chance.

### B40-11 · choice
**Q:** What is embryo screening?
- **0 Testing embryos for inherited disorders ✓** · 1 Choosing a baby’s eye colour · 2 Treating cystic fibrosis in adults · 3 Making embryos by meiosis
- Hint: What is being tested, and what for?
- Explanation: A cell or DNA is taken from the embryo and its genes are tested. Embryo screening is testing embryos for inherited disorders.

### B40-12 · choice
**Q:** Which is an argument against embryo screening?
- 0 It could help stop people suffering · 1 Treating disorders costs a lot of money · **2 It could suggest people with genetic disorders are not wanted ✓** · 3 There are laws to stop it going too far
- Hint: Three of these are reasons people give in support of screening.
- Explanation: Stopping suffering, the cost of treatment and the laws are all given in support of screening. Saying it suggests people with genetic disorders are not wanted is an argument against it.

### B40-13 · choice · `application`, independent · question diagram `inherit-tree-question2` (assessment version hides the answer)
Diagram: a new family with a key: 1 father (not known), 2 carrier mother, 3 son with cystic fibrosis, 4 unaffected daughter.

**Q:** Look at this family tree for cystic fibrosis. Person 1 does not have cystic fibrosis. What is person 1’s genotype?
- 0 FF · **1 Ff ✓** · 2 ff
- Hint: Person 3 has cystic fibrosis. Where did each of person 3’s f alleles come from?
- Explanation: Person 3 has cystic fibrosis, so is ff, with one f from each parent; person 1 does not have the disorder, so is not ff. So person 1 must have one F and one f: Ff, a carrier.

### B40-14 · choice · `calculation`, independent
**Q:** A woman has cystic fibrosis (ff). Her partner is a carrier (Ff). What is the chance that their child will have cystic fibrosis?
- 0 1 in 4 (25%) · 1 3 in 4 (75%) · **2 1 in 2 (50%) ✓** · 3 0 (0%)
- Hint: The woman can only pass on f. Draw the square.
- Explanation: The four possible genotypes are Ff, Ff, ff and ff. 2 of the 4 are ff, so the chance is 1 in 2 (50%).

### B40-15 · choice · `dataInterpretation`, independent · question diagram `inherit-screen-data` (assessment version hides the answer)

**Q:** A survey asked 200 adults in one town when embryo screening should be allowed. The chart shows the results. Which conclusion fits?
- 0 Everyone in the country supports embryo screening · **1 In this survey, more people supported screening for serious disorders than for choosing features ✓** · 2 Screening to choose eye colour is allowed by law · 3 The survey proves embryo screening is right
- Hint: Compare the two bars, and think about who was asked.
- Explanation: 70% supported screening for serious disorders and 10% for choosing features, but only 200 people in one town were asked. So in this survey, more people supported screening for serious disorders than for choosing features.

### B40-16 · written · `teacherOnly`
**Q:** Two parents are both carriers of cystic fibrosis. Explain the chance that their child will have cystic fibrosis. Then give one argument for and one argument against embryo screening.
- Hint: Draw the Ff × Ff cross first. Then give one point on each side.
- Model answer: Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele, so a child needs ff to have it. Each parent is Ff, so each can pass on F or f. The cross gives FF, Ff, Ff and ff, a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of cystic fibrosis. Screening could help stop people suffering. But some people say it suggests people with genetic disorders are not wanted.
- Marking points: Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele, so a child needs ff. · Each parent is Ff, so each can pass on F or f. · The cross gives FF, Ff, Ff and ff: a 1 in 4 (25%) chance of cystic fibrosis. · One argument for, such as it could help stop suffering, or treating disorders costs a lot. · One argument against, such as it suggests people with disorders are not wanted, could lead to choosing features, or is expensive.
- Common errors: Saying cystic fibrosis is caused by a dominant allele. · Saying carriers have the disorder. · Saying a 1 in 4 chance means exactly one child in four will have it. · Giving arguments on only one side.
