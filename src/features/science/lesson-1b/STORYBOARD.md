# Lesson 1b storyboard — Bacteria and comparing cells

Big idea: a bacterium is a whole living thing made of one tiny cell, and it keeps its DNA without a nucleus. Where a cell keeps its DNA sorts every cell into one of two types.

Anchor and route: build one bacterial cell part by part, starting with the parts learners already know (membrane → cytoplasm and ribosomes) and then the new ones (wall → DNA loop → plasmids). Then ask one question of every cell: is the DNA inside a nucleus? Then lay animal, plant and bacterial cells side by side and compare them one part at a time.

Why this order: the familiar parts come first, so the new parts stand out against them. The wall comes before the DNA, because learners already know a plant cell wall and only need "not cellulose". The DNA loop comes before plasmids, because plasmids are defined as extra DNA separate from the main loop. Eukaryotic and prokaryotic come after the DNA loop, because both words are defined by where the DNA is. The three-way comparison comes last, because it needs all three cells and both cell types. Its last frame teaches "one part at a time" and "count no nucleus once", which the written task B1-21 then uses.
