# Working Scientifically Lesson 8 storyboard — Interpreting graphs

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-8`, id `W-DAT-008-W`, skill `W-DAT-008-W`, spec WS 3.5. It owns gradient as steepness and rate, gradient = change in y ÷ change in x from a straight line (with units), time on the x-axis for a rate, and positive, negative and no correlation. Tangents are Higher tier only and left out.

Big idea: the steepness of a line tells you how fast something changes, and you can put a number on it with two points and a triangle. The overall slope of scattered points shows the type of relationship.

Flow note: meaning of gradient first (steeper is faster), then the calculation as a four-step worked example, then correlation. Calculation flow: worked (10 s, 6 cm³ and 30 s, 18 cm³: 12 ÷ 20 = 0.6 cm³/s) → guided (changes 20 cm³ and 40 s; then 20 s, 10 cm³ and 60 s, 30 cm³: 0.5 cm³/s) → independent (graph with A (20 s, 15 cm³) and B (60 s, 45 cm³): 0.75 cm³/s; text: (2 min, 3 g) and (6 min, 15 g): 3 g/min) → written ((20 s, 8 cm³) and (60 s, 32 cm³): 0.6 cm³/s).

## Diagram specs
House style as Lesson 7: soft flat fills, darker strokes, text at least 12px, readable at 360px. Colour code: gradient triangle in one accent, change in y and change in x labelled in two different tints.

- `wsgraph-steep`: a graph frame with a steep line and a shallow line from the origin, labelled "steep: changes quickly" and "shallow: changes slowly"; a flat line labelled "flat: no change".
- `wsgraph-compare`: gas volume (cm³) against time (s) with two lines from the origin: reaction A steeper, reaction B shallower; a small syringe or bubbling beaker icon by each.
- `wsgraph-formula`: a card "gradient = change in y ÷ change in x" with a small line and right-angled triangle, rise labelled "change in y" and run labelled "change in x".
- `wsgraph-rate`: axes with "Time (s)" on x highlighted, line, and the unit built up: "cm³ ÷ s = cm³/s"; tag "time on the x-axis gives a rate".
Worked example share one drawing: axes "Time (s)" 0 to 40 (marks every 10) and "Volume of gas (cm³)" 0 to 24 (marks every 4 or 6), straight line from the origin with gradient 0.6 through (10, 6), (30, 18); a four-step strip along the top with the current step highlighted.
- `wsgraph-work-1`: the two chosen points marked and labelled (10 s, 6 cm³) and (30 s, 18 cm³).
- `wsgraph-work-2`: adds the dashed right-angled triangle, upright side labelled "change in y", bottom side "change in x".
- `wsgraph-work-3`: adds "18 − 6 = 12 cm³" beside the upright side and "30 − 10 = 20 s" beside the bottom side.
- `wsgraph-work-4`: the sum "12 ÷ 20 = 0.6" and the highlighted answer "0.6 cm³/s".
- `wsgraph-positive`: scatter of about ten crosses rising left to right with a faint rising line; caption "positive correlation: both increase".
- `wsgraph-negative`: scatter falling left to right with faint falling line (bubbles per minute from pondweed against distance from a lamp, so it does not repeat the question graph); caption "negative correlation: one increases, the other decreases".
- `wsgraph-none`: scatter with no pattern; caption "no correlation: no relationship".
Question visuals (assessment view):
- `wsgraph-q-line`: graph, x "Time (s)" 0 to 80 (ticks every 20, faint gridlines every 10), y "Volume of gas (cm³)" 0 to 60 (ticks every 10, minor every 5). Straight line from the origin through A (20 s, 15 cm³) and B (60 s, 45 cm³); A and B are marked with dots and letters only, with no coordinates and no triangle. Accessible description: "A straight-line graph with two marked points A and B."
- `wsgraph-q-scatter`: scatter graph, x "Water temperature (°C)" 10 to 80, y "Time to dissolve (s)" 0 to 120, about ten crosses falling left to right, no line and no word "correlation".
