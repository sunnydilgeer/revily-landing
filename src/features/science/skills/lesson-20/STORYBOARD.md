# Working Scientifically Lesson 20 storyboard: Electrical meters and light gates

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-20`, id `W-PRC-020-W`, spec AT 6. It owns the voltmeter in parallel, the ammeter in series, multimeter use, turning the circuit off between readings, and light gates (speed from the object's length, acceleration with a card with a gap, fewer timing errors).

Big idea: the way you connect a meter or a light gate decides what it measures, and the right connection gives better data.

Flow note: voltmeter and ammeter first (the connections students most often confuse); then the multimeter, which reuses the same two rules with ports and a dial; then light gates, which need a calculation (speed = length ÷ time). Calculation flow: worked example → near-identical guided item → independent item on your own. Links back: series and parallel circuits (Physics) in one clause; reaction-time errors (measuring lesson) in a frame.

Sections:
1. Start here (W20-01): how to connect a meter across a bulb.
2. How do you connect a voltmeter and an ammeter? (W20-02–04): voltmeter parallel → red and black ports → ammeter series → turn off between readings. Checks: which meter and connection; why switch off.
3. What does a multimeter do? (W20-05–07): one device, a dial → potential difference (parallel, V) → current (series, A). Checks: current set-up; the dial.
4. How do light gates measure speed? (W20-08–11): beam interrupted → speed = length ÷ time → acceleration with a gapped card → fewer timing errors. Worked: 0.05 m in 0.02 s = 2.5 m/s. Guided: 0.10 m in 0.05 s = 2 m/s. Check: why a card with a gap.
5. On your own (W20-12–14): independent speed (0.24 m in 0.08 s = 3 m/s); a numbered circuit question; written task on light gates against a stopwatch.

Out of scope: circuit calculations (V = IR), resistance measurement, the light gate in specific Physics practicals.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 253 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 6. All wording, examples, numbers and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Acceleration is taught only as "two speeds from two interruptions"; no acceleration calculation is set.
- The speed equation is stated in words as length ÷ time and matches the earlier speed equation.
