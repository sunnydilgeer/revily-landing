# Working Scientifically Lesson 7 storyboard — Presenting data

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-7`, id `W-DAT-007-W`, skill `W-DAT-007-W`, spec WS 3.1. It owns bar charts versus graphs (categoric versus continuous data), the rules for drawing each, plotting crosses, a line of best fit and ignoring anomalies.

Big idea: the type of data decides the display. Groups get a bar chart; two continuous variables get a graph. Either way, clear scales, labels and units make it readable.

Flow note: the choice of display first (it decides everything after), then bar chart rules (short), then graph rules (longer, ending in best fit and anomalies). Examples come from all three sciences (woodlice, gas volume, spring stretch, current and voltage, cooling tea).

Sections:
1. Start here (W7-01): showing how pupils travel.
2. Bar chart or graph? (W7-02–04): groups, continuous data, choosing, examples. Checks: which data suits a bar chart; current and voltage.
3. How do you draw a bar chart? (W7-05–07): even scale, labels and units, gaps and a key. Checks: an uneven scale; a key.
4. How do you plot a graph? (W7-08–11): axes, scale, crosses, line of best fit, anomaly. Checks: best fit; axes; a sensible scale.
5. On your own (W7-12–15): spot the anomaly (numbered graph), read a bar chart, plan a spring graph, written plan for a cooling tea graph.

Out of scope: pie charts, histograms, tables (Lesson 6), calculating gradients (Lesson 8), drawing curves through data beyond "smooth curve".

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 240 (scope only); AQA 8464 WS 3.1. All wording, data and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls: "categoric or counted" data are both shown as separate groups; the frames avoid the word "discrete" to keep one new term per frame. Line of best fit is described as "through or close to as many points as possible".

## Diagram specs
Look as Lessons 17 and 18: soft rounded shapes, flat fills with a darker stroke of the same hue, hand-drawn feel to axes, text at least 12px, readable at 360px. Small pencil-cross style points.

- `wspresent-categoric`: four groups shown as small cards or pictures (blood groups A, B, AB, O; habitats) with gaps between; caption "separate groups".
- `wspresent-continuous`: a number line or thermometer scale from 20 to 21 °C with several marks between, and label "any value in a range".
- `wspresent-choose`: a two-branch decision picture: groups → small bar chart; two continuous variables → small graph with line.
- `wspresent-examples`: three mini-cards: woodlice in four habitats (bar chart), gas volume over time (graph), spring stretch against force (graph).
- `wspresent-bar-scale`: a bar chart of daisies in four fields with even scale 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 highlighted; note "equal steps" and "use at least half the paper".
- `wspresent-bar-labels`: the same chart with both axis titles highlighted: "Field" and "Number of daisies".
- `wspresent-bar-key`: a bar chart with two sets of bars (pond A, pond B) in two colours, gaps between the groups highlighted and a key box.
- `wspresent-plot-axes`: an empty graph frame with x-axis "Time (s)" labelled independent (horizontal) and y-axis "Volume of gas (cm³)" dependent (vertical), with units.
- `wspresent-plot-scale`: two small axes side by side: a bad one squashed into a corner (scale to 500) and a good one filling the paper (scale 0–50 s); largest value 46 marked.
- `wspresent-plot-points`: a close-up of a neat small cross beside a smudged blob, with a pencil.
- `wspresent-plot-line`: a graph with about eight crosses and a smooth straight line of best fit passing through or near them; a faint zig-zag "join the dots" line crossed out.
- `wspresent-plot-anomaly`: the same graph with one cross far from the line, circled, tagged "anomalous result: ignore it".
