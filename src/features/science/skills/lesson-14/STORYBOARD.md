# Working Scientifically Lesson 14 storyboard: Measuring volume, length, angles, temperature and time

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-14`, id `W-PRC-014-W`, spec AT 1. It owns the eureka can method, choice of ruler (centimetre, metre, micrometer), reading at eye level, measuring ten and dividing, protractor use, thermometer use, and stopwatch timing.

Big idea: each instrument has a right way to be set up and read, and small habits (eye level, same start point, exact timing) protect the accuracy of the result.

Flow note: the eureka can comes first because it builds on the measuring cylinder from the previous lesson. Length follows, with a worked example for "measure ten and divide" before a near-identical guided item and an independent calculation on your own. Angles, temperature and time close the lesson, since each is a short set of steps. Examples come from all three sciences (seeds, ripples, marble chips, hot water, ray diagrams).

Sections:
1. Start here (W14-01): finding the volume of an odd-shaped stone.
2. How do you find the volume of a solid? (W14-02–04): what a eureka can is → set the level → lower in the object → read the volume. Checks: why start at the spout; what to read.
3. How do you measure length? (W14-05–08): choose the ruler → line up and eye level → same start point → ten and divide. Worked (W14-06): ten seeds 12 cm → 1.2 cm. Guided: micrometer for a thin wire; ten beads 8.0 cm → 0.80 cm.
4. Angles, temperature and time (W14-09–11): protractor → bulb under the surface → wait then read → stopwatch. Checks: protractor placement; thermometer method.
5. On your own (W14-12–15): protractor diagram; ten ripples 24 cm → 2.4 cm; when to start a stopwatch; written task on the eureka can.

Out of scope: choosing the right graduated equipment for liquids (previous lesson); uncertainty from instrument resolution (Working Scientifically earlier).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 247 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 1. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original; the page's spring and tractor pictures are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The micrometer is named for tiny things only; how it is read is not taught.
- The protractor lesson uses one scale; reading the inner or outer scale is left to the teacher.
