# Working Scientifically Lesson 9 storyboard — Units and converting them

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-9`, id `W-DAT-009-W`, skill `W-DAT-009-W`, spec WS 4.3 to 4.5. It owns SI base units, prefixes (mega, kilo, centi, milli, micro; deci in one clause), converting between units, and converting before using an equation.

Big idea: everyone uses the same units, prefixes make them the right size, and converting correctly (bigger to smaller multiply, smaller to bigger divide) is what keeps calculations right.

Flow note: SI units, then prefixes, then converting (worked), then converting inside an equation (worked). Calculation flow: worked (2.5 km × 1000 = 2500 m) → guided (3.2 kg → 3200 g; 450 mm → 0.45 m) → worked in an equation (60 cm → 0.6 m, v = 0.6 ÷ 2 = 0.3 m/s) → guided (40 cm in 2 s: 0.2 m/s; first step for W = mg) → independent (0.45 kg → 450 g; 500 g → 0.5 kg, W = 4.9 N with g = 9.8 N/kg) → written (1.5 km in 300 s: 5 m/s).

Sections:
1. Start here (W9-01): 15 cm in millimetres.
2. What are SI units? (W9-02–03): standard units, base units.
3. What do prefixes mean? (W9-04–06): kilo and mega, centi, milli, micro. Checks: milli; kilo.
4. How do you convert units? (W9-07–09): direction rule, useful conversions, worked km to m; two guided conversions.
5. Why check units in equations? (W9-10–12): convert first, worked example, two guided items.
6. On your own (W9-13–16): smallest length, kg to g, weight from grams, written speed.

Out of scope: standard form (Lesson 10), conversions with squared or cubed units beyond the dm³ and cm³ chain, kelvin conversions, rearranging (Lesson 10).
