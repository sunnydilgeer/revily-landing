# Working Scientifically Lesson 2 storyboard — Communicating science and its issues

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-2`, id `W-MTH-002-W`, skill `W-MTH-002-W`, spec WS 1.3, 1.4 and 1.6. It owns why and how scientists tell people about discoveries, bias in media reports, the four kinds of issue (economic, social, personal, environmental) and questions science cannot answer (ethics). Risk and hazard are the next lesson; the scientific method was Lesson 1.

Big idea: discoveries only help if people hear about them, but reports can mislead or be biased, developments raise issues of several kinds, and some questions (right and wrong) are for society to decide, not experiments.

Flow note: why tell people first (the good reason to communicate), then what can go wrong in reports (a critical-reader skill), then the four issue types as one short frame each with a fresh example from the three sciences, then the limit of science. Each issue type gets its own frame so the student can name it from a scenario.

Sections:
1. Start here (W2-01): what to ask about a headline.
2. Why tell people? (W2-02–04): discoveries → habits → new technologies. Checks: an example of advice to change habits; who needs to know about a new scanner.
3. Can you trust the headline? (W2-05–07): reports can mislead → oversimplified → biased → spotting bias. Checks: an advert with missing data; a sign of bias.
4. What issues can science raise? (W2-08–10): economic → social → personal → environmental. Checks: name the issue type for two scenarios.
5. Can science answer everything? (W2-11–13): missing data → ethical questions → deciding together. Checks: which question experiments can answer; why scientists disagree.
6. On your own (W2-14–17): a mock report with small print; environmental issue; ethical question; a written reply on trusting a report.

Out of scope: calculating sample sizes or statistics (later lessons), named laws and regulations, how peer review works in detail (Lesson 1).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 235 (scope only); AQA 8464 working scientifically WS 1.3, 1.4 and 1.6. All wording and examples are original; the page's fullerene example, newspaper cartoon and drug-in-exams example are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Each issue type could have several labels (a wind farm can be personal or social). The questions give clear cases and the explanations say why the type fits.
- The ethics examples (animal genes, testing medicines on animals, fireworks) are neutral: the lesson never says what the right answer is.
