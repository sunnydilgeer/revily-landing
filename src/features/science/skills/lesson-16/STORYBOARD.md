# Working Scientifically Lesson 16 storyboard: Safety and ethics in the lab

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-16`, id `W-PRC-016-W`, spec WS 2.4, AT 3. It owns lab clothing, flammable and irritant chemicals, fume cupboards, spatula and funnel, diluting by adding concentrated to water, equipment hazards (clamp stands, hot equipment, low voltage) and ethics with organisms and people.

Big idea: safe practical work is a set of small habits, each with a reason, and ethical work includes the animals and people taking part.

Flow note: it follows the hazards-and-risk lesson, so the vocabulary is used, not re-taught. Chemicals come first because they are the most familiar hazards; equipment second, then ethics. Each frame gives the rule and the reason in one line. Examples come from all three sciences.

Sections:
1. Start here (W16-01): what to do before a practical starts.
2. How do you work safely with chemicals? (W16-02–05): clothing and instructions → hazardous chemicals → fume cupboard → spatula and funnel → dilute the safe way. Checks: fume cupboard; spatula; dilution.
3. Is equipment hazardous too? (W16-06–08): clamp stands and masses → hot equipment → low voltage. Checks: hot block; why low voltage.
4. What about ethics? (W16-09–10): animals → return wild animals → people must agree. Check: pond snails.
5. On your own (W16-11–14): find the unsafe part in a picture; check before lighting a Bunsen burner; consent when using classmates; written plan for a reaction that gives a harmful gas.

Out of scope: hazard symbols and risk assessment (earlier lesson); Bunsen burner use and heating (later lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 249 (scope only); AQA 8464 WS 2.4, AT 3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original; the page's cartoons are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Chlorine is named as an example of a harmful gas.
- Human participants: kept to "happy to take part and able to say no". Caffeine and other substances are not used as examples.
