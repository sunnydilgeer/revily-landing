# Working Scientifically Lesson 1 storyboard — The scientific method

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-1`, id `W-MTH-001-W`, skill `W-MTH-001-W`, spec WS 1.1 and 1.2. It owns the chain observation → hypothesis → prediction → test → peer review → accepted or rejected, the idea that theories change with evidence, and models (spatial, computational, with the Bohr model as the example) and their limits. Designing a fair test is the Lesson 4; risk and communication have their own lessons.

Big idea: scientists explain what they see with a hypothesis, test predictions from it, and let the evidence, checked by other scientists, decide whether the idea is kept, changed or replaced. Models are simple pictures with limits.

Flow note: the chain in order first (the student builds it up one step at a time and ends with the whole chain), then who checks the work, then what happens to an idea over years (accepted or replaced), then models last because they are a separate idea that uses the earlier language (predictions, evidence). Examples are a fence and plant heights (Biology), ice on trays (Physics), and the Bohr model and ball-and-stick molecule (Chemistry). The atom-model history is a one-clause link back to earlier lessons.

Sections:
1. Start here (W1-01): why do scientists drop an idea?
2. How is a hypothesis tested? (W1-02–04): observation → prediction → test → the whole chain. Checks: hypothesis vs observation; picking a prediction.
3. Who checks the result? (W1-05–07): peer review → sharing → more evidence. Checks: why check; results that disagree.
4. When is an idea accepted? (W1-08–10): accepted theory → evidence says no → ideas change. Checks: what makes a theory; what to do when evidence is against.
5. What is a model? (W1-11–13): simple picture → spatial → computational → limits → Bohr. Checks: type of model; limits.
6. On your own (W1-14–17): the numbered chain diagram; conflicting results; models changing; a written account of testing an explanation.

Out of scope: designing fair tests and variables (Lesson 4), risk (Lesson 3), telling people and bias (Lesson 2), scientific "laws", statistical testing.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 234 (scope only); AQA 8464 working scientifically WS 1.1 and 1.2. All wording and examples are original; the page's cartoons and its demons and bad-blood history are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- "One good result does not prove a hypothesis for certain" is added so students do not think a single test proves anything.
- The atom-model history is mentioned in one clause without naming models, because the earlier Chemistry lessons cover it.
- The Bohr model is used only as the page does: it explains periodic-table trends and cannot explain everything.

## Diagram specs
House style: as Biology Lessons 17 and 18 and the other Physics lessons: soft flat fills with a darker stroke, organic and slightly refined, PhysicsKit palette where useful, text ≥ 12px, readable on 360px. Keep a consistent colour per step in the chain: observation = teal, hypothesis = amber, prediction = purple, test = blue, evidence = green. Walkthrough frames in one section reuse one drawing and highlight or add one piece per frame.

W1-02 walkthrough (one drawing: a garden fence with tall plants on the sunny side, shorter plants on the shady side; the step boxes appear along the bottom):
- `wsmethod-observe`: the fence scene. A speech-free eye icon and tag "observation: plants on the sunny side are taller". A thought bubble labelled "hypothesis: more sunlight" appears, faded.
- `wsmethod-predict`: the same scene with one plant pot being moved into the shade and an arrow; tag "prediction: plants moved into the shade will grow less". Observation and hypothesis tags shown small.
- `wsmethod-test`: the same scene: two rows of pots, sun and shade, with a small ruler and a results tick "shade plants grew less: evidence". Tag "one result does not prove it".
- `wsmethod-chain`: the whole chain as four rounded boxes joined by arrows: observation → hypothesis → prediction → test, with a fifth small box "evidence" below the test. Each box has a tiny picture (eye, light bulb, arrow, flask). Names shown.

W1-05 walkthrough (one drawing: three small scientist figures around a paper):
- `wsmethod-peer`: a scientist hands a report to two others who hold magnifying glasses over it; tag "peer review: is the method sensible?".
- `wsmethod-share`: the report becomes a "scientific paper" sheet with arrows going out to three labs; tag "share results, repeat the work".
- `wsmethod-evidence`: two balance-scale pans: "evidence for" and "evidence against" with different amounts, and the words "decide what to believe".

W1-08 walkthrough (one drawing: a hypothesis card going through a "testing" gate):
- `wsmethod-accepted`: the hypothesis card comes out of the gate stamped "accepted theory" and sits on a textbook; a long timeline arrow "many years of testing".
- `wsmethod-rejected`: the card is stopped at the gate with a cross; two arrows go back "change it" and "new hypothesis", both looping to the start.
- `wsmethod-history`: a simple timeline of two ideas: "Sun goes round the Earth" replaced by "Earth goes round the Sun" and "old atom model" replaced by "newer atom model", with the label "new evidence" on each change. No detailed atom drawings, just simple circles.

W1-11 walkthrough (one drawing: a model table with a toy model, a molecule model and a laptop):
- `wsmethod-model`: a real heart or water cycle beside its simple diagram/toy model, with arrows "explains" and "predicts".
- `wsmethod-spatial`: a ball-and-stick water molecule (one large red ball, two small white, sticks) with the label "spatial model: shows where parts are".
- `wsmethod-computational`: a laptop screen showing simple dots spreading across a grid and a line graph; label "computational model: a computer simulation".
- `wsmethod-limits`: the ball-and-stick molecule again with a dotted cloud around it and tag "does not show the electrons"; a question mark.
- `wsmethod-bohr`: a simple Bohr atom (nucleus with two shells and a few electrons), tags "explains periodic table trends" (tick) and "can't explain everything" (cross). Not to scale.

Question visual (assessment view):
