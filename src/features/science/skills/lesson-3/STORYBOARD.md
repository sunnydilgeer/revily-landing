# Working Scientifically Lesson 3 storyboard — Hazards and risk

Chapter WS1, Working scientifically. Folder `skills/lesson-3`, id `W-MTH-003-W`, skill `W-MTH-003-W`, spec WS 1.5 and 2.4. It owns hazard versus risk, estimating a risk from data, perceived risk (familiarity, choice, visibility), the main lab hazards (microorganisms, chemicals, electricity, fire) and how to reduce risk with a risk assessment. Later practical-skills lessons (Lesson 16 equipment and chemical safety, Lesson 19 Bunsen and water baths) build on it.

Big idea: a hazard is something that could cause harm; risk is the chance it does. We can estimate risk from data, people judge it differently, and a risk assessment matches each hazard with a control.

Flow note: hazard and risk come first, with a data example, because everything else uses those two words. Next comes how people misjudge risk (a thinking skill), then the four lab hazards as one frame each, then reducing risk, ending with weighing risk against benefit for new technology. Examples: Biology (bacteria in dishes), Chemistry (acid, alcohol, gas), Physics (electricity), carbon capture as the new-technology case.

Sections:
1. Start here (W3-01): a puddle in a corridor.
2. Hazard or risk? (W3-02–04): a hazard → a risk → chance and outcome → estimating from data. Checks: pick the risk statement; compare two injury rates.
3. Why do people judge risk differently? (W3-05–07): familiar → choice → visibility. Checks: name the factor; why invisible risks are underestimated.
4. What hazards are there in a lab? (W3-08–10): microorganisms → chemicals → electricity → fire and heat. Checks: a scenario; match hazard with harm.
5. How do you reduce a risk? (W3-11–13): risk assessment → controls matched to hazards → electricity and fire → risks and benefits. Checks: best control for eye injury; weighing carbon capture.
6. On your own (W3-14–17): a numbered bench scene; comparing 3 in 100 with 10 in 1000; perceived risk; a written risk assessment for heating water.

Out of scope: hazard symbols (not on the page), named legislation, COSHH, statistics beyond simple comparison. Equipment-specific safety is taught in the practical-skills lessons.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 236 (scope only); AQA 8464 working scientifically WS 1.5 and 2.4. All wording, numbers and examples are original; the page's cartoon and its cycling, air travel, tanning bed and sulfuric acid examples are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Flying versus driving is a standard perceived-risk contrast; the lesson says "each journey is very safe" and "driving is often the riskier of the two", which matches accident statistics per journey.
- W3-15 asks students to scale 3 in 100 to 30 in 1000. This is a comparison, not a full calculation, so its dimension is dataInterpretation.
- The lesson never says an activity is safe with no controls; "Saying it is completely safe" is a reject line in the written task.
