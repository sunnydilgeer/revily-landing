# Working Scientifically Lesson 15 storyboard: Measuring pH and the size of a cell

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-15`, id `W-PRC-015-W`, spec AT 1, AT 7. It owns indicators, universal indicator, litmus and indicator paper, the pH probe, and measuring cell size with a clear ruler (length = 1000 µm ÷ cells counted in 1 mm).

Big idea: pick the pH method to suit the job, and turn a count of cells along 1 mm into a size in micrometres.

Flow note: pH first as a set of tools ordered from simple (indicator) to accurate (probe), each with one clue for when to use it. Cell size second, because it is a short method followed by a calculation lesson shape: worked example → near-identical guided item → independent calculation on your own. The microscope is met in the cells topic; this lesson only reuses it. Examples come from Chemistry (solutions, gas samples, a pond sample) and Biology (tissues).

Sections:
1. Start here (W15-01): a colourless solution, acid or alkali.
2. How do you measure pH? (W15-02–05): indicator → universal indicator → indicator paper → litmus and gas samples → pH probe. Checks: gradual colour change; already coloured solution; most accurate.
3. How big is a cell? (W15-06–09): set up the ruler → count along 1 mm → 1 mm = 1000 µm → formula. Worked: 5 cells → 200 µm. Guided: 8 cells → 125 µm; comparing tissues with 5 and 10 cells.
4. On your own (W15-10–14): 20 cells → 50 µm; a diagram with 10 cells along 1 mm → 100 µm; pH probe for accuracy; damp paper in a gas; written task describing the method.

Out of scope: magnification calculations and unit conversion beyond mm to µm (units lesson); the pH scale numbers and neutralisation (Chemistry).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 248 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 1, AT 7. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original; the page's table and 4-cell example are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Litmus paper is described only as red in acid, blue in alkali; it does not give a pH number.
- Cells are treated as equal in length along the 1 mm, as the method assumes.
