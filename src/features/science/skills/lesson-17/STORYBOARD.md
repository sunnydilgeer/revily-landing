# Working Scientifically Lesson 17 storyboard: Setting up electrolysis and a potometer

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-17`, id `W-PRC-017-W`, spec AT 3, AT 8. It owns collecting gases from electrolysis in upturned test tubes over the electrodes (cathode and anode products, one line each, linking to Chemistry), and the potometer set-up, the bubble method and rate = distance ÷ time.

Big idea: a good set-up turns something invisible (gas made, water taken up) into something you can collect or measure.

Flow note: electrolysis first, as a set-up with two clear outcomes (cathode, anode); the potometer second, because it ends in a calculation and gives the worked example → near-identical guided item → independent calculation shape. The Start here question rehearses distance ÷ time with a snail so the rate is familiar before it is applied to a plant. Links are one clause: gas tests from Chemistry, transpiration from plant transport.

Sections:
1. Start here (W17-01): a snail's speed, 30 cm in 10 min.
2. How do you set up electrolysis? (W17-02–04): the rig → the cathode → the anode → collect and identify. Checks: what forms at the cathode; how to identify a gas.
3. How do you set up a potometer? (W17-05–09): what it is → record the start → the bubble moves → the rate. Worked: 30 mm in 10 min → 3 mm/min. Guided: 40 mm in 8 min → 5 mm/min; why record the start; unit mm/min.
4. On your own (W17-10–14): identify the bubble on a diagram; which electrode gives hydrogen; 24 mm in 6 min → 4 mm/min; why the tubes are upside down; written potometer method.

Out of scope: electrolysis theory, ions, half equations and which products form from which solution (Chemistry); the tests for gases in detail; factors that change the rate of transpiration.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 250 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 3, AT 8. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original; the page's example numbers (25 mm in 10 minutes) are not reused. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Electrolysis products are given only as the two possibilities on the page, at each electrode, with no link to particular solutions.
- The potometer measures water uptake, which is used to estimate the transpiration rate, as the page states.
