# Working Scientifically Lesson 13 storyboard: Measuring mass, liquids and gases

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-13`, id `W-PRC-013-W`, spec AT 1, AT 2. It owns balance use (zero, difference by mass), dropping pipette, pipette with filler and measuring cylinder choice, the meniscus at eye level, the gas syringe as most accurate, the upturned cylinder in one clause (a later lesson owns the method), and counting bubbles as less accurate.

Big idea: choose the right piece of equipment, then use it carefully, so the measurement can be trusted.

Flow note: mass first because it is the simplest reading; then liquids, ending on the meniscus because it applies to any cylinder; then gases, which end on the accuracy ranking that links back to the choice of equipment. Examples come from all three sciences (copper sulfate, salt, indicator, gas from a reaction).

Sections:
1. Start here (W13-01): setting up a balance before weighing powder.
2. How do you measure mass? (W13-02–03): zero the balance → move all of it → difference in mass. Check: why zero with the container on.
3. How do you measure a liquid? (W13-04–06): dropping pipette → pipette and filler → measuring cylinder size → meniscus. Checks: reading the meniscus; a few drops.
4. How do you measure a gas? (W13-07–09): gas syringe → upturned cylinder → counting bubbles. Checks: most accurate; why bubbles are less accurate.
5. On your own (W13-10–14): meniscus reading from a diagram; mass by difference (3.5 g); pipette and filler; gas syringe; written task describing 4.0 g of solid and 25 cm³ of water.

Out of scope: using the upturned cylinder in detail, sealed delivery tubes and collecting samples (later lesson); units and conversions (covered earlier).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 246 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 1, 2. All wording, numbers and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The pipette filler is described as a safety and control aid; no mouth pipetting is shown.
- Volumes are shown with the reading taken from the bottom of the meniscus only (water-like liquids).

- `wsmeasure-difference`: two balances side by side. Left: beaker with solid, display 45.0 g. Right: empty beaker, display 42.5 g. Below, "45.0 − 42.5 = 2.5 g moved".

- `wsmeasure-cylinder`: two measuring cylinders side by side, a small one holding 20 cm³ (labelled "right size") and a very tall wide one holding the same amount that reads low (labelled "too big").
- `wsmeasure-meniscus`: a close-up of a measuring cylinder with an eye at the level of the liquid, a magnified circle showing the curved surface, and an arrow to the bottom of the curve. Labels "meniscus", "eye level", "read the bottom of the curve".
