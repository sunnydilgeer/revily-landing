# Working Scientifically Lesson 18 storyboard: Collecting gases and drawing apparatus

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-18`, id `W-PRC-018-W`, spec AT 3. It owns collecting gas over water in an upturned measuring cylinder (method, volume = difference), the sealed delivery tube, collecting a test-tube sample and storing it with a bung, and drawing 2D scientific apparatus diagrams (beaker, test tube, tripod, gauze, Bunsen burner, heat-proof mat).

Big idea: a gas can only be measured or tested if you trap it, so the system must be sealed, and your method should be shown in a clear, flat drawing.

Flow note: the measuring method first because it is the main skill (with a worked example, a near-identical guided calculation and an independent one on your own); then the choice between a cylinder and a test tube, which builds on the same picture; last, drawing, because it needs the apparatus to be understood first. Links back: gas syringe (measuring lesson) in one clause; test-tube collection in electrolysis in one clause.

Sections:
1. Start here (W18-01): how to catch and measure a gas.
2. How do you collect a gas over water? (W18-02–05): set-up → starting level → gas pushes water out → volume = final − start → keep it sealed. Worked: 5 cm³ to 47 cm³ = 42 cm³. Guided: 8 to 53 = 45 cm³. Check: why the tube is fully inside.
3. Which container should you use? (W18-06–08): test tube for a sample → bung to store → choose the tool.
4. How do you draw your apparatus? (W18-09–11): flat side view → shapes → gauze and mat → sealed tube with bung.
5. On your own (W18-12–14): independent volume (12 to 68 = 56 cm³); a numbered diagram question; written task.

Out of scope: gas tests (Chemistry lessons), gas syringe use (measuring lesson), drawing electrical circuits, heating (next lesson).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 251 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 3. All wording, examples, numbers and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Volume is taught as final level minus starting level, as the page does, without discussing the direction of the scale on an upturned cylinder.
- The electrolysis product gases are not repeated here; only the collection method is linked.
