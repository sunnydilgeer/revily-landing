# Working Scientifically Lesson 21 storyboard: Random sampling

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-21`, id `W-PRC-021-W`, spec WS 2.5, AT 4. It owns random sampling of a population, quadrats at random coordinates from a grid, why non-random sampling is biased, and random selection of people from records.

Big idea: you cannot study everything, so you study a sample, and a random sample is the fairest way to make it represent the whole population.

Flow note: why we sample comes first (population, sample, represent, random); then the field method with quadrats, because it is the most concrete; then people, which reuses the same idea with numbered records. Sample size and precision were met in an earlier lesson and are used here in one clause. No calculation, so no worked example is needed; the quadrat mean is mentioned as a step only. Links back: quadrats in Biology in one clause.

Sections:
1. Start here (W21-01): how to find daisies in a big field.
2. Why do you take a sample? (W21-02–04): population too big → a sample → represents → random. Checks: why sample; what a sample must do.
3. How do you sample a field at random? (W21-05–07): grid → coordinates → place and count → bias. Checks: the right method; the one-corner problem.
4. How do you sample people at random? (W21-08–10): people → records numbered → generator picks the group → estimate from the sample. Checks: picking 200 of 5000; why nearby people are biased.
5. On your own (W21-11–14): a school survey; a biased daisy count; a numbered two-grid diagram question; written task.

Out of scope: stratified or systematic sampling, transects (Biology), sample-size calculations, estimating the population total by calculation.

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 254 (scope only); AQA 8464 WS 2.5, AT 4. All wording, examples and diagrams are original (the page's diabetes example is not reused). Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- The estimate step for people is described in words only, with no calculation.
- "Random" is defined as an equal chance for every member.
