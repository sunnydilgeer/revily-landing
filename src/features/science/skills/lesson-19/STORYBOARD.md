# Working Scientifically Lesson 19 storyboard: Heating substances safely

Strand skills, chapter WS2 "Practical skills". Folder `skills/lesson-19`, id `W-PRC-019-W`, spec AT 2, AT 3. It owns lighting a Bunsen burner and a safe flame (yellow vs blue, hole open or closed), tongs, tripod and gauze, not heating flammables in a flame, the water bath method (maximum 100 °C), and electric heaters with stirring.

Big idea: choose the heating method to match the job, and set the equipment up so that no one is near an unsafe flame or hot glass.

Flow note: the burner itself first, because every later step depends on how the flame works; then holding and supporting what you heat, including the flammables rule; then the two set-temperature methods as the safer alternatives. No calculation in this lesson. The links to hazards and risk (an earlier lesson) are made in the reasons given, not repeated.

Sections:
1. Start here (W19-01): which flame is safest to leave.
2. How do you use a Bunsen burner? (W19-02–04): set up → light with a splint → open the hole (blue, hotter) → close the hole (yellow, visible). Checks: order of lighting; opening the hole.
3. How do you hold what you heat? (W19-05–07): tongs → tripod and gauze set up before lighting → flammables. Checks: holding a tube; when the tripod goes in place.
4. When is a set temperature better? (W19-08–10): water bath → method → electric heater and stirring → the 100 °C limit. Checks: why 150 °C is not possible; stirring.
5. On your own (W19-11–14): heating ethanol; choosing a heater for 120 °C; a numbered Bunsen diagram question; written task.

Out of scope: using a Bunsen burner in named reactions; safety goggles and lab rules (Lesson 16); measuring temperature (Lesson 14).

Source boundary: supplied revision-guide page 252 (scope only); AQA 8464 AT 2, AT 3. All wording, examples and diagrams are original. Draft pending teacher review.

Judgement calls for the teacher:
- Frame text says the yellow flame is "cooler"; some teachers may prefer "the safety flame".
- Ethanol is used as the flammable example.
- The tongs frame states "near the top" as on the page; larger containers use the tripod.
