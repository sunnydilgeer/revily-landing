# Lesson 18 storyboard — Water and food on the move

Big idea: water travels up a plant and out of its leaves; food travels around the plant in a different tube. The lesson follows the water from start to finish before switching to food. Guard cells come before the rate factors, so "light opens stomata" already makes sense.

1. Start here (B18-01): wet washing dries because water evaporates into the air as water vapour.
2. Follow the water (B18-02–04): water evaporates inside the leaf and leaves through stomata (transpiration) → replaced from the xylem → roots take in more (link to Lesson 4) → the transpiration stream, which also carries mineral ions. Checks: correct order; why water moves up.
3. The leaf's doors (B18-05–07): guard cells swell to open the stoma (carbon dioxide in, water vapour out) or go floppy to close it (water saved, less carbon dioxide); most stomata are on the cooler, shaded underside.
4. What speeds it up? (B18-08–10): key idea — water vapour moves from lots (inside) to less (outside), faster when the difference is bigger; then warmer, windier, more humid (defined), brighter (stomata open). Checks: slowest conditions; night-time.
5. How fast? (B18-11–12): rate = water lost ÷ time, worked from a sealed-pot balance example (6 g in 3 hours = 2 g per hour), then 4.5 g in 3 hours.
6. Food on the move (B18-13–15): phloem carries dissolved sugar to growing tips, roots and stores, up or down (translocation); side-by-side comparison with xylem.
7. On your own (B18-16–19): interpret original still-air/moving-air data without over-claiming; calculate a rate from it; apply to a hot, dry, windy afternoon; teacher-reviewed greenhouse explanation.

No required practical is attached. The balance example is an illustrative model result, not a practical procedure. Out of scope: potometers, cohesion/tension, how guard cells take up water, turgid/flaccid terminology, desert-plant adaptations.
