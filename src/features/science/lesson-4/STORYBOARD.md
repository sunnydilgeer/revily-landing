# Lesson 4 storyboard — Specialisation and differentiation

Big idea: a cell's shape and parts fit its job. A cell like this is called specialised, and a cell becomes specialised by differentiating.

Anchor / route: define "specialised" first, using the shape-fits-job idea. Then meet two teams of specialised cells: an animal team (sperm, nerve, muscle), then a plant team that follows the water in through a root hair and up the xylem, with phloem carrying sugar from the leaves. Finally, go back in time: how did those cells become specialised? Each cell is taught once.

Why this order:
- "Specialised" and the feature → how it helps → job method come first, so every example cell is read through them (the old lesson defined it only after all six cells).
- Mitochondria are recalled at the start (Lesson 1), because sperm and muscle both depend on them.
- The plant team follows one route (soil → root hair → xylem → leaves; leaves → phloem → other parts), so each cell has a place in the story; root hair is taught once and reused only in the written task.
- Differentiation comes after the examples, because learners need to know what a specialised cell looks like before they can see an unspecialised cell become one.
