## Sequence and teaching script

1. Starting check: a swimming cell benefits from a long tail. Optional Cells refresher is a short, non-gating disclosure, not a replay.
2. Sperm, separately: meet the whole cell; tail helps swimming; many mitochondria release energy for movement; head carries genetic information and enzymes help entry into the egg. Guided tail/energy causal check.
3. Nerve, separately: long fibre carries electrical impulses over distance; branched ends connect with other cells. Guided long-fibre check.
4. Muscle, separately: fibres shorten to contract; many mitochondria supply energy through respiration. Guided energy check.
5. Root hair, separately: long extension gives a large surface for water/mineral uptake; no photosynthesis in this underground example. Guided surface-area check.
6. Xylem, separately: mature vessel elements form hollow tubes without end walls, thickened with lignin; water/minerals travel from roots and the wall supports the plant. Guided hollow-tube check.
7. Phloem, separately: elongated living cells form tubes, pores in end walls allow dissolved sugars through. No companion-cell mechanism or detailed transport machinery. Guided pore check.
8. Only now compare animal/plant examples: structure → effect → useful function. A worked root-hair explanation reveals one step at a time, optionally.
9. Differentiation: an unspecialised cell acquires structures suited to its job, not just size. Most animal types differentiate early; many plant cells retain differentiation ability through life; mature animal division mainly repairs/replaces. Avoid “all animal cells”/“plants never differentiate”.
10. Independent checks: supplied information on sperm, phloem and plant differentiation; novel absorbing cell with folds; tissue replacement context. Final written transfer explains why a provided elongated/branched signalling cell suits communication. Model and rubric only after save; no automatic correctness.
