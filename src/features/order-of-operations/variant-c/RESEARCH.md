## Evidence base

This variant is source-led rather than a claim of independent curriculum research. Its authoritative content set is the three supplied tutor PDFs and three supplied silent captioned videos, mapped in `SOURCE-MAP.md`. ## Video interaction reference

The owner requested familiar short-clip integration. [Khan Academy's video-player documentation](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/204794840-What-features-does-the-video-player-have) describes learner-controlled speed and transcripts. Lesson 2 uses native playback/seek/fullscreen controls, an explicit Replay button, speed selection and a written mathematical walkthrough. It does not reproduce another platform's visual branding or claim equivalent learning efficacy.

The three cleaned BIDMAS recordings are silent with baked-in on-screen captions. Their written alternatives preserve all worked examples and clarify equal priority, fraction grouping and like terms. No fabricated audio transcript or empty caption track is presented. Playback is not a completion gate, and videos do not autoplay or advance the lesson automatically.

## Content boundaries

- Difficulty badges and mark allocations are not learning interactions and are omitted from the learner UI.
- Source questions and mathematical outcomes are retained. Multiple-choice response formats are used for open reasoning prompts so learners receive immediate complete feedback without natural-language grading.
- No claim is made that completing the lesson proves durable mastery.
- Algebra normalisation only recognises the exact authored monomial answer forms; it is not a computer algebra system.

## Corrections and clarifications

- A fraction bar creates numerator and denominator groups. “Work out the top first” is presented as a workflow, not a priority rule.
- Treating a fraction as one line is equivalent only with explicit brackets around the complete numerator and denominator.
- Algebra teaching distinguishes numerical coefficients from variable factors and avoids the video shorthand “multiply the letters.”
- Equal-priority operations are evaluated left to right, not in the letter order of the BIDMAS acronym.
